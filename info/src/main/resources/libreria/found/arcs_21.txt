@ fivespears | The Five Spears of Durnhold
> When the raiders came up the valley to Durnhold, the old spearmaker had five sons and no soldiers. He made each son a spear from whatever he had in the forge: stone, copper, iron, and for the youngest, the last good diamond in the valley. They held the bridge for a day and a night. The spears are kept together still.
stone_spear | The Eldest's Spear | The eldest got the stone spear, because he said he didn't need the good metal: he was the strongest, and stone would do. It did. He stood in the middle of the bridge and didn't move all night. In the morning, they had to help him put it down. His hands wouldn't open.
copper_spear | The Second Son's Spear | The second son was the clever one, and he said copper was a fine metal if you knew how to use it. He spent the night tricking the raiders into the river, one at a time. He counted. He told everyone the number for the rest of his life. It was a different number every time.
iron_spear | The Third Son's Spear | The third son was afraid. He said so to his brothers, at the start of the night, and they said they were too, and that helped. He held his place on the bridge with this, and shook the whole time, and didn't run. His father says he was the bravest of the five, because of the shaking.
iron_spear | The Fourth Son's Spear | The fourth son was hurt in the night and kept fighting, and nobody knew until morning, when he sat down on the bridge and said "I think I'll just sit a moment." His brothers carried him home. He lived. He has a limp. He says it reminds him to sit down sometimes.
diamond_spear | The Youngest's Spear | The youngest was fourteen, and their father gave him the diamond spear because it was the lightest. He didn't fight much that night. He held the bridge's far end and passed water to his brothers. When the sun came up and the raiders left, he was the one who cried. They all did, after.

@ coppergreen | The Copper Guard of Rustmoor
> The town of Rustmoor sits by the sea, where the salt air turns copper green in a single season. Its guard wears copper armour anyway, out of tradition, and so the guardsmen of Rustmoor are green, all of them, from helm to boot. They're very proud of this. Anybody who calls them "the cabbages" gets a night in the cells.
copper_chestplate | The Captain's Plate | The captain of the guard refuses to have his armour scraped. He has worn the same copper plate for twenty-two years, and it has gone green, then blue, then nearly black, crusted with salt. He says it's the colour of experience. The children say it's the colour of a pond.
copper_leggings | The Recruit's Greaves | New recruits get bright orange armour, and are mocked mercilessly until it turns. The quickest way to turn it, they're told, is to stand in the sea for a week. Several have done this. It doesn't work. The guard has never once admitted it was a joke.
copper_boots | The Clanking Boots | Copper boots on cobblestones make a sound you can hear across the town. Thieves in Rustmoor have a very easy life, as a result. The guard has tried wrapping their boots in cloth. The cloth goes green too. Nobody knows how. It's the air.
copper_sword | The Cabbage Sword | When a visiting lord called them "the cabbages" to their faces, the captain drew this green sword and challenged him on the spot. The lord, who was a famous duellist, lost, very quickly. He later said the captain fought like a demon. The captain said he fought like a cabbage.
copper_axe | The Waxing Day | Once a year, on the town's feast day, the guard is allowed to wax their armour, so for one day it shines orange again, and they march down the main street gleaming, and the whole town cheers. By the next morning, it's started to go green. They say that's the point.

@ chainmailmonk | The Monk in the Chain Shirt
> The monks of the high monastery wore plain brown robes and took a vow of peace. Brother Aldric wore chainmail under his robe and took the vow anyway. When the abbot asked why, Aldric said peace was a fine thing, and he meant to keep it, but that the road up the mountain was long and not everyone on it had taken the vow.
chainmail_chestplate | The Shirt Under the Robe | Nobody knew he wore it for eleven years, until a bandit's knife bounced off his ribs on the mountain road. The bandit stared at the hole in the robe. Aldric stared at the bandit. Then he said: "go in peace, brother," and the bandit, very confused, did.
chainmail_helmet | The Coif in the Hood | Under his hood, a coif of chain links. He said it helped him think, the weight of it. He also said it helped when the novices threw apples at him from the orchard wall, which they did, every autumn, because he was the only monk who never told them off.
iron_shovel | The Garden Spade | He kept the monastery garden, and kept it well. He talked to the vegetables. The other monks said that was mad. Aldric said he'd taken a vow of peace, not a vow of silence. His cabbages were the best on the mountain, and his chainmail clinked as he weeded.
chainmail_leggings | The Day of the Raid | When raiders came up the road to burn the monastery, Aldric stood in the gate and didn't fight, and didn't move, and the chainmail took every blow they gave him until they got tired. Then he invited them in for soup. Two of them became monks. One of them is the abbot now.
chainmail_boots | The Long Road | He walks the mountain road now, very old, in his chain boots and his robe, and walks travellers up to the gate, and tells them about peace. Nobody has robbed a traveller on that road in thirty years. Nobody is quite sure whether it's the sermons or the clinking.

@ firsttoolmakers | The First Tools
> Before kings, before towns, before anyone wrote anything down, there were people who learned to break stone so it had an edge. The archaeologists who dug up their camp at the bottom of the gorge found the tools where they were left, in a ring around a cold hearth, and tried to imagine who held them.
wooden_pickaxe | The First Pick | Wood, hardened in fire, worn smooth on the handle by a hand much smaller than ours. It was found nearest the hearth. The archaeologists think it was a child's. They think the child was learning. There are tooth marks on the handle. The child was also, it seems, teething.
stone_axe | The Woman's Axe | A stone head lashed to a wooden handle with sinew. The edge has been re-chipped so often that the flakes still lay in a little heap where she sat. Whoever owned it used it for a lifetime and kept it sharp. It was found with a bracelet of shells, placed next to it, carefully, in a row.
stone_pickaxe | The Miner's Pick | Found at the back of the cave, at the end of a tunnel that someone had spent years digging into the rock, following a seam of something shiny. The archaeologists followed the seam. It was copper. Whoever dug it never got there. They were a hand's width away.
stone_sword | The Hunter's Blade | A long, sharp stone set in a bone grip, closer to a knife than a sword. There were marks on it that the archaeologists couldn't explain, until a hunter from a nearby village looked and said: "those are tallies. One for every animal. He was proud." There are two hundred and forty.
stone_spear | The Spear by the Hearth | It was found standing upright in the hearth's ash, the point down, the shaft carved with a pattern of rings. It had been placed there on purpose, when the camp was left for good. Nobody knows why. The archaeologists left it there, standing up, when they filled the dig back in.

@ goldfever | The Last Prospector of Embergulch
> When the gold ran out at Embergulch, everyone left. Everyone except Old Morty, who said there was more down there, and he'd find it, and he dug every day for thirty-one years with nothing but a golden shovel, a golden pick and an unshakeable belief that tomorrow was the day. He was right. It just took a while.
golden_shovel | The Golden Shovel | Morty made it out of the first nugget he found, back in the rush, which every other prospector said was madness: gold's too soft for digging. He agreed. He said that's why he liked it. It reminded him, every time it bent, what he was digging for.
golden_pickaxe | The Pick That Broke | It broke four hundred times. He mended it four hundred times, by melting down the gold flakes he found in the gravel, a pinch at a time. It got smaller every year. By the end it was the size of a spoon. He said it was the finest tool he'd ever owned.
golden_hoe | The Garden He Grew | Between digs, he grew potatoes, in a little garden by his tent, with a golden hoe, because he'd run out of anything else to make one from. Travellers laughed at a man with a golden hoe and no money. He told them it was a family heirloom. It was. He'd made it himself.
golden_helmet | The Last Day | On the morning of the thirty-first year, he struck a vein so big that the whole hill shone. He sat down next to it and looked at it for a long time. Then he took one nugget, filled the hole back in, and went to town to buy a hat. He said the finding was the point.
golden_axe | Morty's Hut | He's still out at Embergulch, in a hut made of old crates, with the hill full of gold behind him that only he knows about. Young prospectors come and ask him for advice. He says, "dig every day." They ask where. He says, "anywhere. It's not about the gold." They don't understand. He knows.

@ diamondheist | The Great Diamond Heist
> Five thieves, one night, one plan: break into the deepest vault in the world, under the ancient city where nothing makes a sound, and steal the diamonds kept there since before the world was new. The plan was perfect. The thieves were not. This is what each of them took, and what it cost.
diamond_hoe@ancient_city | The Lockpick's Prize | The lockpick was the best in the kingdom and had never once been caught. She picked the first door in seven seconds and grabbed this, because it was the nearest thing that shone. It was a hoe. A diamond hoe. She was furious. She kept it anyway. She grows prize turnips with it now.
diamond_leggings@ancient_city | The Strongman's Prize | The strongman was supposed to carry the loot. He tried on the diamond leggings instead, because he'd always wanted diamond leggings. He was still trying to do up the buckles when the Warden woke up. He ran in them. He's never run so fast in his life. He's never taken them off.
diamond_pickaxe | The Brains' Prize | The planner had worked out everything: the guards, the doors, the route. Not that the deep city's floor was alive and listening. His stomach rumbled. Something enormous and blind turned its head. He dropped his notes and ran with only this. He never eats before a job now.
diamond_sword | The Lookout's Prize | The lookout's job was to watch the tunnel. She saw the Warden coming, and shouted, and everyone ran, and she grabbed this sword on the way out, and later traded it for a farm. She's the only one who did well out of it. She says the trick was screaming early.
diamond_chestplate@end_city | The Fence's Prize | The fence, who was to sell the diamonds, never went into the vault. He waited at the top. When the others came out with a hoe, some leggings, a pickaxe and a sword, he sighed, and went off to the End on his own, and came back with a chestplate. Professionals, he says. Honestly.

@ moonlesslake | The Witch of the Moonless Lake
> At the bottom of the world there is a lake where the moon has never shone, and on the lake, in a boat of black wood, sits a witch with four arms who once loved the moon and was abandoned by it. She is very old. She is very patient. She waits for the moon to come down to the water and apologise.
bow | The Bow of the Lake | She shoots from her boat with a bow of black wood, and her arrows fly across the water and do not make a sound. Travellers on the lake shore never see her. They only see the arrow in the tree beside their head. It is a warning. She gives one. She has never given two.
iron_helmet | The Crown of No Light | A crown of iron that does not shine, worn by a queen whose kingdom is a lake and whose subjects are the fish. She says the fish are loyal. She says they never leave. She says it in a tone that suggests she has thought a great deal about those who do.
trident | The Four-Armed Spear | Her spear has four prongs, one for each of her arms, and she says she uses all four at once. Nobody has seen her do it. Nobody who would be able to tell you about it, anyway. The fishermen on the far shore say it's for fish. The fishermen on the far shore are wrong.
leather_chestplate | The Robe of the Waiting Witch | Once it was white, silver at the edges, embroidered with stars: a robe for a moon-bride. She dyed it black the night the moon left her. She has not worn another. She says she'll dye it white again when the moon comes back. She keeps a pot of white dye by her, just in case.
fishing_rod | The Night the Moon Came | Once, the songs say, the moon did shine on the lake, for one night, and the witch rowed out into the light and sat very still. In the morning she was gone, and her boat was empty, and the lake was silver at the edges. Nobody knows if the moon said sorry. The lake is not dark any more.

@ brothersboat | My Brother's Boat
> My brother built a boat when he was nineteen and I was twelve. He took me out in it every Sunday for ten years. We caught fish, mostly, and talked, sometimes. Then one Sunday he went out alone. I'm forty now. I still go out in it on Sundays. I'm writing this down so somebody remembers him besides me.
fishing_rod | His Rod | He fished with this rod his whole life. He caught a fish every Sunday without fail and never once kept one. He'd look at it, and say something to it, quietly, and let it go. I asked him once what he said. He said "I tell them they're doing well." I laughed. I do it too now.
iron_axe | The Axe He Built It With | He built the boat in our father's barn with this axe, out of a tree that fell in a storm. It took him a whole summer. Our father said it would never float. It floated. Our father stood on the shore and didn't say anything. Then he asked if he could go out in it. He went out every week after.
leather_chestplate | His Sailing Jacket | Too big for me when I was twelve. He'd put it round me when the wind got up. I wear it now. It still smells of him, a little, though I think I imagine that. The pocket has a note in it, folded small, that I've never read. I don't know when I'll read it. Not yet.
leather_boots | The Sunday He Didn't Come Back | The sea was calm that day. Nobody knows what happened. The boat came back to the shore on its own, in the evening, empty, rocking very gently, with his boots in the bottom of it and his line still baited. As if he'd only stepped out for a moment.
shield | The Fish | Last Sunday I caught a fish, and I looked at it, and I told it it was doing well. And I let it go. And I sat in the boat for a long time, and then I took the note out of the jacket pocket and read it. It says: "you're doing well." That's all. I think he knew I'd need it. I did.

@ lighthouserivals | The Rival Lighthouse Keepers
> On two islands, a mile apart across the strait, there stood two lighthouses, and two keepers who had been rivals for twenty years over whose light was brighter. They had never met. They sent each other insults by flag signal, every evening, at sunset. Then one evening, one of them signalled something else entirely.
flint_and_steel | The First Light | Old Bram lit his lamp every night at sunset, at the same moment as the keeper across the strait, and then spent the rest of the evening squinting across the water to see whose was brighter. His always was, he said. He kept a log. Every page said "mine brighter".
shield | The Signal Flags | He sent his insults with flags. "Your light is dim." "Your light is a candle." "Your light is a firefly with ambitions." The other keeper sent back: "Your light is a disgrace." "Your light is a lie." "Your flags are crooked." The flags were not crooked. He checked.
leather_chestplate | The Storm Coat | In the great storm of the autumn, Bram's lamp failed. He climbed his tower in this coat in the screaming wind to relight it and couldn't. Across the strait, the other light blazed brighter than he'd ever seen it, all night, and the ships came in safe, guided by one light.
fishing_rod | The Rowing Trip | The next morning, he rowed across the strait for the first time in twenty years to say thank you. The other keeper met him at the dock. She was seventy, with a pipe, and a terrible scowl, and said, "your flags were crooked." He said, "they were not." She said, "come in for tea."
leather_boots | The Last Signal | That evening, after he'd rowed home, she sent a new signal across the strait, very carefully, with all her flags: "your light is quite bright." He sent back: "so is yours." They were married in the spring. They keep both lights now. They still argue about which one's brighter.

@ icetomb | The Ice Tomb of King Haldor
> In the frozen north there's a mountain with a cave in it, and in the cave there's a king, sitting on a throne of ice, with his whole court around him, frozen exactly as they were the night the winter came for them. The ice has kept them perfectly. Explorers who've seen them say that's the worst part.
iron_helmet | The King's Helm | Haldor sits upright on his throne in this helm, with his eyes open, frost on his eyelashes, and his mouth open, as if he was in the middle of saying something. Explorers argue about what it was. The bravest of them, who looked closest, says it was "wait".
golden_sword | The Sword on His Knee | His sword lies across his knees, unsheathed. There's frost on the blade, but under it, there's blood. Whatever happened in that hall, it happened before the cold. Someone was fighting, at the end. Nobody knows who. Nobody wants to know who.
golden_helmet | The Queen's Crown | The queen is not at his side. Her throne is empty, and her crown lies beside it on the ice, and a trail of small frozen footprints leads out of the hall and down the mountain, into the snow. Nobody has followed them. They were filled in by snow a long time ago.
leather_boots | The Explorer's Boots | An explorer went into the cave alone, to bring out a relic. He was found a year later, sitting in the court with the others, frozen, with a smile on his face, as if he'd just been made very welcome. His boots were outside the cave, by the entrance, as you'd leave them at a friend's door.
shears | The Cut Rope | The cave is sealed now. The village at the foot of the mountain cut the rope bridge that led to it and won't speak of the place. But every winter, on the longest night, they say, there's music coming down from the mountain. A feast. A king's voice, laughing. Nobody goes up to look.

@ bardnobody | The Bard Nobody Asked For
> Every adventuring party needs a bard, said Wendel, who was a bard. The adventuring party disagreed. He followed them anyway, for three years, through dungeons and deserts and one regrettable swamp, playing his lute at every possible moment, including the wrong ones, which was most of them.
leather_helmet | The Feathered Cap | He wore a cap with a feather so long it brushed the ceiling of every dungeon. The party could always tell where he was by the sound of the feather scraping the stone. So could the monsters. So could everything. The feather was, the party agreed, a liability.
shield | The Lute Case | He wrote a song about every battle, while it was happening, and sang it, loudly. "And lo, brave Garrick swung his axe!" Garrick had not swung his axe. Garrick was hiding. "And lo, the troll did fall!" The troll had not fallen. The troll was confused. It worked, oddly often.
bow | The Bow He Never Used | He carried a bow for appearances. He couldn't shoot. Once, when the party's archer was knocked out, he picked it up and fired, and hit the dragon in the eye by complete accident. He wrote a song about it. It was eleven verses long. They begged him to stop.
leather_boots | The Dancing Boots | He danced, too, while he sang, in these boots, which had bells on them. The bells could be heard for miles. The party tried to take the bells off in his sleep. He woke up and wrote a song about it called "The Betrayal of the Bells". It was surprisingly moving.
leather_chestplate | The Ballad | He left the party after three years, and they were relieved, and then, after a while, they weren't. The dungeons were too quiet. Nobody sang about their battles. Garrick went and found him, in a tavern, in the end, and asked him to come back. He said yes. He wrote a song about it.

@ granulfa | Granny Ulfa Who Shouted
> In the mountains there's a tradition of the old ways: words of power, spoken by those who learn them, that can knock a man flat or call the weather. Most who learn them spend years in a monastery at the top of seven thousand steps. Granny Ulfa learned them in her kitchen, from shouting at her grandsons.
leather_helmet | The Headscarf | She tied it under her chin every morning and went out to feed the chickens. When the chickens wouldn't come, she shouted. The first time it happened, the chickens flew backwards into the barn. She didn't know what she'd done. She tried it again. The barn fell over.
wooden_hoe | Her Garden Hoe | The monks of the high monastery heard about her and came down the seven thousand steps to see. She was hoeing her garden. They asked her to demonstrate. She shouted at the weeds. The weeds left. So did the top layer of the garden, and one of the monks.
iron_axe | Grandson's Axe | Her grandsons were the laziest boys in the valley, until she learned to shout. She said "WAKE" once, in the morning, and they woke, and so did the next village. She said "CHOP" and the woodpile chopped itself. The boys say it was the most productive year of their lives.
leather_chestplate | The Apron | When a dragon came to the valley, the villagers ran to her house. She came out in her apron, very cross, wiping her hands, and looked at the dragon, and said one word. Nobody heard the word. The dragon did. It sat down, sheepish, and flew away, and never came back.
fishing_rod | The Quiet Days | The monks asked her to come up to the monastery and teach. She said she was too old for seven thousand steps. They built her a cottage halfway up instead. She sits there and fishes in the stream, and when the monks come down for lessons, she tells them to use their indoor voices.

@ letterman | The Postman of Pale Hollow
> Pale Hollow is the most remote village in the kingdom, three days' walk from the nearest road, and for forty years one postman has carried every letter in and out of it. He knows every secret in the valley. He's kept every one. When he retired, the village found a way to thank him that nobody had ever thought of before.
leather_boots | The Postman's Boots | Three days in, three days out, every week, for forty years. He worked it out once: he's walked round the world four times. His feet are, he says, very tired. He also says they're the most trustworthy feet in the kingdom. They've never once gone anywhere they shouldn't.
leather_chestplate | The Mailbag Coat | A leather coat with a dozen inside pockets, each one for a different family in the valley. He never mixed them up. Not once. The baker's love letters, the widow's bills, the shepherd's letters from his son in the army. He carried all of them close to his heart.
iron_sword | The Sword for the Pass | The pass into Pale Hollow has wolves, and bandits, and once, memorably, a very territorial goose. He carried a sword for forty years and drew it once, for the goose. The goose won. He took the long way round after that. It added a day. He never complained.
shield | The Secret Keeper | He knew everything: who was in love with whom, who owed money, whose son had run off, whose letters were never answered. Nobody in the valley ever learned a secret from him. When the new postman asked him for advice, he said: "carry the letters. Forget the words."
fishing_rod | The Party | At his retirement, every single person in the valley wrote him a letter, and he sat by the river with this rod, and read them, one by one, all afternoon. He'd never had a letter of his own before. Not one. He cried. Then he went home and wrote back to every single one.
