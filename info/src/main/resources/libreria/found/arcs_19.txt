@ tenyearsold | The Girl Who Left Home at Ten
> In some parts of the world it was the custom, when a child turned ten, to give them a small creature of their own and send them off down the road to see the world. Nobody thought this was strange. I didn't either, until I had children of my own. Here is what I took with me, and what I brought back.
leather_helmet | The Cap | Red and white, a little big, turned backwards because that's how the older children wore it. My mother put it on my head at the gate and said, "change your underwear every day." I didn't. I want to be honest about that, here, at the beginning.
leather_boots | The Running Shoes | I ran everywhere in these: down the road, through the tall grass, into every cave and every forest and every gym in every town, challenging everyone I met. I was ten. I thought the world was a list of people to beat. It took me a long time to learn it wasn't.
fishing_rod | The Old Rod | A man gave me this rod in the second town, and said it was the worst rod in the world. It was. I caught nothing but one floppy orange fish, over and over, for a year. I kept it. It grew into the biggest, angriest sea-serpent you ever saw. Never judge a fish.
leather_chestplate | The Pocket Coat | Every pocket had something in it: berries, potions, rope, a bicycle somehow. My little yellow friend rode on my shoulder and refused to travel in the pocket I'd made for her. She didn't like being put away. I came to understand that. Nobody does.
shield | Home Again | I came home at fourteen, champion of nothing in particular, with a dozen friends of every shape and size. My mother was waiting at the gate. She said, "did you change your underwear?" I said, "every day." She knew I was lying. She hugged me anyway.

@ giraffes | The Girl Who Saw the Giraffes
> The world ended when she was born, so she didn't miss it. The man who looked after her did. He'd lost a daughter at the very beginning, and he didn't want to lose another, and he never said so. They crossed the whole ruined country together. She wanted to see everything. He just wanted her to live.
bow | Her Bow | He taught her to shoot with this, in an old shopping hall full of the sick, in whispers. She got good, fast. He said she shot like his brother. She asked what his brother was like. He didn't answer for a day. Then he told her, all at once, and she didn't interrupt.
iron_axe | His Axe | He never once let her see him use it, if he could help it. She saw anyway. She saw a lot of things she wasn't meant to. On the long nights she'd tell him terrible jokes from a joke book she'd found. He never laughed. Until the one about the scarecrow. Then he did.
leather_boots | The Long Road | Two years on the road, across mountains, through the snow, through cities fallen down and taken back by forest. She talked the whole way. He didn't. She says that's what made them work. He was the quiet, and she was everything else.
leather_chestplate | The Jacket | On the last day of their journey, in a city of empty towers, she ran ahead, round a corner, and stopped. A herd of giraffes was walking down the old street, tall and slow and golden, eating the leaves off the tops of the ruined buildings. She fed one. He watched her.
iron_sword | The Lie | At the end, he did a terrible thing, to keep her alive, and he lied to her about it. She knew. She asked him to swear it was true. He swore. She looked at him for a long time and said, "okay." She has never forgiven him. She has never stopped loving him either.

@ starscourge | The General Who Held Back the Stars
> In the age of the shattering there was a general so mighty that, as a youth, he stopped the very stars in their courses, so that their fates could not be written. He was a giant of a man. His horse was not. He would not abandon the horse. So he learned the magic of gravity, so as not to crush it.
iron_sword | Greatsword of the Starscourge | Twin blades, each taller than a man, forged from fallen stars. He swung them both at once and the earth cracked open beneath. In the end, when the rot took his mind, he wandered the red battlefield swinging them at nothing, and devoured the dead for strength.
iron_helmet | The Lion's Helm | Maned like a red lion. His men loved him; when he went mad, they would not abandon him, and stood around him in the desert for years, rotting with him, waiting for a hero who could grant him the death of a warrior. A festival was held for it.
leather_boots | The Little Horse | A small, sturdy, utterly loyal horse, which he rode from his boyhood until his death, though he grew to the size of a hill. He used gravity magic to make himself light enough to ride it. It is the most loving thing any warlord ever did. Nobody mentions it in the songs.
iron_chestplate | The Plate of the Red Lion | When the festival came and the champions came to give him his death, he called the meteors down on them and laughed. Most fell. The one who didn't fall struck the final blow. As he died, a star fell behind him. Everyone said it was his. Perhaps it was.
bow | The Long Shot | His last trick, from his youth: he fired arrows so huge and so far that they landed beyond the mountains. A town on the far coast found one embedded in their harbour wall, the size of a ship's mast. They never pulled it out. It's a landmark now. They say it's for luck.

@ halfwolf | The Half-Wolf Who Guarded the Prince
> She made him to guard her son, from blood and fur and loyalty. He was half a wolf and half a warrior and all of him was hers, and her son's. When the prince went missing, the half-wolf searched the whole broken land for him, alone, and said, every time he met a stranger: "I'm looking for my brother."
iron_sword | The Greatsword of the Wolf | He swung it as if it weighed nothing, a blade almost as tall as he was. He spared everyone who did not stand between him and his prince. He was gentle with fools. He was gentle with children. He was not gentle with anyone else.
leather_chestplate | The Cloak of the Wolf | Grey fur on grey fur. He wore it in the rain on the high cliffs, howling at the moon on the nights he couldn't sleep. Strangers who heard him thought it was a beast. Strangers who met him found a sad, kind, enormous man who just wanted to find his brother.
leather_boots | The Search | He walked every road in the broken land, asking. Most people didn't know. Some lied to him. He believed them all, because he did not know how not to, and found nothing, and kept walking. His loyalty was a kind of madness. He never knew it.
shield | The Betrayal | At last the prince's guardian, who had been told to hold him, did as he was told, and chained the half-wolf in a cell, in case he stopped the prince from his terrible plan. The half-wolf broke free. He was too late. He went mad with grief in the tower and fought the wind.
iron_helmet | Brother | The stranger who'd helped him find the tower came back for him, and found him mad, and had to give him peace. As he died he said: "thank you, friend. I'm sorry." Then: "I'll go to my brother now." He believed it. Everybody who has heard the story hopes he was right.

@ liarcat | The Liar of the Roads
> On every road in the northern kingdom, sooner or later, you'll meet a ragged cat-man in a coat who will tell you, with great confidence, something that is very slightly untrue. Nobody knows where he lives. He is always just there, wherever you are. He says he knows much that he does not tell. He is probably lying.
leather_chestplate | His Coat | Patched, ancient, the colour of road dust. He says it was given to him by a king. He also says that the king had no tail, and that this made the king very sad. Kings do not have tails, you point out. "This is why the king was sad," he says.
leather_boots | The Walking Boots | He says he once walked from one end of the kingdom to the other in a single day. He says he did it by walking very fast. He also says he does not like to walk, and that the kingdom is smaller than it looks. You realise, after you leave, that the kingdom is in fact quite small.
iron_sword | The Sword He Doesn't Have | He says he does not carry a sword. He is carrying a sword. You point this out. He says that this is a different sword, and so it does not count, and that the sword he does not carry is much better. He knows much that he does not tell.
fishing_rod | The Fishing Advice | He says the fish in the north kingdom are very wise, and know your name, and will only bite if you whisper it to them. You laugh. Then you try it. You catch a fish. He is gone when you turn round. He is always gone when you turn round.
shield | The One True Thing | He says, the last time you meet him, that everything he has ever told you was true. You laugh again. He says: "the cat that tells only truths has no friends. The cat that tells only lies has no friends. The cat that tells both has many friends." Then he's gone.

@ forsale | Everything's for Sale, My Friend
> In the market town at the foot of the mountain, there's a shopkeeper who will buy anything you bring him and sell you anything you ask for, and who has, on at least one documented occasion, offered to sell his own sister. "Everything's for sale, my friend," he says. He means everything. He has never once been joking.
leather_chestplate | The Shop Apron | He has worn the same apron for thirty years. It is not for sale. It is the only thing in the shop not for sale. Several adventurers have offered him enormous sums for it, just to see. He refused. "The apron," he says, "is the shop. Without the apron, I am just a man in a room full of junk."
iron_sword | Stolen Goods | He buys stolen goods, which is illegal, at very low prices, and sells them back to their owners, which isn't. One man sold him a sword, and the next morning the owner of the sword came in and bought it back. Then the thief came back and bought it again. He sold it a fourth time that week.
iron_helmet | The Helm of Thirty Owners | This helmet has passed through his shop thirty times. He knows, because he scratches a mark inside it every time he sells it. He likes the helmet. He says it's always nice to see an old friend come in. He says this about most things. He says this about most customers, too.
shield | The Sister | His sister runs the shop with him and she has heard him offer to sell her many times. She says she's never been insulted, because he always quotes a very high price. "If someone ever pays it," she says, "I'll go with them, because frankly, they'll have earned me."
golden_helmet | The Price of Everything | A rival shop opened across the square and the owner offered him a fortune to close and leave town. He took it. He opened again the next day, a street over, with a sign saying "everything's for sale, my friend". The rival's shop closed in a month. Some things aren't for sale.

@ threedays | The Town of Three Days
> Over a certain clock-tower town, the moon was falling. It had a face, and the face was angry, and in three days the moon would land. A boy in green came to the town and, on the last night, played a song on his flute that turned time back to the morning of the first day. He did it again. And again. He lived those three days for years.
leather_helmet | The Green Cap | The townsfolk never remembered him. Every first morning, he walked through the town gate and they looked at him as if he'd never been there before. He knew all of them by then: what they needed, what they'd lost, who they were waiting for. Nobody knew him at all.
leather_boots | The Running Boots | In three days you can only do so much. He ran everywhere. He learned every schedule in the town, every door that opened at a certain hour, every person who passed a certain corner at noon. He knew the town better than anyone ever had. It didn't know he existed.
fishing_rod | The Couple | Two lovers in the town had been separated, and would only meet again on the last night, right before the moon came down. He brought them together, again and again, every time the three days came round. They married at dawn on the last morning. Then he played the song, and they forgot.
iron_sword | The Masks | The people he helped gave him masks, which he kept when time went back, though nothing else did. He has a wall of them. Each one is a person who, three days later, didn't remember him. He says the masks are proof. Proof of what, he doesn't say.
shield | The Last Night | On the last night of the last time, he stopped the moon. The town woke on the fourth day for the first time in years, and sang. Nobody knew what he'd done. He walked out through the gate into the woods, alone, and the couple, holding hands, waved to a stranger they'd never met.

@ crowsperch | The Baron of Crow's Perch
> The Baron of Crow's Perch was a drunk, and a brute, and a good soldier, and he loved his wife and his daughter, and they both left him one night and never came back. He hired a monster hunter to find them. The hunter found everything. Nobody in the story ends up happy. Some of them end up free.
iron_sword | The Baron's Sword | He'd been a soldier before he was a baron, and a good one, he'd tell you, between drinks. His men loved him. His servants feared him. He swore he'd never once raised his sword in his own house. That was true. He'd raised other things. He knew it.
golden_helmet | The Baron's Coronet | Gold, dented, set on a skull that was always hungover. He wore it to dinner, where he sat at the head of an empty table set for three, every night, waiting for his wife and daughter to come home. They didn't. The servants cleared the plates every night, untouched.
leather_chestplate | The Daughter's Coat | His daughter, the hunter learned, had run off to the city to fight in a war. She was fine. She sent no word to her father. When the hunter told her he was looking for her, she said: "let him look." The hunter told the baron she was safe. He wept for an hour.
shears | The Wife's Thread | His wife had gone into the swamp, to three old women who took her in and took something else from her, as they always do. The hunter found her. She was not well. The baron took her away to the mountains to find a cure. It's not clear there was one.
iron_axe | The Tree | Some versions of the story end with the baron in the courtyard, under a tree, by himself, and his men cutting him down in the morning. Some end with the baron in the mountains with his wife. The hunter always says he doesn't remember which. He remembers.

@ glasscaravan | The Caravan of the Glass Desert
> Out in the far south there's a desert where the sand fused into glass in some long-ago fire. It shines like a mirror in the day and burns your eyes; it rings like a bell at night when the wind passes over. Caravans cross it once a year, on the longest night. This is the tale of the year a caravan didn't come back, and then did.
leather_helmet | The Veil | The caravan travellers wear wrapped veils of pale cloth against the glare. A woman called Sefa led the caravan that year, in her grandmother's veil, which had crossed the desert sixty times. She was twenty-two. It was her first crossing as leader.
leather_boots | The Wrapped Boots | Your boots must be wrapped in three layers of wool, or the glass will cut you to the bone. One of the travellers forgot. She carried him for four days. He married her the next spring. He says it was the best decision of his life to forget. She says that's how he still is.
iron_shovel | The Storm | On the fourth night a sandstorm came, and the glass sang so loud it deafened them, and the caravan dug into the sand with these and lay still for three days. They had no water left. Sefa was the only one who could still hear. She heard something else, out in the storm.
shears | What She Followed | A cat, a desert ocelot, sitting on a dune, watching. When the storm stopped, it got up and walked away. She followed it. The caravan followed her. The cat led them to a spring nobody had ever found in the glass desert. They named it after her. She named it after the cat.
fishing_rod | The Return | When they walked out of the glass desert, a month late, the whole town was at the edge, waiting, dressed for funerals. Sefa's grandmother pushed through the crowd and took the veil off Sefa's head and kissed her. Then she put it back on and said: "you'll do."

@ spiderqueen | The Spider Queen of Webhollow
> Under the forest of Webhollow there's a cave, and in the cave there's a queen, and the queen has eight legs and a hundred children and a terrible, patient hunger. Hunters went in for her, over the years. A few came back. They all came back very quiet, and wrapped their wrists in cloth for the rest of their lives.
iron_sword | The Hunter's Sword | A hunter called Brask went in first with this. He came back after a week, wrapped in silk from the neck down, standing upright, alive, at the forest's edge, unable to move. He was put there as a warning. He says the queen spoke to him, the whole week. He won't say what about.
leather_boots | The Sticky Boots | The tunnels under Webhollow are hung with webs so thick you have to cut your way through. These boots were found stuck to the floor of one, empty. The webs around them were very neatly arranged, almost decorative, like a gift-wrap. The owner was not found.
shears | The Silk-Cutters | Brask's daughter went in after him, years later, with these, cutting every web she found. She reached the queen's chamber. She says the queen was very large and very old and had been waiting. She says they talked. She left with her father's sword. The queen let her.
flint_and_steel | The Fire That Was Never Lit | The village wanted to burn the forest down to end it. Brask's daughter stopped them, and wouldn't say why. She goes into the cave every winter now, with a basket. She comes back a day later with silk, the finest anyone has ever seen. The village has grown very rich.
bow | The Bargain | Nobody knows what she gives the queen. Livestock, the village says. Stories, Brask says. His daughter says only that the queen is lonely, and old, and has eaten nobody for twenty years. She wraps her wrists in cloth like the others. Nobody has seen what's under it.

@ dadjokes | My Dad's Jokes, Ranked
> My dad has told me approximately ten thousand jokes in my life and I have laughed at about four. I am sixteen. He is undeterred. This is a ranking of the worst ones, each written on the thing he was holding when he said it, because Mum says if I'm going to complain I may as well do it properly.
iron_shovel | Number Five | He was digging the garden and I asked what he was doing and he said "I'm looking for my keys, but it's a long shot. Actually, it's a groundbreaking discovery." Then he kept digging, waiting for me to laugh. I did not. He said he'd "dig deeper next time". I left.
fishing_rod | Number Four | We went fishing and he caught nothing all day and then said "I guess I'm just not a reel fisherman." Then he said "sorry, that was a bit of a stretch. I'll stop. Just for the halibut." Mum laughed. I think she was being kind. She says she wasn't.
iron_axe | Number Three | He was chopping wood and his axe got stuck and he said "well, this is a bit of a sticky situation, I axe you." He'd been saving that one. I could tell. He looked so pleased with himself I nearly laughed. I didn't. He knew I nearly did. He hasn't let it go.
leather_boots | Number Two | I asked him for new boots and he said "fine, but they have to be the right size, because I don't want to put my foot in it." Then he said "I'll get them on Tuesday, because it's shoe-sday." Mum walked out of the room. I followed her.
leather_helmet | Number One | When I got my exam results, he put this hat on my head and said "this is a thinking cap. I've had it since I was your age. I thought you'd earned it." It wasn't a joke. I didn't laugh. I don't know why I'm putting it on this list. I wear it every day.

@ smithandbaker | The Blacksmith and the Baker
> On the same street in the same small town, for eleven years, there lived a blacksmith and a baker, who were in love with each other, and both too shy to say. Everybody in the town knew. Everybody in the town had opinions. It took an iron golem, a burnt loaf, and a very nosy grandmother to sort it out.
iron_sword | The Sword With a Heart | The blacksmith made the finest swords in the province. Every one had a little heart hidden in the pattern on the blade. He said it was his maker's mark. It was. He'd been making them since the day the baker moved in. Nobody had ever noticed except the grandmother.
leather_chestplate | The Flour-Dusted Apron | The baker baked the finest bread in the province. Every loaf she sold him had a little heart scored into the crust. He thought it was her baker's mark. It was not. She did it only on his. The grandmother noticed that, too. She notices everything.
iron_helmet | The Golem's Helm | One day the town's iron golem picked up the blacksmith and carried him to the bakery and put him down in the doorway and stood there, blocking it, until he'd said something. He said, "nice weather." The golem didn't move. He said, "nice bread." The golem didn't move.
flint_and_steel | The Burnt Loaf | Meanwhile, inside, she was so flustered she burnt an entire tray of loaves. The smoke came out the door. The golem still didn't move. Finally he said, "I've loved you for eleven years." She said, "I know. So has the whole town. You've burnt my bread." He said, "sorry."
golden_helmet | The Wedding | The grandmother organised the wedding, because she'd been waiting eleven years and wasn't about to let them make a mess of it. The golem gave the bride away. He wore a golden helm for the occasion. Every loaf at the wedding had a heart on it. Every sword, too.
