@ deepdarkscholar | Field Notes From the Deep Dark
> The scholar Imre Vass spent four seasons studying the ancient city under the mountains, where a blind giant hunts by sound and the walls are grown over with something that listens. He worked in socks. He spoke only in writing. His field notes are the only record of the city made by someone who came back.
diamond_leggings@ancient_city | Note One | "Do not run. Do not jump. Do not drop anything. The growths on the walls cry out when they hear you, and when they've cried four times, it comes. I found these leggings in a chest by the great portal-frame. I left my boots at the entrance. I'm writing this sitting very still."
iron_leggings@ancient_city | Note Two | "Second season. Wool on the floor deadens sound. I have laid a path of wool from the entrance to the centre. It's taken a month. My assistant sneezed on the first day of it. I will not record what happened to my assistant. I will record that I am now unassisted."
diamond_hoe@ancient_city | Note Three | "The city was built by people who lived here before the dark came. They farmed, it seems: I found this hoe, in a house, by a window that looks out on nothing. There's a mural on the wall of the sun. They must have remembered the sun. They must have missed it."
shears | Note Four | "Third season. I saw it. I was cutting a sample from a sensor with these, very slowly. It walked past me at arm's length. It has no eyes. Its chest glows with the souls it's eaten. It stopped. It turned its head. I did not breathe for a minute. It walked on. I wept, silently."
leather_boots | Final Note | "Fourth season. The giant isn't a guardian. It's a prisoner. The people who lived here made it to keep something from getting out. I don't know what. I'm going home. I left my boots at the entrance in the first season. They were still there. Side by side. Somebody had cleaned them."

@ cherrypainter | The Painter of the Cherry Grove
> In the mountains there are groves where the trees are pink all year round, and the petals fall forever, and never run out. An old painter has lived in one for thirty years, trying to paint it. She's done four thousand paintings. She says not one of them is right. Her village has them on every wall. They say every one is perfect.
leather_helmet | The Straw Hat | She paints outside in all weathers, in a straw hat with petals stuck in the brim. The petals fall into her paint, too, and onto the paintings, and she paints over them, so every canvas has a few real petals buried in it. She says that's cheating. The village says it's the best part.
shears | The Brush-Cutter | She makes her own brushes from the hair of the goats in the high pastures, trimmed with these. She says shop brushes are too stiff to paint petals. A goat-hair brush, she says, is soft enough to paint something that falls. The goats are not consulted.
leather_chestplate | The Paint-Stiff Smock | So covered in pink paint that it stands up on its own. Visiting painters come to the grove to learn from her, and she gives them all the same advice: "don't paint what it looks like. Paint what it does." Most of them don't understand. One, a boy of ten, did.
iron_axe | The Fallen Tree | One of the old cherry trees came down in a storm, and the village wanted it for firewood. She wouldn't let them. She cut it into panels herself, with this, and painted on them, the grove, on its own wood. She says those are the closest she's come. She says that's not close enough.
fishing_rod | The Boy | The boy who understood her advice comes every summer now. He paints too. Last summer, he painted the grove, and showed her, and she looked at it for a long time, and said: "that's right." He asked what she meant. She said: "that's what it does." She's never said that about one of her own.

@ derbyhorse | Turnip, the Horse Who Won the Derby
> Every spring the great derby is run in the capital, and the finest horses in the kingdom race for the golden horseshoe. One year, a farm horse called Turnip was entered by mistake, after a clerk misread the form. Turnip was old, fat, short, and mostly interested in apples. Nobody bet on Turnip. Except one small girl. With her whole savings.
golden_helmet | The Golden Shoe | The prize is a horseshoe of gold, which has been won by the same three noble stables for sixty years. The nobles laughed when they saw Turnip. Turnip ate their flowers. The nobles stopped laughing. Turnip went on eating their flowers. It set the tone for the day.
leather_boots | The Girl's Boots | The girl was the farmer's daughter, and she'd brought Turnip to the capital to sell him, and instead the clerk had written him into the race. She bet her savings, which were three coins, on him. The bookmaker asked if she was sure. She said Turnip was the best horse in the world.
fishing_rod | The Apple on a Stick | At the start, Turnip stood still, eating grass. The other horses were halfway round. Then the girl, at the rail, held up an apple on the end of her fishing rod. Turnip saw it. Turnip's ears went up. Nobody had ever seen a horse that fat move that fast. Nobody has since.
iron_sword | The Noble's Protest | The noble whose horse came second drew his sword and demanded the race be run again, as it was not fair for a horse to be bribed with apples. The judges consulted the rules. There was no rule against apples. There is now. It's called "the Turnip rule". It's very strict.
leather_helmet | Turnip's Retirement | The girl's three coins became four hundred. She bought the farm next door. Turnip retired to it, and ate apples, and never raced again, and was painted, by a famous painter, wearing the golden shoe round his neck. In the painting he is eating flowers. That part was not posed.

@ chickenjockey | The Terror of Little Wickstead
> Every village fears something. In Little Wickstead, what they feared was very small, very fast, and riding a chicken. A baby zombie, on a chicken, which raced through the village every night at a speed no zombie should have, and bit everyone, and could not be caught. They called it the Rider. The Rider was the worst night of everyone's life.
iron_sword | The Constable's Sword | The constable stood in the square with his sword and waited. The Rider came out of the dark on its chicken, clucking at full speed, and ran between his legs, and bit him on the ankle, and was gone. He said he'd never felt so humiliated. He said the chicken looked smug.
leather_boots | The Bitten Boots | Everyone in the village had a pair of boots with tooth-marks in the ankle. The cobbler started selling reinforced ankles. He sold out in a week. The Rider bit through the reinforced ankles. The cobbler said that was the most impressive thing he'd seen in his life.
fishing_rod | The Trap | The farmer's wife said the Rider only stayed out so late because the chicken wouldn't go home. She sat at the edge of the village with a rod and a line, baited with wheat seeds, and when the chicken came past, the chicken stopped to eat them. The Rider fell off.
shield | The Capture | It took nine men and a shield to hold the baby zombie down. It bit all nine. When the sun came up, it burned away to nothing, and the village cheered. The chicken, left alone, looked around, and walked over to the farmer's wife, and sat on her foot.
shears | The Chicken | The chicken lives with the farmer's wife now. It's called Rider. It lays an egg every day and sleeps in a basket and seems to have entirely forgotten its past. On some nights, though, the farmer's wife says, it stands at the window, and looks out at the dark, and clucks very quietly. As if calling.

@ seertuesday | The Seer Who Could Only See Tuesdays
> The great seer Morwenna could see the future clearly, perfectly and in every detail, but only Tuesdays. Any other day was a fog to her. Kings came to her for prophecy. She would tell them, with total accuracy, what would happen next Tuesday. It was a very successful career, with very specific limits.
golden_helmet | The King's Visit | The king asked if he would win the war. She said that next Tuesday, he'd lose his left glove in the garden. He asked about the war again. She said the glove would be under the roses. He went home and lost the war on a Thursday. On Tuesday he found his glove under the roses.
leather_chestplate | The Seer's Shawl | She wore a shawl of deep purple, embroidered with seven moons, one for each day, only one of which was coloured in. People asked why. She said it would be dishonest to embroider the rest. She'd never seen them. She was very strict about honesty.
iron_sword | The Duel | Two knights fought a duel on a Wednesday and both asked her who'd win. She said she had no idea, but that on the following Tuesday, the winner would be having lunch with the loser's sister. Both knights were furious. On the Tuesday, she was right. They're married now.
fishing_rod | Fishing Tuesdays | She's never caught a fish on any day but a Tuesday. On Tuesdays, she catches exactly what she wants, every time, first cast. The fishermen of the town have stopped going out on Tuesdays. They say it's not worth it. She says it's the only day she can relax.
shield | The Last Prophecy | When she was old, her apprentice asked her what she'd see on the Tuesday after she died. She thought about it a long time. Then she smiled and said: "a girl in my shawl, telling a king about his gloves." The apprentice wears the shawl now. She's coloured in Wednesday.

@ grimsbybrothers | The Brothers Grimsby
> The Brothers Grimsby, Albert and Norbert, set out to become famous monster slayers, having read every book on the subject and fought nothing. Their first monster was a slime. It took nine hours. Their second was a sheep, by mistake. Their third, through no fault of their own, was a dragon. This is the story of how they lived.
iron_sword | Albert's Sword | Albert was the elder, and therefore, he said, the leader, and therefore he got the sword. He swung it like a man swatting a wasp. He missed the slime for nine hours, until the slime, out of what seemed like pity, split into smaller slimes, which he also missed.
shield | Norbert's Shield | Norbert got the shield, as the younger, and said this was unfair, until the first time something hit him, after which he said nothing, and hid. Norbert hid behind the shield in every battle. In the dragon battle, he hid behind it for eleven hours. It was a very good shield.
leather_helmet | The Sheep Incident | In the dark, on their second night, they heard something moving in the bushes, and charged. It was a sheep. Albert hit it with the flat of the sword. The sheep head-butted Norbert into a stream. They told everyone it was a wolf. It was a sheep. The farmer told everyone.
flint_and_steel | The Dragon | The dragon found them, not the other way round. It came down on their campsite at night, and Albert, waking, threw the flint at it, which bounced off its nose and struck sparks into the dragon's own oil-soaked wing, which caught fire. The dragon fled, screaming. Nobody believes them.
fishing_rod | Famous At Last | They are famous now: as the brothers who frightened off a dragon by accident and still won't stop talking about it. They tour the taverns. Norbert tells it. Albert interrupts. The sheep is a wolf in every version. They're making a lot of money. The farmer gets a cut.

@ bloodhound | The Bloodhound Knight
> In the age after the shattering, the noble houses kept knights who hunted down deserters and runaways for them, by scent. They wore masks shaped like hounds' heads. They walked on all fours when the trail was fresh. They were made to forget that they had ever been anything else. One of them remembered.
iron_helmet | The Hound Mask | Iron, shaped into a snout, with slits for eyes and holes for breathing that whistled when he ran. It could not be taken off: it was fastened at the back of the skull with a lock, and the key was kept by his master. He wore it for twenty years. He did not remember his face.
iron_sword | The Curved Fang | A single blade, curved like a hound's tooth, which he held in his teeth when he ran on all fours. He was so fast nobody saw him coming. He tracked deserters across the whole broken land and brought them back. What happened to them after, he was trained not to ask.
leather_boots | The Paws | His boots were cut away at the toes, for climbing, for running, for the grip of the ground. He was made to walk like a hound so long he couldn't walk like a man. When he finally fled his master, he crawled. It took him a month to stand. He did it alone.
leather_chestplate | The Deserter's Coat | The last deserter he was sent after was a girl of fifteen who'd run from the house rather than marry. He found her. She looked at him, at the mask, and said, "what's your name?" He didn't know. He stood there. Then he gave her his coat, and turned round, and went back alone.
shears | The Lock | He went back to his master's house, and found the key, and opened the lock at the back of his head with these, and took the mask off, and looked in a mirror for the first time in twenty years. Nobody knows what he saw. They found the mask on the floor of the hall. The house was empty.

@ tworivers | The Treaty of Two Rivers
> The Treaty of Two Rivers, which ended the eleven-year war between the river kingdoms of Aran and Vell, was signed on a raft, in the middle of the river where the two currents meet, because neither king would set foot on the other's bank. The negotiations took nine days. The raft is preserved. So are the minutes.
golden_helmet | The Crown of Aran | King Osmund of Aran arrived on the first day in full armour and crown, with forty guards on the bank behind him. The minutes record his first words: "I am here to discuss your surrender." The King of Vell's reply is not recorded. A footnote says it was "rude, and in dialect".
golden_sword | The Sword of Vell | King Teodor of Vell brought his sword to the raft and laid it on the table between them, unsheathed, as was the custom of Vell, to show he hid nothing. The King of Aran thought it was a threat. Nobody explained. The first two days were spent on the sword.
shield | The Third Day | On the third day, a storm came, and the raft broke loose, and the two kings spent eleven hours floating downriver, alone, together, bailing with their helmets. The minutes for that day are blank. When they came ashore at last, they were laughing, and very wet, and had agreed on everything.
fishing_rod | The Fishing Clause | The final treaty has one strange clause, number nineteen: "both kings shall fish together from the raft on the anniversary of its signing, for as long as both shall live." Historians argue about it. The kings didn't. They fished together every year for twenty-two years.
iron_helmet | The Last Anniversary | When the King of Vell died, the King of Aran went out on the raft alone, on the anniversary, and fished all day, and caught nothing. The minutes of that day were written by his steward. They say only: "the king fished alone. He set out a second rod. He did not take it in."

@ cornmaze | When My Sister Got Lost in the Corn Maze
> Every autumn the farm at the end of our road makes a maze out of the corn field and you pay a coin to go in. Last year my little sister went in and didn't come out. Everyone looked for HOURS. I'm the one who found her. Mum says I should write it down because I was very brave. I was a bit brave.
leather_helmet | Lily's Hat | Lily is five and she wears a yellow hat with a bee on it. She went into the maze running and laughing and I said "wait for me" and she didn't. That's how it started. When we came out she wasn't there. Mum went white. Dad went in. The farmer went in. Everybody went in.
iron_shovel | The Farmer's Shovel | The farmer said the maze was very simple and nobody could get lost in it. Then he got lost in it. We could hear him shouting "Lily!" and then "hello?" and then just "hello? anyone?" Dad found him. Then they were both lost. Mum said "for goodness' sake" and went in too.
leather_boots | My Wellies | I went in last because nobody remembered me. I didn't run. I walked very slowly and I listened. Lily hums when she's scared. She hums the bee song. I heard it. I followed it round the corners and the corners and the corners and it got louder and louder.
shears | The Secret Bit | She was in a little bit in the middle where somebody had cut the corn into a little room with a bench. She was sitting on the bench humming the bee song and eating a corn. She said "I found a secret room!" I said "yes you did." I sat down next to her and we hummed until she stopped being scared.
fishing_rod | How We Got Out | I held her hand and walked backwards the way I'd come, because I'd counted all the corners. Seventeen. When we came out, the grown-ups were all still lost inside. We had to wait an hour for them. Dad says I'm the bravest. Lily says the secret room was the best day of her life.

@ lastclock | The Clockmaker's Last Clock
> The clockmaker of Brindle made every clock in the town for fifty years, and kept them all running, every one, and when he was dying, he said he had one clock left to make, and asked for a month. He was given it. Nobody knew, until after, what the clock was for.
iron_pickaxe | The Mining for Parts | He couldn't buy the parts any more; the shops had stopped making them. So, at seventy-nine, he went down into the old mine with this pick and dug the copper and gold himself. His grandson went with him to carry it. His grandson says it was the best month of his life.
golden_pickaxe | The Gold Cogs | The cogs of the last clock were gold, because gold doesn't rust. He said he wanted this one to last. His grandson asked how long. He said: "longer than me. Longer than you. Long enough." His grandson didn't understand. He wouldn't explain. He went on filing.
shears | The Spring-Cutter | He cut the springs with these, one by one, very slowly, his hands shaking. His grandson offered to help. He said no. Then, near the end, he said yes, and taught him, and the grandson cut the last spring himself. The old man watched, and nodded, and said: "now you know."
leather_chestplate | The Workshop Coat | He finished it on the last day of the month and died the next morning, in his workshop, in this coat, with the clock ticking on the bench. It had no numbers. It had one hand. The hand was pointing straight up. His grandson didn't know what it meant.
iron_axe | The Hand Moves | Once a year, on the anniversary of the clockmaker's death, the single hand moves forward by one notch. It has moved thirty-one notches now. The grandson is old. He's worked out what it is. It's counting the years since. He says it was the old man's way of saying: I'm still keeping time.

@ trollbridge | The Troll Under the Toll Bridge
> There's a troll under the bridge at Gorsey Crossing who charges a toll of one joke per crossing, and if the joke's not good enough, you go in the river. He has a very particular sense of humour. He likes puns. He really likes puns. Over forty years he's heard every joke in the kingdom. Here are his favourites.
iron_sword | The Knight's Joke | A knight once tried to cross without paying, and drew his sword. The troll said "a sword? That's a bit sharp." The knight said "you're blunt." The troll laughed so hard he fell in the river himself, and the knight crossed. It's the only time anyone's crossed without paying. Technically.
fishing_rod | The Fisherman's Joke | A fisherman paid with: "why don't fish play tennis? Because they're scared of the net." The troll thought about it for a whole minute, then let him cross, and then, when he was on the other side, shouted, "it's not even a good one!" and threw a fish at him. He kept the fish.
leather_helmet | The Child's Joke | A little girl, crossing with her grandfather, paid with: "what do you call a troll with no bridge? Homeless." The troll went very quiet. The grandfather apologised. The troll said "no. No, she's right." He's been saving up for a house ever since. He's nearly there.
shield | The Toll Collector's Joke | The kingdom sent a toll collector to make the troll pay taxes on his toll. The troll asked him for a joke. The collector said he didn't do jokes, he did taxes. The troll said "that's the funniest thing I've ever heard," and didn't throw him in. They're friends now.
golden_helmet | The Best Joke | The troll says the best joke he ever heard was from an old woman who crossed every day for thirty years and told him a new one each time, never repeating. When she died, he closed the bridge for a day. The joke he liked best of hers? He won't tell. He says it's for her.

@ herbalist | The Herbalist's Ledger
> For thirty years I kept a ledger of every remedy I sold, and to whom, and why, and whether it worked. I'm passing my shop to my niece now, and I've decided she should have the ledger. Mostly so she knows what not to sell to the miller. And partly so she knows what this work actually is.
shears | The Herb Snips | Page one: sold the miller something for his back. It was chamomile. Told him it was a rare mountain root. His back got better. I never told him. I've sold him the same rare mountain root for thirty years. It's the most expensive chamomile in the kingdom. He swears by it.
wooden_hoe | The Herb Garden | Page eighty: grew foxglove in the back garden. Sold a little of it, very carefully, to the doctor for old Mrs Harrow's heart. Wrote, very large, in red: never sell this to anyone else. Niece, reading this: never. Some of what we grow will help, and some will kill, and you have to know which.
leather_chestplate | The Shop Apron | Page two hundred: a young woman came in and asked for something to make a man love her. I said there's no such thing. She cried. I gave her tea and listened. She came back the next week and bought soap. I've learned that most of what people want from me is somebody to listen.
flint_and_steel | The Brewing Fire | Page four hundred: the fever came to the valley. I brewed for nine days and nights without stopping. I kept a list of everyone I gave medicine to. Most of them lived. I've written the names of the ones who didn't on the inside cover. All of them. I read them every year.
leather_boots | The Last Page | Last page. For my niece: you'll make mistakes. I made hundreds; they're in here. Write them down. Write down the good ones too. The miller will come in on Tuesday for his mountain root. It's in the blue jar. It's chamomile. Don't you dare tell him.
