@ monumentprisoner | Eleven Years in the Monument
> A treasure diver went into the great sunken temple off the coast to steal the gold blocks at its heart. The ancient one-eyed guardians cursed him so that he could barely lift his hands, and he couldn't swim out, and he couldn't dig out. He lived there for eleven years, in an air pocket, with the fish. His journal survives.
trident | The Spear From the Hall | Day forty. Found this in the big hall where the old one-eyed things float. Took it while they weren't looking. They're always looking. I don't know what they are. They watch me through the walls. They're patient. I think they've been waiting for someone for a long time. I'm someone.
fishing_rod | The Rod I Made | Year two. Made a rod from kelp and a bent nail. I fish through a crack in the floor of my air pocket. I've named every fish I've caught. I eat them anyway. I apologise first. I think I'm going a little mad. The guardians watch me fish. I wave at them. They don't wave.
leather_helmet | The Diving Cap | Year five. My cap is rotten through. I made a new one from a dead pufferfish. I think it's a good hat. I asked the big one-eyed thing what it thought. It shot a beam at me. I think that's a no. I've decided we're friends anyway. Somebody has to be.
iron_pickaxe | The Pick I Couldn't Lift | Year nine. Every month, I try to dig out with this, and every month the curse comes back, and I can barely swing. I've dug eleven blocks. I need forty. I've started digging even when I'm cursed, one tap a day. A tap a day is still a tap.
leather_boots | Year Eleven | Year eleven. The big one died. I don't know how. Old age, maybe. The curse lifted. I sat in my air pocket for a whole day and didn't dig. Then I dug out. I'm on the beach writing this. I took one gold block. I left the rest. And a fish. For the little ones.

@ pressureplate | The Archaeologist Who Stepped on the Plate
> Professor Hemsworth-Pyle spent thirty years studying the desert temples, and wrote the definitive book on their construction, including a whole chapter on the famous trap in the hidden chamber beneath: a pressure plate, wired to a floor full of explosives. His expedition notes describe his first visit to a real temple. They are brief.
leather_helmet | The Sun Helm | "Day one. Arrived at the temple. Magnificent. Exactly as I described in chapter four. I have explained to the students that the hidden chamber is beneath the central blue block, and that the pressure plate in the middle is a trap, and that they must not, under any circumstances, step on it."
iron_shovel | The Excavation Shovel | "Day two. Opened the hidden chamber. Four chests, as predicted. The pressure plate gleams in the centre. The students are very excited. I have reminded them of the trap. I have drawn a chalk circle around the plate. I have put up a little sign. I have been very thorough."
leather_boots | The Professor's Boots | "Day three. I stepped on the plate." That's the whole entry. The next entry is written by a student, in a shaky hand: "The professor is alive. The temple is not. He was thrown seventy feet into a sand dune. He landed on his head, which the doctor says is the reason he survived."
leather_chestplate | The Scorched Coat | "Day nine. The professor has recovered enough to dictate. He wishes to add a note to chapter four: that the trap is very effective, that it is extremely difficult to remember not to step on a thing when you are looking directly at it, and that he was looking at the chests."
golden_helmet | The Second Edition | The second edition of his book has a new foreword, which reads, in full: "Do not step on the plate. I know you think you won't. I thought I wouldn't. I was the world's leading expert on not stepping on the plate. Look at the plate. Then look away. Then walk round it." It became a classic.

@ igloobasement | What Was Under the Igloo
> Out in the snowy plains there are little igloos, and under some of them, if you lift the carpet, there's a ladder going down. At the bottom there's a room with a brewing stand, two prison cells, a villager, and a zombie villager, and nobody has ever explained who built it. One family found out.
leather_boots | The Snow Boots | We were caught in a blizzard, my father and me, and we found the igloo and crawled in. There was a carpet on the floor. My father lifted it, because he lifted everything, he couldn't help it. There was a trapdoor. He said, "well, that's not ominous." He went down. I followed.
iron_sword | Father's Sword | At the bottom there were two cells with iron bars. In one, a villager, a priest, sitting very still, hands folded. In the other, a zombie. A zombie villager, groaning, pressing its face against the bars. My father drew his sword. The priest held up his hand.
golden_helmet | The Golden Apple | The priest pointed at the brewing stand, at a potion of weakness, and at a golden apple on the shelf. He said that was his brother, in the other cell. He said he'd been trying to cure him for nine years. He said he'd run out of gold. My father looked at the apple.
leather_chestplate | My Father's Coat | My father took off his coat, gave the priest the golden buttons, and threw the weakness potion at the zombie, and fed it the apple through the bars. It shook. It screamed. It took four minutes. Then there were two priests in the cells, holding each other's hands through the bars.
iron_shovel | The Way Out | They'd been down there so long that neither of them could remember why they'd built the cells, or who had locked whom in. My father dug them out through the snow. They walked away together into the blizzard. My father never got his buttons back. He said that was fine.

@ arrowtrap | The Jungle Temple and Uncle Fergus
> Uncle Fergus went into the jungle temple to show us how it's done. He'd been in a hundred temples, he said. He knew all the tricks. He knew about the tripwires. He knew about the dispensers full of arrows. He knew about the lever puzzle. He'd been in a hundred temples. This was his hundred and first.
leather_helmet | The Explorer's Hat | "Now watch," he said, "and learn." He stepped very carefully over the first tripwire, and turned to wink at us, and stepped on the second tripwire, which he'd forgotten. An arrow went through his hat. He took it off, looked at the hole, and put it back on. "Well, that's one," he said.
leather_chestplate | The Arrow-Proof Coat | It was not arrow-proof. He'd bought it from a man in a market who'd said it was. The second arrow went through the sleeve. He said the man had meant arrow-resistant. The third arrow went through the other sleeve. He said the coat was still doing its best.
iron_sword | The Lever Puzzle | The lever puzzle has three levers and only one right combination. Uncle Fergus said he knew it by heart. He pulled them. A section of the floor opened, and he fell into the pit underneath. He shouted up, "that's the wrong one!" We pulled the levers the other way. The door opened.
bow | The Bow He Got | In the chest at the end, we found arrows. Lots of arrows. Uncle Fergus picked up the bow we'd found and said, "now, this is how you use one of these," and shot an arrow, and it hit the tripwire, and set off the dispenser, which shot him in the leg. "That's two," he said.
shield | Uncle Fergus Today | He's fine. He walks with a stick now, and he tells everyone about the temple, and every time he tells it he gets past more of the traps, and in the latest version he defeats a jaguar. There was no jaguar. We don't tell anyone. We love him. He's been in a hundred and one temples.

@ minecartghost | The Ghost in the Minecart
> Deep in the old abandoned mines there's a stretch of rail that runs downhill into the dark, and miners say a cart comes down it every night, by itself, rattling, with nobody in it. If you stand on the track and say "hello," the cart stops beside you, and waits. Nobody knows who it's waiting for.
iron_pickaxe | The Miner's Pick | The mine was closed fifty years ago after a cave-in. Eleven men were lost. Ten were found. The eleventh was the cart-man, who rode the carts down to the bottom of the shaft every night to bring the ore up. His pick was found on the rails. He wasn't.
leather_helmet | The Lamp Helm | A miner who stood on the tracks one night and said hello saw a light in the cart: a little lamp, like the ones they wore on their helmets. When he looked closer, there was nobody there. Just the light. It waited for a minute. Then it rolled away, down into the dark.
iron_shovel | The Search Party | The miner's son, now an old man, went down with a search party fifty years after the cave-in, following the ghost cart into the deepest tunnels. It led them to a fall of rock nobody had dug through. They dug. Behind the rock, there was a cart, and in the cart, a lamp.
iron_helmet | What They Found | And there was the cart-man, sitting in his cart, as if he'd just stopped for a rest. He'd been trapped behind the fall. He'd waited. His son took off his own helmet and put it on his father's knees. Then they carried him out, in the cart, up into the sun.
fishing_rod | The Quiet Tracks | The ghost cart doesn't come any more. The tracks are quiet. The son, who is the oldest man in the village now, goes down sometimes and stands on the rails and says hello. Nothing answers. He says that's how he knows his father's resting. He says it's a good silence.

@ strongholdlibrarian | The Keeper of the Stronghold Library
> In the stronghold under the world, where the portal to the End waits, there's a library that nobody built a reason for. Its shelves go up into the dark. Its books are written in no language anyone speaks. And in one corner, behind a fallen shelf, a keeper still sits at a desk, writing, in the same strange hand. He has been writing since before the portal.
iron_helmet | The Keeper's Helm | He wears a helm of iron with no eyeholes. He does not need to see to write. He says, if anyone asks, that the words come up through the stone, and he writes them down, and that's all. He says the words are the world's dreams. He says they're mostly about cows.
leather_chestplate | The Ink-Stained Robe | His robe is black with ink. It drips from the cuffs. He has been writing so long that the ink has soaked through him; when he bleeds, which he has done twice in a thousand years, he bleeds ink. Both times, he wrote it down. Both times, the words were about rain.
iron_sword | The Sword at the Desk | There's a sword propped against the desk, which he has never used. He says it belongs to whoever comes through the portal and kills the dragon, and that he's been asked to give it to them. Many have killed the dragon. He hasn't given it to anyone. He says none of them was the one.
shears | The Page-Cutter | He cuts each page from the roll with these, and binds it, and shelves it, and the shelves go up and up. He has filled a hundred thousand books. He says he's about halfway. When asked halfway through what, he points up, at the portal, and says: "the dream. I'm waiting for it to end."
shield | The One He Gave It To | A girl came to the library once, who didn't come for the portal. She came for the books. She sat with him for a year and learned the strange hand and read the world's dreams. When she left, he gave her the sword. She asked why her. He said: "because you read the books."

@ endcaptain | The Captain of the Ship That Never Sailed
> At the edge of the void, beside the cities of the End, there float great purple ships with dragons' heads on the bows. None of them has ever sailed. They hang in the air, waiting. Aboard each one, there is a captain who has been waiting to sail since the ship was built. One of them, at last, got bored.
golden_helmet | The Captain's Hat | Purple and gold, with a feather from a bird that does not exist in any world. The captain wore it every day, on deck, looking out at the void, giving orders to a crew of nobody. "Hoist the sails." There were no sails. "Weigh anchor." There was no anchor. He gave the orders anyway.
diamond_chestplate@end_city | The Captain's Coat | Diamond, cut into a coat, which is the most uncomfortable thing anybody has ever worn. He wore it with great dignity. When a traveller climbed aboard to steal it, he took it off, folded it, and handed it over. "It doesn't suit me," he said. "It never has. Take it."
bow | The Ship's Bow | He kept a bow on the bridge for repelling boarders. He repelled none. He said boarders were the only visitors he ever got, and he couldn't bring himself to shoot company. He'd offer them tea. They'd take the treasure. He'd ask them where they were going.
leather_boots | The First Voyage | The last traveller he asked said, "home." He said: "where's that?" The traveller described it: grass, rain, cows, the sun. The captain listened for an hour. Then he said: "I should like to see it." The traveller said, "then come." The captain went down the ladder.
fishing_rod | The Captain Ashore | He lives in a cottage by the sea now, in the green world, in his purple hat, and sails a little boat every morning around the bay. He gives the orders out loud. "Hoist the sails." There are sails. "Weigh anchor." There's an anchor. He weeps every time. He says it's the salt.

@ griffin | The Griffin of the Orchard
> On the old trade road there's an orchard, and above the orchard, for one summer, there nested a griffin that took sheep, horses and one very unlucky tax collector. The village posted a contract. A monster hunter took it, with his apprentice, a girl of twelve, who was absolutely not supposed to be there.
iron_sword | The Hunter's Steel | He told her to stay at the inn. She followed him anyway, through the orchard, hiding behind every tree. He knew. He let her. He said it was the best way to teach someone about griffins: let them see one, from a distance, and then decide whether they still want this life.
crossbow | The Crossbow for the Wings | Griffins fly. You have to bring them down first. He showed her how, with this, from behind a cart: wait for it to dive, aim for the wing, don't rush. She watched him miss three times. On the fourth, it came down. He said missing was the most important part.
bow | The Apprentice's Bow | She had brought her own bow, which she'd been told not to, and when the griffin came down and turned on the hunter, she shot it in the eye. She was shaking. She hit it anyway. The hunter looked at her for a long moment, then said, "well, that's that decided, then."
shears | The Feathers | He cut a feather from the griffin's wing, and gave it to her, and said every hunter keeps the first one. She put it in her hair. She's an old woman now, a hunter herself, and she still has it. It's grey now. She says it was golden then, like the orchard in the sun.
fishing_rod | The Reward | The village paid him in apples and a bit of silver. He gave her half the silver, which was not the custom. She asked why. He said: "you earned it." Then he said: "don't tell anyone. They'll all want half." She hasn't. Until now. He's long gone. She thinks he'd laugh.

@ dragonpickpocket | The Thief Who Picked a Dragon's Pocket
> In the guild of thieves in the old northern city, the masters still tell the story of Silk Tamsin, who, on a bet, picked the pocket of a dragon. Dragons do not have pockets. This was the first objection raised at the time. Tamsin said that was the point: anybody can pick a pocket that's there.
leather_helmet | The Thief's Hood | Black, close-fitting, the hood of a master thief. Tamsin wore it to the dragon's cave, which was, for a professional, rather silly: the dragon could see in the dark and smell her from a mile off. She said the hood wasn't for hiding. It was for style. The guild agreed.
leather_boots | The Silent Boots | Soft-soled, oiled, utterly silent. She walked right up to the sleeping dragon in these and it didn't wake. She circled it three times. On the third circle, she saw it: a gold coin, wedged under one of its scales, glinting. A dragon's pocket. She smiled.
shears | The Lock-Tools | She slid a thin blade under the scale with these, very slowly, and the dragon shifted in its sleep, and sighed, and she froze with one hand between its scales for an hour. Then it settled. She slid out the coin. The coin was very old. It had a face on it. The face was the dragon's.
iron_sword | The Escape | She was nearly out of the cave when it woke, and said, behind her, in a voice like rocks grinding: "that was a gift from my mother." She turned. She held it out. The dragon looked at her for a long time. Then it laughed, a sound like an avalanche, and said: "keep it. Nobody's ever managed that."
golden_helmet | The Coin Today | The coin is kept in the guild hall, under glass, with a sign: "Picked from a dragon. Returned. Given back." Tamsin is the only thief in the guild's history whose loot was given back to her by the victim. She says that's the real achievement. The guild agrees. Mostly.

@ nomadbells | The Nomad Merchants
> Long ago, a caravan of travelling merchants crossed the broken land, ringing bells as they went, so the towns would know they were coming. When the great war came, the merchants were accused of a crime they did not commit, and buried alive, beneath the earth, all of them together. They sing still. You can hear it in the caves.
golden_helmet | The Bell-Ringer's Cap | The head merchant wore a cap of gold and rang the great bell at the head of the caravan, and towns ran out to meet them. He was the first buried. The others say he rang his bell all the way down, until the earth closed over it. They say it rang a long time after.
leather_chestplate | The Merchant's Coat | Thick, warm, full of pockets, each one full of things to sell: needles, string, seeds, little books. When the soldiers came for the caravan, the merchants took off their coats and gave them to the children of the towns, so the soldiers would think they were just children. Some lived.
shears | The Last Song | Underground, they sang. Not in anger. A song of the road, and of the bells, and of selling a needle to a woman in a town who needed it. A miner, a hundred years later, broke into the cave where they lay and heard it. He sat down and listened until it ended. It never ended.
iron_shovel | The Miner's Shovel | He came back with a shovel and a priest and dug them out, every one, and buried them again under the open sky, by the road, with a bell over each grave. The bells ring when the wind passes. Travellers on that road say it sounds like a caravan coming. They stop and wait for it.
leather_boots | The Child's Boots | One of the children given a merchant's coat grew up to be a merchant herself, and rang a bell as she went, and told every town the story of the caravan. Her children did too, and theirs. There are bells on that road still. Nobody remembers what the crime was. Everybody remembers the song.

@ trialthief | The Thief of the Copper Halls
> Under the hills there are halls of copper and tuff, full of trials: spawners that wake when you come near, and vaults that open only for the keys the trials give. The halls are guarded by breezes and by stranger things. One thief decided that trials were for other people, and went for the vaults directly.
chainmail_helmet@trial | The Disguise | She wore the armour of the halls' own skeletons, a rusted chain helm, and walked straight through the first trial, rattling, while the real skeletons looked at her and didn't shoot. She thought this was very clever. It was. Until the breezes came, who were not fooled by helmets.
iron_chestplate@trial | The Stolen Plate | She found this lying in an open trial chest, which was a trick: the chest was bait, and the spawner around it woke the moment she touched it. She wore it out of the room anyway, at a run, with thirty slimes behind her. She says the plate was worth the slimes.
crossbow | The Lock-Shot | The vaults open with keys. She didn't have a key. She shot the vault's lock with a crossbow bolt, nine times, from different angles. It did nothing. The vault doesn't have a lock. It has a key-hole that only takes keys. She sat down and laughed for a long time.
leather_boots | The Running Boots | In the end she did the trials, one by one, because there was no other way, and earned the keys, and opened the vaults, and found, inside, a single pair of leather boots. That was it. That was the treasure. She wore them out of the halls, furious. They're very nice boots.
diamond_helmet@trial | The Ominous Vault | She went back a year later, under a dark omen, and the halls were worse, and the trials were harder, and the vaults were black instead of copper. In the ominous vault she found this. She says the lesson is: there are no shortcuts. She says the second lesson is: there are better boots.

@ oldcouple | The Day They Swapped Chores
> For fifty-one years, Ada did the cooking and Henrik did the garden. Then, one spring, Henrik's knees gave out, and Ada's hands got stiff, and they decided, after a great deal of grumbling, to swap. It nearly ended their marriage. It also, their children say, made it.
wooden_hoe | Ada in the Garden | Ada had never held a hoe in her life. She attacked the garden like it had insulted her. She dug up the carrots to see if they were ready. They were not. She put them back. Henrik watched from the window, white-knuckled, saying nothing, which was the hardest thing he'd ever done.
iron_axe | Henrik in the Kitchen | Henrik had never cooked anything. He chopped the vegetables with his woodcutting axe, because it was the only blade he trusted. The carrots went across the kitchen. The table got a dent. Ada watched from the garden, through the window, laughing so hard she had to sit down in the cabbages.
leather_chestplate | The Apron Swap | She gave him her apron. He gave her his gardening coat. They both said the other one's was ridiculous. They both wore them every day for a year. Their grandchildren say they've never seen two people so happy to be bad at something together.
shears | The First Harvest | Her first harvest was three potatoes, a cabbage that was mostly holes, and a sunflower taller than the house, which she had not planted and could not account for. Henrik cooked the potatoes. They were raw in the middle. She said they were the best potatoes she'd ever eaten.
fishing_rod | The Anniversary | On their fifty-second anniversary, they swapped back, and agreed, at dinner, that they'd each been doing the other's job wrong for a year, and also, it turned out, their own job wrong for fifty-one. They go fishing together now instead. Neither of them is any good at it.

@ trades | The Butcher, the Baker and the Candlestick Maker
> In the town of Bramblebury, three shopkeepers on the same street have been at war for forty years: the butcher, the baker and the candlestick maker. Nobody remembers how it started. Each of them says the other two did it. The rest of the town has given up. This is the story of the year they had to work together, and the puns that resulted.
iron_axe | The Butcher's Cleaver | The butcher said he'd never work with the baker, because the baker kneaded too much attention. The baker said he'd never work with the butcher, because the butcher was a cut below. The candlestick maker said they were both very dim. Nobody laughed. It was going to be a long year.
leather_chestplate | The Baker's Apron | The baker said the whole feud was the candlestick maker's fault, because he'd started it with a wick-ed remark in the spring of '72. The candlestick maker said he'd never been so insulted. The butcher said that was a rare thing to say. The baker said it was well done. They all paused.
flint_and_steel | The Candlemaker's Flint | When the town hall burned down and the mayor ordered the three of them to rebuild it together, the candlestick maker said he was fired up about it. The butcher said he'd meat them halfway. The baker said he'd rise to the occasion. The mayor put her head in her hands.
iron_shovel | The Foundations | They dug the foundations together, in silence, for three days. Then the butcher made a joke about sausages, very quietly, and the baker snorted, and the candlestick maker lost it completely. They laughed for an hour. Nobody could remember, afterwards, which joke it was.
golden_helmet | The New Town Hall | The town hall was finished in the autumn, and the mayor held a feast, catered by all three. Meat pies, with bread, by candlelight. At the end, the three of them stood up and gave a speech together. It was entirely puns. The town gave them a standing ovation, mostly so it would stop.
