@ happyghast | The Ghast Who Carried Passengers
> Ghasts are the terror of the burning world: great white screaming things that spit fire. But once, a girl found a dried-up ghast in the soul sand, and put it in water, and it woke up small and soft and happy. It grew. It never learned to scream. It learned to carry people instead.
leather_helmet | The Goggles | She wore riding goggles so the wind wouldn't sting her eyes, flying high over the valley on the back of a ghast the size of a house. Everyone below pointed and screamed the first time. By the tenth time, they waved. By the hundredth, they asked for a lift.
leather_chestplate | The Harness | She made a harness for it out of wool and string, with four seats on top, and charged a coin a ride. The ghast flew slowly, very slowly, humming. Children loved it. Old people loved it. Merchants hated it, because it was faster than their carts and only cost a coin.
iron_shovel | The Soul Sand Shovel | She dug it out of the Nether with this, a hard, dry lump of a thing nobody else would have looked at twice. She says she knew it was alive because it was warm. Her father says she brings home everything that's warm. They have nine cats.
fishing_rod | The Snowball Trick | It won't go anywhere unless it can see a snowball, so she dangles one on this rod in front of its face, and it follows, happy, forever. It has never once caught the snowball. She says that's the secret of a happy life: something to follow that you never quite catch.
shears | The Last Ride | When she was very old, it carried her up above the clouds, one last time, where she could see the whole world, and set her down very gently on the top of the mountain. She's buried there. It still flies up every evening, alone, and hums.

@ armadilloknight | The Knight of the Scutes
> In the dry red hills there lived a knight who wore no armour of her own. All the armour she ever made, from the little plates the armadillos shed, went onto wolves. She said wolves had better reasons to fight than knights did. Her pack was the most feared in the hills, and the best dressed.
leather_chestplate | The Plain Coat | She wore this, and only this, into every fight. Her wolves wore heavy plate. People thought she was mad. She said she didn't need armour: she had eleven wolves who'd die for her. The wolves would have. They never had to. Nobody came close enough.
iron_shovel | The Scute-Collector | Armadillos roll up into a ball when they're frightened, and shed their plates when they're not. She sat with them for hours, very still, brushing them gently with a soft brush and collecting what fell off with this. They trusted her. Armadillos don't trust anyone.
shears | The Armour-Shears | She cut every plate to fit with these, measured to the wolf, every wolf different. The big grey one's armour had a dent in it from a pillager's axe. She said she'd fix it. He wouldn't let her. He said, in his way, that he was proud of it.
iron_sword | The Sword She Didn't Use | She owned a sword. She never needed it. In thirty years she drew it once, when a wolf of hers was cornered and she thought she couldn't get there in time. She got there in time. She says it's the only time she was ever afraid. Not for herself. She's never been afraid for herself.
bow | The Hills Now | She's gone now, but her wolves still wear the plates, and their pups, and their pups' pups. Travellers in the red hills sometimes see a pack of armoured wolves on the ridge, watching. They don't attack. They're waiting, the locals say, for her to come back.

@ allaysong | The Allay Who Remembered a Song
> Allays are little blue spirits that collect things for you, and dance when they hear music. This one was freed from a pillager's cage by a girl who played the flute badly. It never forgot her song. It's been looking for her for a hundred years, humming it, dropping cookies at every note block it finds.
iron_axe | The Cage Axe | The pillagers kept it in a wooden cage at their outpost. The girl broke the cage open with an axe twice her size, and the allay flew out and circled her head three times and stayed. She hadn't come for it. She'd come for her brother. She found them both that day.
leather_helmet | The Girl's Hat | She played a little tune on her flute every morning, the same four notes, and the allay danced. It brought her everything she dropped. It brought her things she hadn't dropped. It brought her the neighbour's cat once. She had to give the cat back.
leather_boots | The Wandering Boots | She grew up and moved away and the allay couldn't follow, because it only followed the music, and she stopped playing. It waited by the note block in her old house for years. Then it went looking. It's still looking. It's very small. The world is very large.
fishing_rod | The Rod by the River | An old woman fishing by a river, a hundred years later, heard a little blue thing humming four notes she hadn't heard since she was a child. She wasn't the girl. She was the girl's granddaughter. She'd been taught the song. She played it back. The allay cried.
shears | Home | The allay lives with her family now, three generations of them, and they play the song every morning, and it dances, and it brings them everything they drop. It brought them the neighbour's cat once. They had to give the cat back. Some things don't change.

@ blueaxolotl | Seventeen Hundred Axolotls
> I have been breeding axolotls in my cellar for nine years. I have one thousand seven hundred and eleven of them. They are pink, brown, gold, white and cyan. They are not blue. Somewhere out there, in one in a thousand two hundred births, is a blue one. My wife has left me. I am close.
iron_shovel | The First Tank | I dug the first tank with this, out by the lush caves, and put two wild ones in it, pink and brown. They had a gold baby. I was thrilled. My wife said it was very nice. I said, "but it's not blue." She asked what I meant. That was the first time she looked worried.
fishing_rod | The Tropical Fish | They only breed if you feed them tropical fish, scooped up in buckets from the warm sea. I have caught nine thousand tropical fish. The fish now flee when they see my boat. I am known on the coast as "the fish man". It is not a compliment.
leather_boots | The Wet Boots | Nine years of standing in axolotl water, sorting them by colour, counting. My boots have never been dry. My feet have never been dry. My doctor asked if I'd considered a different hobby. I asked if he'd considered that one in a thousand two hundred is not a lot.
iron_helmet | The Divorce | My wife took the house, the dog, and the good chairs. She left the cellar. She said the cellar was mine, and the axolotls, all of them. As she left she said, "I hope you find it." I think she meant it. I hope she finds what she's looking for, too.
shears | The Blue One | It happened on a Tuesday. A little blue baby, bright as the sky. I sat down in the water and wept. Then I wrote to my wife. She came to see it. She said it was beautiful. She stayed for dinner. She's staying for dinner again tonight. One in a thousand two hundred.

@ llamastrike | The Llama Strike of Caravan Road
> The traders of the long road used llamas to carry their goods, in caravans of ten, roped nose to tail. The llamas did not like this. They did not like the traders, or the road, or the weather, or each other. One spring, they went on strike. Nobody had known llamas could go on strike. The llamas knew.
leather_chestplate | The Trader's Vest | Old Ormond had run caravans for forty years and thought he understood llamas. On the first morning of the strike he went to rope them up and they all, together, turned and spat in his face. All ten. At once. It was very precise. He went back inside to think.
shears | The Carpet Shears | The llamas wanted carpets. Specifically, they wanted nice carpets on their backs, in good colours, instead of the old brown ones. Ormond cut new ones from his best wool, in blue and orange and red. The llamas inspected them. Two were rejected. They spat on those.
leather_boots | The Picket Line | They stood across the road, all ten, in a line, and spat at anybody who came near. The other caravans couldn't get past. Soon there were forty llamas in the line. Then a hundred. The road was closed for three weeks. Wandering traders came to look at it.
iron_shovel | The Negotiations | Ormond and the lead llama, a white one called Margot, sat across from each other on two hay bales for six hours. He offered carpets, shorter days and better hay. She spat once, at the start, to set the tone. Then she listened. Then she nodded. The road opened.
fishing_rod | The New Terms | The caravans of the long road now stop every two hours for a rest and a drink by a river, and Ormond fishes while the llamas graze. The llamas have nice carpets. Margot leads the caravan. Ormond walks beside her. She hasn't spat at him since. Mostly.

@ endergift | The Enderman Took My Flower
> When I was little an enderman came to our garden every night and took one block of grass and went away. Mum said not to look at it. I didn't look at it in the eyes. I looked at its feet. One night it took my favourite flower, a red one I'd grown myself. I cried. Then something happened.
leather_helmet | My Bobble Hat | I wore my bobble hat to go and wait for it. Mum said it would teleport and I wouldn't see it, but I saw it, tall and black and wobbly, at the end of the garden. I said "that's my flower" really loud, looking at its feet. It made a funny noise.
leather_boots | My Wellies | I walked down the garden in my wellies very slowly so I wouldn't scare it. I told its feet that I'd grown the flower myself from a seed and it took me AGES. The enderman stood very still. Then it was gone, and so was I nearly, because I jumped.
shears | Mum's Garden Shears | The next morning, in the middle of the garden, where the enderman had stood, there was a block of grass with a flower on it. Not my flower. A different one. A white one I'd never seen, with a purple middle. Mum said it was from the End. I don't know where that is.
wooden_hoe | My Little Hoe | I planted the white flower next to where my red one used to be. It grew. Then the enderman came back and took a block of dirt and left a block of grass with a red flower on it. MY red flower. It must have been looking after it. It must have known it was special.
iron_shovel | Dad's Shovel | Now every night it brings something and takes something. Dad says it's trading. Mum says it's lonely. I think it just likes flowers. Dad dug a special bed for its flowers by the fence. We call it the enderman's bed. Sometimes in the morning it's standing in it.

@ retiredhero | The Retired Hero's Garden
> I saved the world twice, by the way. Once from a dragon, once from a man who wanted to be a dragon. They gave me a medal and a pension and a cottage at the edge of a village nobody's heard of. I grow cabbages now. Nobody here knows who I am. I'd like to keep it that way. This is mostly about the cabbages.
iron_sword | The Sword Under the Bed | It's under the bed. I haven't touched it in twelve years. Sometimes in the night I hear something outside and my hand goes down to it before I'm even awake. Usually it's a fox. Once it was my neighbour, drunk, looking for his house. I gave him a cabbage.
wooden_hoe | The Hoe | The hoe and I took a while to understand each other. A sword, you swing. A hoe, you have to coax. The first year all my cabbages were eaten by slugs. I took it personally. The second year I fought the slugs. It's the hardest war I've ever been in. I'm winning.
leather_chestplate | The Gardening Coat | The village thinks I'm a retired accountant. I let them. When the raiders came last spring, I was in this coat with a trowel, and I'm afraid I forgot myself for about a minute. The raiders left. The village thinks they were scared of the dog. I let them.
leather_boots | The Cabbage Show | I entered the village show this year with a cabbage the size of a cow's head. I came second, to a woman who's been growing cabbages for sixty years and has, in her own way, saved the world many more times than I ever did. She deserved it. I've asked her for tips.
shears | My Retirement | They wrote to me from the capital last month. There's another dragon. They want me back. I wrote that I'm busy. It's true. The cabbages need thinning, and the woman with the cabbages is coming round for tea, and I'm making soup. Somebody else can save the world for once.

@ catmercenary | Whiskers, Sword for Hire
> In the taverns of the southern coast there's a mercenary who'll take any job if the price is right: escort, bodyguard, rescue, revenge. He's quick, he's deadly, he's never lost a fight. He's a cat. A big orange one, with a feathered hat and a very small sword, and a habit of falling asleep halfway through negotiations.
iron_sword | The Rapier | Tiny, needle-thin, it's one of the very few weapons in the world sized for a cat. He uses it with deadly skill, mostly to pop the buttons off people's trousers. A man with his trousers round his ankles, he says, is a man who is no longer fighting you. He's right. It works every time.
leather_helmet | The Feathered Hat | A wide, swooping hat with an ostrich feather, which he takes off with a deep bow before every fight, and puts back on after. He has never once lost the hat, in a hundred battles. He lost a tooth, two lives and most of one ear. Never the hat.
leather_boots | The Boots | Tall, black, and polished to a shine, the finest cat-boots ever made. He wears them on his hind legs only. He says that four boots would be vulgar. He walks on two legs in them, upright, like a gentleman. When nobody's looking, he goes on all fours.
fishing_rod | The Fee | He charges in fish. One fish for an escort. Ten for a rescue. A hundred for a dragon. He's done three dragons. He stores the fish in a cellar under a tavern, where they go bad, and he doesn't care, because he likes knowing they're there.
shield | The Weakness | He has one weakness, and every enemy who's beaten him has found it: a little red dot of light on the floor. He will drop anything to chase it. He's been beaten twice, both times by a mirror and the sun. He says it was fair. He says he'd do it again.

@ hundredyears | The Seventy-Year Game
> Two old enemies, a king and the general who overthrew him, were sent to live on opposite sides of the same mountain when the war ended, and never to meet again. They began a game of chess by letter. A move a week, carried over the mountain by a boy with a mule. The game lasted seventy years.
leather_boots | The Messenger's Boots | The first messenger was a boy of twelve. He carried the letters over the mountain for sixty years, until his knees gave out, and then his grandson carried them. Neither of them ever opened a letter. They say it was a matter of honour. They say they could tell, anyway, from the faces.
golden_sword | The King's Sword | The old king kept his sword on the wall above his chessboard, and looked at it every time he made a move, as if asking it for advice. He never once took it down. He said a sword can only end a war. A chess game can go on as long as you like.
shield | The General's Shield | The general's shield was split down the middle in the war and he never had it mended. He used one half as a tray for his chess pieces. He said the other half belonged to the king. He sent it over the mountain in the fortieth year. The king sent back a pawn.
iron_helmet | The Fiftieth Year | In the fiftieth year, the king wrote, at the bottom of his move: "I am sorry about your sons." The general didn't reply for three weeks. Then he wrote back his move, and below it: "I am sorry about your crown." It was the first time either had mentioned the war.
golden_helmet | Checkmate | Both men died in the same winter, in their nineties, within a day of each other, and the game was unfinished. The boy's great-grandson finished it for them, at a little table in the pass between the two houses. He says it was a draw. He says it was always going to be.

@ lastdragonegg | The Last Egg of the Dragon
> Once, the End was full of dragons. Now there is one, or there was, until somebody came and killed it, as somebody always does. When it died, it left an egg on the portal: dark, warm, and humming. Whoever holds the egg, the old songs say, holds the last of something, for as long as it takes to come again.
diamond_sword | The Slayer's Blade | The one who killed the dragon carried this. She cut down the crystals on their towers, one by one, then shot the dragon from the sky, and when it fell, it fell slowly, rising into light as it died. She says it was the most beautiful thing she ever saw. She says she wishes she hadn't.
bow | The Bow That Downed It | Every arrow she fired, the dragon screamed. The endermen watched without moving. When it fell, they made a sound she had never heard from them: low, all together, like a bell. She thinks they were mourning. She has never killed an enderman since.
leather_chestplate | The Egg Bag | She carried the egg home in a bag on her chest, close to her heart, because it was cold in the void, and the egg was the only warm thing. It hummed. She swears it hummed louder when she talked to it. She talked to it all the way back through the portal.
iron_shovel | The Garden Bed | She put the egg in a bed of obsidian in her garden, because she didn't know what else to do with it. It has never hatched. It has been forty years. Her children have grown up around it and they talk to it too. She says it's listening. She says one day it'll answer.
shears | The Last Watch | She's old now, and she sits by the egg in the evenings with these, trimming the flowers around its bed. Somewhere out in the void, she knows, another one will come back, because the dragon always comes back. She wants to be there when it does. To say sorry.

@ brewaccident | The Brewing Accident at Fennick's
> Fennick ran the finest potion shop in the city for thirty years, and never once had an accident, until the day he sneezed while adding the pufferfish. What came out of the cauldron that afternoon has never been identified. It got everywhere. It did different things to different people. Most of them have recovered.
leather_chestplate | The Apron | His brewing apron, scorched in eleven colours. It survived the explosion. It has never been quite the same: on hot days, it turns invisible for a few minutes, so Fennick appears to be brewing in his shirt. The apron seems to enjoy this. Fennick does not.
leather_helmet | The Apprentice's Cap | His apprentice was standing closest, and for the next three weeks, she could only speak backwards. Her first words after the accident were "on ho!" which, everyone agreed, was fair. She speaks forwards now. She still sneezes in reverse.
leather_boots | The Customer's Boots | A customer who'd come in for a healing potion floated up to the ceiling and stayed there until teatime. Fennick passed him tea on a broom. The customer was a lord. He said it was the most interesting afternoon he'd ever had. He's come back every week since, hoping.
fishing_rod | The Cat | Fennick's cat, who was asleep on the counter, woke up able to breathe underwater and has lived in the city fountain ever since. She catches fish. She seems very happy. Fennick visits her every morning with a rod, to see if she'd like to come home. She declines.
shears | The Shop Today | Fennick has a sign in the window now: "no sneezing near the cauldron". People think it's a joke. It isn't. Every year on the anniversary he brews the same mixture again, very carefully, without sneezing, to see if he can understand what happened. He never can.

@ brackenford | The Witch Trials of Brackenford
> In the forty-first year of the reign of the old queen, the market town of Brackenford put eleven women on trial for witchcraft over the course of a single summer. The parish records survive. They are written in a careful, frightened hand, by a clerk who, it emerges, was keeping a second record of his own.
leather_chestplate | The Clerk's Coat | The clerk's official record lists the charges: souring milk, laming horses, a hailstorm, a child's fever. His private record, found in the lining of this coat after his death, lists other things: the land each woman owned, and who wanted it.
wooden_hoe | The First Accused | Widow Ashby, aged sixty, accused of blighting her neighbour's crop. The official record notes that her own crop was untouched. The clerk's private record notes that her neighbour married her daughter the following spring and took the farm. He writes the name twice.
shears | The Midwife's Shears | Goody Marl, midwife, accused because a child she delivered was born with a crooked foot. She was acquitted, because the child's mother spoke for her at the trial, in front of the whole village. The clerk writes: "the bravest thing I saw that summer."
flint_and_steel | The End of It | The trials ended in late summer, when the eleventh woman accused was the magistrate's own sister. The clerk's private record ends there too, with a single line: "it was only ever about who could be blamed, and now it is them."
iron_shovel | The Graves | Four of the eleven did not survive the summer. They are buried outside the churchyard wall, as the law then required. In the parish record of the following century, a later clerk notes that someone had moved the wall. Nobody knows when. It encloses them now.

@ gravedigger | The Gravedigger's Apprentice
> I was apprenticed to the gravedigger at fourteen, which was nobody's first choice for me, least of all mine. Old Hob had dug every grave in the town for fifty years. He talked to all of them. He said the dead were the best neighbours you could have. He was mostly right. He was not always right.
iron_shovel | Old Hob's Spade | The spade had a name, which was Margaret, after his wife, who was buried in the first row. He said she'd want to be useful. On my first day he handed me Margaret and said: "six feet, straight sides, and say good morning to everyone on the way in." I did. Nobody answered. Mostly.
leather_boots | The Muddy Boots | You dig in all weathers. You learn things. You learn which graves are always wet. You learn which ones are always warm, even in winter. You learn not to dig on the full moon in the north corner, and you don't ask why, and Hob doesn't tell you, and that's that.
iron_pickaxe | The Rocky Corner | One grave in the old corner took us four days to dig, because the ground had gone hard as stone. When we got to the bottom, there was already somebody in it. Not old, either. Hob looked at him for a long while and filled it in again. We dug the new one somewhere else.
leather_chestplate | The Night Watch | Some nights Hob and I sat up in the graveyard with a lantern, because the dead don't always stay where you put them, in that town. Mostly they just wandered about a bit, confused, and Hob talked them back down. He knew all their names. He always knew their names.
iron_sword | The Last Grave | Hob's own grave, I dug myself, next to Margaret's, in the first row. Six feet, straight sides. I said good morning to everyone on the way in. That night, sitting with the lantern, I heard a voice from the first row say "not bad, lad." I've kept the sword handy since. Just in case.
