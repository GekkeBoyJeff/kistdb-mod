@ warriorjar | The Warrior Jar
> On the road through the broken land, you may find a large clay jar stuck in a hole, unable to get out, calling for help in a deep, cheerful voice. Help him. He is a warrior. He is a jar. He is stuffed with the remains of fallen fighters, and he means to become the greatest warrior of all. He is the most honourable creature you will ever meet.
leather_helmet | The Jar's Lid | He wears a little cap over his lid when it rains, so the warriors inside him don't get wet. He says they deserve dignity. He says this very seriously. The cap is knitted. He doesn't say who knitted it. Some things, he says, a jar keeps to himself.
iron_sword | The Sword He Doesn't Have | He has no arms, and so carries no sword, and fights by rolling into his enemies and shouting. He says a warrior's weapon is his spirit. Also his weight. He is very heavy, being full of warriors. Enemies flatten. He apologises to them afterwards.
shield | The Shield of Clay | He took a crack in his side in a fight with a great beast and was very worried about it, because a cracked jar leaks warriors. He held his side together for a whole battle, through sheer will. The hero he fought beside patched him with mud. He never forgot it.
leather_boots | The Long Roll | He rolled the whole broken land, from the green south to the frozen north, to find the strongest warriors and test himself against them. He mostly got stuck in holes. Every time, a passing traveller pulled him out. He thanked each one with a bow, which for a jar is a wobble.
flint_and_steel | The Last Battle | At the end, in the fire at the top of the world, he fought one last fight and shattered, and the warriors in him went free. "Well fought," he said, as he broke. "I go now to join them." Travellers say there's a little cairn of clay shards where he fell. People leave mud.

@ zombiewedding | The Day the Groom Was Cured
> Three days before his wedding, Tobin the blacksmith was bitten by a zombie, and turned. His bride, a cleric's apprentice, refused to call off the wedding. She said she'd cure him in time. The whole village said she was mad. The whole village came to the wedding anyway, just in case.
leather_boots | The Chase | First she had to catch him. He wandered off into the forest, groaning, in his wedding boots, which he'd been trying on when he was bitten. She tracked him by the boots, which squeaked. Brand-new boots always squeak. It's the only time she's been grateful for it.
golden_helmet | The Golden Apple | The cure needs a potion of weakness and a golden apple. She had the potion. She didn't have the gold. The whole village gave up their rings, their earrings, one gold tooth, to make the apple. The baker gave the gold leaf off his best cake. The cake was for the wedding.
iron_chestplate | The Barricade | She locked him in the church with the apple and the potion and waited, as you must, for the cure to work. It takes minutes. It feels like years. He groaned. He banged on the doors. He shook. Outside, the whole village stood, holding its breath, holding the cake without the gold.
iron_sword | The Best Man's Sword | His best man stood guard at the door with his sword the whole time, in case it didn't work. He'd known Tobin since they were boys. He said, afterwards, that he would not have used it. He said he'd have gone in. Everybody believed him. Tobin believes him most of all.
shears | The Ribbon | At noon, on the wedding day, the church door opened and Tobin walked out, green fading from his face, and said, "did I miss anything?" She cut the ribbon herself. The cake was plain. Nobody minded. They say it was the best wedding the village ever had. The squeaky boots are on the mantelpiece.

@ puddlefordlaws | The Laws of Little Puddleford
> The village of Little Puddleford has the oldest charter in the kingdom, and its laws have never been changed, because nobody can agree how to change them. Every law was made for a reason. Most of the reasons have been forgotten. The laws are still in force. Visitors are advised to read them first.
fishing_rod | Law the Fourth | "No man shall fish on a Wednesday, for Wednesday belongeth to the fish." Nobody remembers why. The fish of Little Puddleford, however, seem to know. On Wednesdays they swim right up to the edge of the river and look at you. It is deeply unsettling. People stay indoors.
leather_helmet | Law the Ninth | "Every hat worn in the square shall be lifted to the goat." There was a goat, once, who was mayor. Nobody knows how. The goat is long dead. There is a statue. Hats are still lifted to the statue. Visitors who don't lift their hats are given a small fine and a pamphlet.
iron_shovel | Law the Fourteenth | "No hole shall be dug deeper than a man is tall, lest he find what he is looking for." The law was made in the year of the incident at the old well. The records of the incident are sealed. The well has been filled in. The village digs very shallow holes and is very nervous about it.
golden_helmet | Law the Twenty-First | "The tallest man in the village shall be crowned King of the Harvest, and shall ride the cart, and shall not complain." The tallest man is currently a farmer named Gregor who has complained every year for nineteen years. He is fined a goat every year. He doesn't own a goat. He owes nineteen.
leather_boots | Law the Last | "Any law of this charter may be ignored by a child under the age of seven, on a sunny day, if they say please." This is the only law that is enforced with any enthusiasm. On sunny days, the children of Little Puddleford fish on Wednesdays, dig deep holes and refuse to lift their hats to anything.

@ heartthief | The Thief and the Guardswoman
> She was the finest guard in the city watch. He was the finest thief in the city. She caught him eleven times. He escaped eleven times. On the twelfth, she let him go. Nobody knows exactly when it happened between the first time and the twelfth. They're not entirely sure themselves.
iron_helmet | Her Helm | The first time she caught him, he'd stolen a ruby from the duke. She put him in a cell. He was gone by morning. On the floor of the cell, where he'd been, there was a single red rose. She thought it was mockery. She kept it anyway. She still doesn't know why.
leather_boots | His Boots | The fifth time she caught him, she chased him across nine rooftops in the rain and they both fell through the roof of a bakery into a vat of dough. He laughed so hard she couldn't arrest him for a full minute. She laughed too. It was the first time anyone had heard her laugh.
iron_sword | Her Sword | The eighth time, he'd stolen her sword, from her hip, while she was arresting him. He handed it back to her with a bow. She arrested him for that too. He said it was the best arrest of his life. She wrote in her report that the prisoner was "insufferable". She underlined it twice.
shears | The Lock | The eleventh time, he cut his way out of the cell bars with a pair of shears he'd somehow hidden in his boot, and on the way past her desk he left a note. It said: "dinner, if you catch me again." She tore it up. She taped it back together. It's in her drawer.
golden_helmet | The Twelfth Time | The twelfth time, she caught him stealing a crown, and looked at him for a long time, and then she put it back on its cushion, and walked him to the edge of the city, and said, "go and be somebody honest." He went. He's a locksmith now. They have dinner on Thursdays.

@ krakenteeth | The Teeth of the Deep
> Whalers who sail far enough out, past where the water turns black, come back with stories of something under the ships that is larger than the ships. Most don't come back. The ones that do bring teeth: white, curved, longer than a man's arm. They sell them in the ports. They don't sleep much.
trident | The Harpoon | Captain Morne put this harpoon into the thing, under the black water, with his whole crew hauling on the line. The line went taut. Then the line went down. Then the ship went down. Morne was found on a plank, three days later, holding the end of the rope. It had been bitten through.
iron_axe | The Line-Cutter | On the second ship that met it, the cook saw the arm come up over the rail, longer than the mast, and cut the line that tied them to it with this axe. He saved the ship. He never cooked again. He opened a tavern in the port, where he tells the story for free, and drinks for money.
leather_boots | What Came Up | A diver went down from the third ship, on a rope, to see what was under them. They pulled the rope up after an hour. His boots were tied to the end of it, laced together, neatly. Nobody had tied them. The crew sailed straight for home and burned the ship in the harbour.
fishing_rod | The Tooth | A fishing boat, years later, pulled up a tooth on a line, still warm, still wet, longer than a man's arm. The fisherman sold it for a fortune in the port. With the money, he bought a farm, as far from the sea as you can get. He says he can still hear it, some nights. Breathing.
shield | The Last Ship | Nobody whales past the black water any more. The last ship that tried was found drifting, perfect, every sail set, every bunk made, the dinner on the table, not a soul aboard. In the captain's cabin, the shield was on the wall, and behind it, scratched in the wood: "it was so patient".

@ badghost | The Worst Ghost in Grimsby Hall
> Every old house should have a ghost, and Grimsby Hall had one, and he was terrible at it. Sir Percival Grimsby died two hundred years ago falling down the stairs and has spent every night since trying to be frightening. Nobody in the house has ever been frightened. Several have been mildly inconvenienced.
leather_helmet | The Sheet | He started, as ghosts do, with a white sheet with two holes cut in it. He couldn't hold it up, being a ghost. It lay in a heap on the floor. The maid tripped over it, every morning, for forty years. She thought it was the laundry. She sent it to be washed. He was very upset.
iron_sword | The Rattling Sword | He tried rattling his old sword in the attic at midnight. The family thought it was the pipes and called a plumber. The plumber spent three weeks looking for the leak. Sir Percival was so embarrassed for him that he stopped, and the plumber was paid in full and very proud.
leather_boots | The Footsteps | He tried slow footsteps on the stairs at night, to frighten the new family. The children thought it was their father coming to check on them, and went straight to sleep, every night, out of habit. Their father had the best-behaved children in the county. He had no idea why.
shears | The Cold Spot | He tried making a cold spot in the drawing room, as ghosts do. The family put the cheese there. It's the best cheese room in the county. Sir Percival guards the cheese now. He says it's not what he imagined for his afterlife. He says it's not bad.
fishing_rod | The Reveal | One night the youngest daughter saw him, plainly, standing at the top of the stairs. She said "hello". He said "boo", weakly. She asked if he'd like to play cards. He's been playing cards with the children every night for six years. He cheats. They know. They let him.

@ maptonowhere | The Map That Led Nowhere
> In the market at Cog's End, a girl bought an old treasure map for a copper. It was clearly a fake: the coastlines were wrong, the X was in the middle of a field, and the writing was in a child's hand. She followed it anyway. It took her eleven years. It led exactly where it said.
leather_boots | The First Step | The map began at a cottage that no longer existed, on a road that had been moved. She found the old foundations by asking every grandmother in the valley. The last one remembered the cottage. She'd grown up in it. She said there had been a boy there who loved to draw.
iron_shovel | The Wrong Coast | The map's coast was wrong because it had been drawn before the great flood changed the shoreline. She dug where the old coast would have been and found a little wooden boat, buried, with "my ship" carved into it in a child's hand. She kept going.
leather_chestplate | The Field | The X was in the middle of a field that, she found, had once been the boy's family's farm. The farm was gone. The field belonged to a man who wouldn't let her dig. She worked his harvest for two years to earn the right. He liked her. He let her dig in the third.
iron_pickaxe | The Box | Under the X, there was a tin box, rusted shut. She opened it with the point of her pick. Inside: a toy soldier, a marble, a feather, and a note in the same child's hand. It said: "this is my treasure. if you found it, you are my friend." She sat in the field and laughed and cried.
fishing_rod | The Friend | She found him. He was eighty-seven, living by the river, fishing. She gave him the box. He opened it and looked at the soldier for a long time and said, "I'd forgotten all about these." Then he looked at her and said: "well. I suppose you're my friend, then." She is.

@ sweetrollplot | The Great Sweetroll Conspiracy
> For three years the city of Highcliff suffered a crime wave. Not murders, not robberies: sweetrolls. Every morning, from every bakery, from every windowsill, from the hands of children on their way to school, sweetrolls went missing. The guards made a joke of it. Then they made a task force. Then they made an arrest.
iron_helmet | The Task Force Helm | The Sweetroll Task Force was three guards, one of whom was mainly there because he'd been asking every citizen he met whether someone had stolen their sweetroll, as a joke, for years. They gave him the task force to make him stop. It did not make him stop. He was very good at it.
leather_boots | The Footprints | There were footprints at every crime scene: very small, very light, leading up walls and over roofs. The guards measured them. They were the size of a child's. They had claws. The task force spent a week arresting cats. None of the cats had sweetrolls. One of the cats had a very good alibi.
fishing_rod | The Bait | They set a trap: a single sweetroll, on a fishing line, on a windowsill, with three guards hiding in the room. At midnight, a small furry hand came over the sill. The guards jumped. The hand jumped. The sweetroll went out of the window on the line, with something attached.
shield | The Culprit | It was a raccoon. A very large, very old raccoon, living in the bell tower, with a hoard of three thousand sweetrolls, some of them three years old and petrified solid. The raccoon was arrested. The raccoon bit the captain. The raccoon was released on the condition it moved to the next city.
golden_helmet | The Medal | The task force were given medals. The joke guard, whose name was Hadvar, said at the ceremony: "let me guess. Someone stole your sweetroll?" The whole city laughed for the first time in three years. The bakers baked him a golden sweetroll. Somebody stole it the next morning.

@ chickenrevenge | Never Hit the Chickens
> In a certain green kingdom, every village has chickens, and every traveller is told the same thing on arrival: never hit the chickens. Most travellers think it's a joke. A young swordsman, on his first day, hit one. Just once. Just to see. What happened next is why the village has a memorial garden.
iron_sword | The First Swing | He swung his sword at the chicken in the village square, playfully, because he was bored. The chicken squawked and ran away. He laughed. The villagers didn't. An old woman put down her washing very slowly and walked indoors and shut her door and locked it. That was the first sign.
leather_helmet | The Second Swing | He swung again. The chicken squawked again. From every roof, from every fence, from every bush in the village, there came an answering squawk. Hundreds. Then the sky went dark. Not clouds. Chickens. All of them. Coming down. He ran for the river. They followed him.
shield | The Shield He Hid Behind | He held up his shield. The chickens pecked through it in a minute. He hid in the river. They waited on the bank, all night, hundreds of them, in perfect silence. In the morning they were gone, and the shield was there on the bank, pecked into a shape like a chicken.
leather_boots | The Running | He ran all the way out of the kingdom, in the end, and never came back. Travellers say that every chicken in that land knows his face. Travellers say he wears a hood everywhere now and won't eat eggs. Travellers say he wakes up screaming when a rooster crows.
fishing_rod | The Memorial | The village made a garden by the river, with a sign: "In memory of a young man's dignity. Never hit the chickens." The chickens like the garden. They sit there in the evenings. The village children feed them and are very polite to them. Nobody hits the chickens.

@ ratcontract | The Monster Hunter and the Rat
> The village elder of Muddlebrook posted a contract for a monster hunter: something in the cellar of the inn, terrible, enormous, eating everything. A monster hunter came, with two swords, white hair and a scar, and charged the full fee in advance. He went down into the cellar. He came back up an hour later with a rat.
iron_sword | The Steel Sword | He went down with this drawn, very slowly, step by step, into the dark, as he'd been trained to do. The cellar was full of chewed barrels and gnawed sacks and a smell like the end of the world. At the bottom, on a pile of cheese, sat a rat the size of a small dog.
golden_sword | The Silver Sword | For monsters, though this one was only gilded. He did not draw it. He looked at the rat. The rat looked at him. He said, very calmly, "this is a rat." The innkeeper said, from the top of the stairs, "it's a big rat." He said, "it's a rat." There was a long silence. The rat ate some cheese.
leather_boots | The Chase | The rat ran. He chased it. Round the barrels, over the sacks, up the stairs, through the inn, out the door, across the square, into the well, out of the well, and back into the cellar. It took an hour. Half the village watched. The other half placed bets. The rat was the favourite.
fishing_rod | The Rat Bait | In the end he caught it with a fishing rod and a bit of cheese on the hook, sitting on a barrel, while the whole village watched from the top of the stairs. He held it up by the tail. The village cheered. The rat looked very cross. He took it to the woods and let it go.
golden_helmet | The Fee | He gave most of the fee back, kept enough for a bath and a meal, and told the elder: "next time, get a cat." The elder said, "we have a cat. It's afraid of the rat." He looked at the cat. The cat looked at him. He said, "I'll take the job for nothing, if I can have the cat." He did. It's a good cat.
