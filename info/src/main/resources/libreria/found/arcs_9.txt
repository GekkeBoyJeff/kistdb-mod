> Hear the tale of four sisters of the shallows, who fished with spears and feared no tide: Ninna the youngest, patient as stone; Ada, who belonged to the sea; Ylva, who guarded the others and never spoke of it; and Hedda the eldest, who stood before the great guardian when it rose. Three came home.
stone_spear | Ninna's Spear | Hear now of the four sisters of the shallows, who fished with spears and feared no tide. The youngest, Ninna, bore stone, for she said a spear should be as patient as the sea floor.
copper_spear | Ada's Spear | The second, Ada, bore copper, which greeneth in the salt like weed upon a wreck. She said that a spear should belong to the sea, and the sea should know its own.
iron_spear | Ylva's Spear | The third, Ylva, bore iron, and she alone of the four was ever wounded, guarding her sisters from the drowned on the night the moon went dark. She spoke of it to no one.
diamond_spear | Hedda's Spear | The eldest, Hedda, bore diamond, a gift from a lord who loved her and was refused. When the great guardian rose from the deep, it was Hedda who stood before it. She did not come back up.
iron_spear | The Spear Left on the Shore | Three sisters came home. They drove Ylva's spear into the sand where Hedda went down, and every year, on the dark of the moon, they sit by it and say her name to the water.

> Chainmail Charlie played every knight in every play in every village for thirty years, in the same jingling armour, with total commitment and no talent whatsoever. Critics hated him. Audiences adored him. He would want you to know that he took both of those as compliments.

chainmail_chestplate | The Coat That Jingled | It jingled during every dramatic pause. Audiences thought it was intentional. Critics called it "a bold choice". Charlie took the credit and the jingle stayed in every show.
chainmail_leggings | The Understudy's Leggings | Charlie's understudy wore these the one night Charlie was ill. The understudy was brilliant. Charlie was never ill again. Out of principle.
chainmail_boots | The Final Bow | His last performance was in a village that had just survived a raid. Nobody had laughed in weeks. He played the clumsiest knight in history, and the whole village laughed until morning.

shield | The Painted Moon | A shield painted as the moon, for a play about a knight who fell in love with it. Every night he held it up and said his lines to it, and somebody in the audience always cried. Usually Charlie.

> Private Gale was sixteen when he signed up, and he wrote to his mother every single week: about his helmet, the songs, the marching, the pocket with a button. These are his letters, in order. You know how this kind of story ends. Read them anyway. He'd want you to.

leather_boots | The Fourth Letter | "Dear Mum, tomorrow we go into the pass. I'm not scared, mostly. If I'm late writing, it's because there's no post up there. Don't worry. Make the pie. I'll be home for the pie."

@ ostra | Granny Ostra's Three Hoes
> Granny Ostra has started a garden in every place she has ever lived, with three hoes in sixty years: the one her grandmother gave her, the one her husband brought back from his travels, and the one her granddaughter bought her. Every one of them came with the same advice. Start a new one.
wooden_hoe@archaeology | The Wooden One | Granny Ostra's grandmother's hoe, dug up from the old garden after the village moved down the valley. Ostra was a girl then. She cried for the garden. Her grandmother handed her this and said: "Then start a new one."
golden_hoe@ruined_portal | The Golden One | Ostra's husband brought it back from his travels, a hoe of solid gold, completely useless for farming. She used it every day for forty years. Out of stubbornness and love, in about equal amounts.
diamond_hoe | The Diamond One | Her granddaughter's, bought with her first wages and tested in the old woman's garden. Granny Ostra looked at it, looked at the girl, and said: "Fancy. Now start a new garden." So she did.
leather_helmet | Ostra's Sun Hat | Her garden hat, with a brim so wide that birds once nested on it while she was weeding. She weeded around them for three weeks rather than disturb them. She said it was the best company she'd had all summer.
shears | The Deadheading Snips | For cutting off the flowers that are finished so the plant puts out new ones. Ostra said it was the most important job in a garden, and in a life. She was never sentimental about it. She just kept snipping.

@ notdiamond | The Fable of the Greedy Delver
> Gather round, children, for a fable. There was once a delver who found a diamond, and then wanted another, and then another, and then didn't stop. There is a moral at the end of this story. It's a very good moral. It's about lunch.
diamond_pickaxe | The Pick of Wanting | There was once a delver who found a diamond, and made of it a pick, and with the pick dug for more diamonds, for one is never enough to a delver who has one.
diamond_shovel | The Shovel of More | With his diamonds he made a shovel, to dig faster, so as to find more diamonds, to make more tools, to dig faster still. He forgot what the diamonds were for. He forgot to eat lunch.
diamond_axe | The Axe of Alone | He cut down the trees around his house, so that no one could see his diamonds. Then there was no one to see them at all. He showed them to his own reflection. It was not impressed.
diamond_sword | The Sword of Enough | At the end he made a sword, to guard his diamonds, and sat with it at the bottom of his mine, guarding, until he was old. What is the moral? Have lunch. Show your friends your diamonds. Go outside.
diamond_chestplate | The Plate of Too Late | When he was very old he made himself a breastplate of diamonds, so he could wear them at last, outside, where people could see. There was nobody left to see. He sat in the sun anyway. It was, he admitted, very nice.

@ apprentice | The Apprentice's Pieces
> Wenna was apprenticed to the best smith in the valley at fourteen, and she made something every week: crooked, sharp at the wrong end, too heavy to lift. Her master kept every one of them. These are her practice pieces, in order, and the last thing they made together.
iron_sword | Practice Piece One | Wenna's first sword. Her master looked at it for a long time and said, "Well, it is certainly sharp at one end." It was, in fact, sharp at both ends. She learned to hold it carefully.
iron_axe | Practice Piece Two | "The head's on crooked," said her master. "It's on at a jaunty angle," said Wenna. He laughed so hard he burned his beard on the forge. He kept the axe. It chops wood just fine.
iron_pickaxe | Practice Piece Three | Her first pick that the miners actually wanted to buy. She sold it for three emeralds and then followed the miner to the mountain to watch it work. He let her watch for an hour.
iron_shovel | Practice Piece Four | She hid her mark on the inside of the socket, a tiny bird, where nobody would look. Her master found it at once. He said nothing. He added his own mark next to hers, an even tinier bird.
iron_spear | Her Master's Piece | Made the year her master's hands got too shaky for the hammer. She made it to his design while he talked her through every strike. It's the best thing either of them ever made.
