# Stories, thirty-first book: fables, a case, a myth, a tall tale and other shapes of story.

@ missingsaddle | The Case of the Vanished Saddle
> Inspector Pell of the Cobbleford watch was called to the stables of Lady Ashby one wet Tuesday: her prize saddle had vanished in the night from a locked tack room. Five people had a key. Each left something behind. Pell solved it in a day. He wrote it all up, in the dry style of a man who has seen far too many saddles.
chainmail_helmet | Clue One: The Guard's Coif | Found in the straw by the tack room door, a chainmail coif belonging to the night guard. The guard said he'd taken it off because it itched. Pell noted that the coif was dry, though the guard had come in from the rain. He said nothing. He wrote "dry" in his notebook, twice.
shears | Clue Two: The Groom's Shears | The groom's mane shears were found in the tack room, open, with a few red threads caught in the blades. The saddle's girth was red. The groom said he'd been trimming a horse. Pell checked every horse in the stable. None of them had been trimmed. All of them looked at him knowingly.
golden_shovel | Clue Three: The Gardener's Spade | A golden spade, stuck in a fresh mound of earth behind the stables. Pell had it dug up. There was nothing underneath but a very old and very surprised mole. The gardener said the spade was a gift from her ladyship. Her ladyship said she'd never seen it before.
crossbow | Clue Four: The Steward's Crossbow | The steward kept a crossbow on the wall of his office "for rats". It had been fired recently. Pell found the bolt in the stable roof, with a thin cord tied to it, running down to a pulley. "Rats," he wrote, "do not, as a rule, need a pulley."
leather_boots | Clue Five: The Lady's Boots | Lady Ashby's riding boots were by the fire, wet through, though she said she'd been in bed all night. Pell asked her where the saddle was. She said she didn't know. He asked again. She sighed and said it was in the lake, where it couldn't be sold to pay her late husband's debts.
iron_sword | The Solution | The saddle was her husband's, pledged to a moneylender who would come for it on Wednesday. She'd sunk it in the lake with the steward's help and staged the rest. Pell wrote "case closed: no crime that I can find" and went home. The saddle is still in the lake. So is the moneylender's claim.

@ threebrothers | The Three Brothers and the Spear in the Well
> There were once three brothers, and their father was dying, and he told them that at the bottom of the old well there lay a spear that would cure him, but that it could only be brought up by someone who did not want it for himself. The eldest went first. You know how this kind of story goes. It goes that way anyway.
stone_spear | The Eldest's Spear | The eldest went down the well with a stone spear and his pride, and at the bottom a toad asked him to share his bread. He said he had none to spare. He came up an hour later with nothing, wet to the waist, and said the well was empty. The toad said nothing. Toads don't, mostly.
copper_spear | The Middle Brother's Spear | The middle brother went down with a copper spear and his cleverness, and at the bottom the toad asked him to share his bread. He gave it half a crust and asked for the cure in return. The toad ate the crust and sat on it. He came up with nothing but a cold and a grudge against toads.
leather_boots | The Youngest's Boots | The youngest went down in his old boots with nothing at all, and at the bottom the toad asked for bread, and he said he was sorry, he had none, but he'd fetch some. He climbed all the way up and all the way down again with a loaf. The toad looked at him for a long time.
diamond_spear | The Spear in the Well | The toad hopped aside, and under it, in the mud, was a spear of diamond, which the youngest brought up into the sun. The father was cured by morning. The brothers asked what the spear was. The youngest said it was a spear. The toad lives in the kitchen now. It gets bread.

@ bighilda | Big Hilda of the Timberlands
> They say Big Hilda was born the size of a cow and grew from there, that she felled her first forest before she could walk, that she combed her hair with a pine tree and dug the great river by dragging her axe behind her one afternoon when she was bored. They say a lot of things about Big Hilda. Most of them are almost true.
diamond_axe | Hilda's Axe | The axe was made from a diamond the size of a cartwheel, which she found in her porridge one morning and had to do something with. Its handle was a whole oak. When she swung it, the trees didn't fall so much as lie down out of respect. The woods are still very respectful round her old camp.
golden_pickaxe | The Gold She Dug | She dug a hole one day to bury a bad mood, and struck gold, a whole mountain of it, and made a pick out of a lump of it, and went on digging. The hole became a valley. The mood stayed buried. The gold she gave to the loggers, who'd never been paid that well before or since.
iron_boots | The Footprints | Her footprints made the lakes of the Timberlands, all forty-one of them, from the day she went for a long walk to think. The fish in them have never seen anything as big as her and are said to talk about it still. It is the only known example of fish gossip.
leather_chestplate | Hilda's Shirt | Her shirt took a hundred sheep and a year of weaving, and was red and black squares, and when she hung it up to dry on the mountain the town below thought it was the sunset for a week. It's still up there, people say, on the peak, flapping. Climbers say it's a very big flag.
fishing_rod | Hilda Retires | When she was old, Hilda sat down at the edge of the sea and fished, with a rod made of a ship's mast, and caught a whale, and let it go, and caught it again, every day for a year. The whale didn't mind. They became friends. They're out there somewhere now. Fishing, probably.

@ tortoiseknight | The Slow Knight and the Swift Knight
> Two knights of the realm agreed to race from the castle to the sea, and the one who arrived first would win the king's favour. The first was Sir Swiftly, who wore almost no armour and rode the fastest horse in the kingdom. The second was Sir Plod, who wore all of it. Fables being what they are, you've heard this one before. You haven't heard this version.
golden_boots | Sir Swiftly's Boots | Gold, light as feathers, made for speed and admiration. Sir Swiftly was halfway to the sea by noon. He stopped at an inn for lunch, then for a nap, then for a game of cards, then for dinner. He said there was no hurry. He said Sir Plod would be lucky to reach the inn by Tuesday.
chainmail_leggings | Sir Plod's Greaves | Sir Plod walked. He could not ride; his armour was too heavy for any horse. He walked at a steady pace, without stopping, through the noon heat and the evening rain and the night, rattling. Farmers came out to watch him pass. Children walked beside him for a while. He thanked them.
iron_chestplate | Sir Plod's Plate | At dawn, Sir Plod passed the inn. Sir Swiftly was asleep at a card table with his face in a pie. Sir Plod did not wake him, because that would not be knightly. He did leave a note. The note said "good morning". Sir Swiftly woke at noon and read it and screamed.
iron_sword | The Sea | Sir Plod reached the sea at sunset on the second day, and stood in the waves in his armour, and wept, because he had never seen the sea before. The king's favour was his. He asked for a cottage by the water. Sir Swiftly arrived an hour later, and joined him for dinner. They're friends now.

@ endermyth | Why the Endermen Carry Blocks
> Before the world was finished, the old tellers say, there were tall dark folk who lived in it and helped the maker build. Each carried a piece of the world from where it was to where it should go. When the maker went away, the work was not done. The tall folk were sent to the End. They come back at night, to keep working.
iron_pickaxe | The First Builder | The first of the tall folk took stone from the deep places and set it up as mountains. It was very careful. Every mountain in the world, the tellers say, was set in place by a hand long and dark and patient. They say if you climb high enough, you can still see the fingerprints.
leather_boots | The Wanderer | The tall folk do not walk the way we do. They step, and then they are somewhere else. The tellers say it is because they know where everything belongs, and they are always where they are needed. When an enderman takes a block from your house, the tellers say, it was in the wrong place.
shears | The Flower | A shepherd once caught an enderman moving his wall, stone by stone, a foot to the left. He shouted. It vanished. That winter, for the first time, the wind did not get through the wall. The tellers say this is how the tall folk say sorry. They don't like being looked at. They like being thanked.
iron_shovel | The Unfinished Work | The tellers say that when the world is finally finished, when every block is in its proper place, the tall folk will stop coming at night, and the dragon will sleep, and the maker will come back to look. Nobody knows when that will be. The endermen are working on it. Every night. Don't stare.

@ goatelection | The Election at Little Puddleford
> Little Puddleford elects its mayor every five years by putting candidates in a field and letting the villagers stand next to the one they want. This year there were three candidates: the sitting mayor, a scarecrow entered as a protest, and a goat that wandered into the field and refused to leave, exactly as the village's first goat mayor once had. The result is still being argued about.
golden_helmet | The Mayor's Chain | The sitting mayor had been mayor for twenty years and wore a gold chain and a gold hat of office so heavy they bent his neck. His campaign promise was "more of the same". The village had heard this promise four times. On polling day, eleven people stood next to him. Four of them were his relatives.
leather_helmet | The Scarecrow's Hat | The scarecrow was entered by the young people of the village, who said it would do less harm than the mayor. It stood in the field all day without moving. Its supporters stood next to it, chanting. It got fourteen votes. It also got two crows, who were not registered.
leather_boots | The Goat's Victory | The goat walked into the field at noon, ate the mayor's speech, knocked over the scarecrow and stood on the stage. By evening thirty-one people were standing next to it, for reasons none could explain afterwards. The goat was declared mayor. The goat ate the declaration.
golden_shovel | The Goat's First Act | The goat's first act as mayor was to dig up the mayor's prize flower bed with its hooves. The village planted vegetables there instead. The vegetables fed the poorhouse all winter. The council has since passed a motion that the goat is "not bad, actually". It's up for re-election in four years.

@ piglinshepherd | The Piglin and the Shepherdess
> A piglin came through a ruined portal into a meadow one spring and saw sheep for the first time, and a shepherdess, and fell in love with both. He did not speak her language. She did not speak his. He brought her gold every day, because that is what piglins do. She did not know what to do with it. This is a love story. It is a very silly one.
golden_sword | The Gift of the Blade | The first gift was his golden sword, laid at her feet with a grunt. She thought he was surrendering. She gave him an apple. He had never had an apple. He ate it in one bite, core and all, and grunted so happily that she laughed, and he took this as a yes, and he was right.
golden_boots | The Gift of the Boots | The second gift was golden boots, which she wore once, to be polite, and fell in the stream. He fished her out. He did not know what water was, really; it doesn't happen much in the Nether. He was terrified of it. He jumped in anyway. She remembers that most.
shears | The Gift Returned | She taught him to shear sheep. He was very bad at it. The sheep didn't like him, then got used to him, then followed him about. She gave him a wool coat she'd made from their wool, and he wore it in the summer heat and refused to take it off. He said, in his way, it was warmer than gold.
golden_helmet | The Gift of the Crown | He went back through the portal once, and came back with a golden crown from a bastion, very battered, and put it on her head, and knelt. She said yes. They live in the meadow. Their children have curly tails and sheep's patience. The portal is overgrown with roses. Nobody uses it now.

@ coppermine | The Thing in the Copper Mine
> The copper mine at Tinsley was the richest in the county until the night the miners heard something knocking back. Not echoes: answers. Three knocks for every three. They broke through into a cavern nobody had dug, full of green light, and something in it was wearing copper too. The mine has been sealed for fifty years.
copper_chestplate | The Foreman's Plate | The foreman led the men into the green cavern with his lamp. The light down there wasn't from the lamp. It came off the walls, the floor, the thing at the far end that sat very still and wore copper gone green right through, and turned its head when the foreman knocked three times.
copper_leggings | The Second Man's Greaves | The second man in was the foreman's son. He saw the thing stand up. He said, afterwards, that it was as tall as two men and that its copper had grown into it, like bark on a tree, and that it had no face but a knocking. He ran. He did not stop running until he was in the next county.
copper_boots | The Boots Left Behind | The rest of the men ran too. The foreman didn't. They found his boots at the entrance to the green cavern the next morning, green already, as if they'd been there fifty years. They sealed the mine that week. They didn't go in to look. Nobody wanted to look.
copper_sword | The Sword Through the Wall | Fifty years later, the old mine was opened by a company from the city, who laughed at the stories. On the first day, a copper sword came through the wall of the main shaft from the other side, point first, and stopped. Then three knocks. The company sealed it again that afternoon.
iron_pickaxe | The Last Miner | The foreman's son, an old man now, sat outside the sealed mine all night with his father's pick on his knees, and knocked three times. He says three knocks came back. He says one of them was softer, the way his father knocked on his door when he was small. He goes back every year.

@ iceboat | The Ice Boat Races of Frostmere
> When the lake at Frostmere freezes in midwinter, the whole town builds boats with runners and sails and races them across the ice at speeds no boat was ever meant to reach. People are thrown out at every corner. The town doctor sets up a tent at the finish. It is the happiest day of the year. It is also the most bruised.
iron_boots | The Pusher's Boots | Every boat has a pusher, who runs behind it on the ice to get it going and jumps in at the last moment. Iron boots with spikes on the soles, for grip. The best pusher in Frostmere is a grandmother of seventy who has never missed the jump. She says the secret is not thinking about it.
leather_helmet | The Sailor's Cap | A sailor in a fur-lined cap steers the boat by its little sail and prays. The wind on the frozen lake is wild and sudden. Boats spin. Boats tip. Once a boat took off entirely, flew for a moment, landed on the far shore and kept going for a mile into the woods. They gave it the prize anyway.
trident | The Steering Fork | Some crews steer with a trident jammed into the ice, which works well until it doesn't, and then the boat spins round it like a top and throws everyone out. The crowd loves it. The crews say it's on purpose. It is never on purpose. The doctor's tent has a special queue for trident crews.
shield | The Brake | Nobody has ever built a boat with a working brake. The traditional way to stop at the finish is to throw a shield down on the ice in front and hope. It mostly works. Sometimes it doesn't, and the boat crosses the finish, the town square, the bakery and half a garden. The baker charges a fee now.

@ noteblocks | The Note Block Orchestra of Belltown
> A retired music teacher in Belltown decided the village needed an orchestra, and since nobody in the village could play an instrument, she built one out of note blocks: a hundred of them, tuned by hand, played by pressure plates in a great hall, with the whole village jumping on the plates in time. It was chaos. It was music. It was both.
iron_pickaxe | The Tuning Pick | Each note block's sound depends on what it stands on: stone for the drum, sand for the snare, wood for the bass, gold for the bells. The teacher dug the floor of the hall with this, a different stone under every block, for a year. She tuned them by tapping them and frowning. She frowned a lot.
golden_shovel | The Bell Floor | Under the high notes, she laid gold, which rings like bells, and it took every bit of gold in Belltown, which the village gave her gladly, mostly to see if she'd actually manage it. She did. When the bells rang the first time, the old blacksmith wept. He'd given his wedding ring.
leather_boots | The Jumping Boots | The orchestra is played by jumping: each villager stands on a plate, and jumps when the teacher points at them with a fishing rod. The village jumped so hard at the first concert that the hall floor cracked. They fixed it. They jumped harder. The teacher says that's what music is for.
fishing_rod | The Conductor's Rod | She conducts with a fishing rod, because, she says, a baton doesn't reach the back row. She points it at whoever's next. The baker, who is always slightly late, has been hooked by the collar twice. The concerts are held once a month. People come from three villages.

@ blackcopper | Captain Blackcopper's Last Mutiny
> Captain Blackcopper was the most feared pirate on the southern coast, not because she was cruel but because she was unpredictable: she once sank a warship and then sent the admiral flowers. Her crew mutinied four times. She won every mutiny. This is the story of the fifth, which she lost on purpose, and why.
copper_sword | The Captain's Cutlass | Copper, gone black at the edges from salt and smoke, which is how she got her name. She fought four mutinies with it and won all four by talking, mostly. She'd draw the cutlass, lay it on the table and say: "right. What's the problem?" Usually, the problem was the food.
chainmail_chestplate | The First Mate's Mail | Her first mate wore a chainmail shirt under his coat at all times, because he'd been on her ship long enough to know that anything could happen. He led the fifth mutiny. He was very nervous about it. She'd known about it for a month. She'd been hoping he would.
crossbow | The Mutineers' Crossbows | The mutineers pointed their crossbows at her and said she was relieved of command. She said: "good. I'm tired." She'd wanted to retire for years, she said, and the only way a pirate captain can retire is to be mutinied against. She shook the first mate's hand. He nearly fainted.
iron_boots | Ashore | They put her ashore, as is the custom, on a little island with a barrel of water and a single biscuit. The island was hers. She'd bought it three years before. There was a house on it, and a garden, and a library. She waved them off from the beach. The first mate cried.
fishing_rod | The Visits | The crew visits every summer, the first mate captain now, and anchors off her island, and she rows out with a basket of fruit and tells them what they're doing wrong. They do everything wrong. They love it. She fishes off the jetty and watches them sail away and laughs and laughs.

@ twosisters | The Sister in the Fire
> Two sisters were playing by an old ruined portal when it lit, and the elder fell through, and the portal closed behind her. The younger grew up on one side of it, the elder on the other. They wrote to each other for forty years, by throwing notes into the purple light whenever it flickered, and never once knew if the other one read them.
golden_helmet@nether | The Elder's Crown | The elder learned to wear gold, so the piglins would leave her alone, and then learned their ways, and then their language, and then, some years later, she was something like their queen. She wrote to her sister about it. "You won't believe this," she wrote, "but they listen to me."
leather_boots | The Younger's Boots | The younger stood by the ruined portal every evening in these, waiting for it to flicker. When it did, she threw in a letter, wrapped round a stone. "The farm is doing well. Mother asks for you. I married the miller. You'd hate him. I love him." She never knew if the letters arrived.
crossbow@nether | The Elder's Crossbow | The elder's letters came through as ashes and cinders, mostly, the heat burning them. Once in a while one survived. The younger kept every one, even the burnt. The last legible one said: "I am well. I am loved. I am sorry I fell. I would fall again, I think. Don't tell Mother that."
flint_and_steel | The Lighting | When the younger was old, she found an old book that said how to light a portal from this side. She took her husband's flint and steel and stood by the ruin and struck it. The purple light came up. She stepped through, old and slow, with a basket of apples. She has not come back.
shears | The Last Letter | A letter came through the portal a year later, unburnt, wrapped in a fine golden cloth. It said, in two hands, one old, one older: "We are together. The apples went down well. Don't wait for us. Keep the farm. The miller can visit." The family keeps it framed by the fire.
