@ nightmarket | The Night Market of Lanternfall
> Once a year, on the longest night, the town of Lanternfall holds a market that opens at sunset and closes at dawn. Everything is lit by lanterns. Everything is a little strange. Merchants come who aren't seen the rest of the year, and sell things that can't be bought anywhere else. One girl went every year of her life.
leather_helmet | The Lantern Hat | The first year, she was four, and her father bought her a hat with a tiny lantern on the brim from a merchant with no face. It lit itself at sunset and went out at dawn. She wore it every year after. It never needed oil. Her father never could say what the merchant looked like.
fishing_rod | The Fish That Sang | At ten, she won a fish at a stall where you fished in a barrel of stars. It sang, quietly, in a language nobody spoke. She kept it in a bowl by her bed. It sang her to sleep for eleven years. When it died she buried it by the river, and the river sang for a week.
shears | The Thread Seller | At sixteen, she bought a reel of silver thread from an old woman who said it would mend anything. She mended her father's coat, her mother's heart after a quarrel, and a friendship with her best friend that had torn. She never ran out of thread. She's still sewing with it.
leather_boots | The Shoe Merchant | At twenty, a merchant sold her boots that, he said, would always take her home. She thought he was joking. She moved to the city. The boots kept walking her back to Lanternfall, every weekend, whether she meant to go or not. She stopped fighting it. She lives there now.
iron_sword | The Last Stall | At eighty, she went one last time, and found the stall with no faces, and asked to buy something. The merchant shook their head and gave her a sword, very old, very plain, and said: "this was your father's. He traded it for your hat." She hadn't known. She wept in the lantern light.

@ brotherknight | My Brother Thinks He's a Knight
> My little brother Pip is six and he thinks he's a knight. He has a knighting certificate he made himself. He has a quest every day. Mum says to play along. I'm nine so I'm his squire, which means I do everything and he gets the credit. This is our quest log. I wrote it. He drew the pictures.
leather_helmet | Quest One: The Hat | Pip's helmet is a cooking pot with a handle that he wears backwards so the handle is a plume. He wore it to school. The teacher said he couldn't wear a pot in class. He said he wasn't wearing a pot, he was wearing a helmet. He had to stand in the corner. He stood very nobly.
wooden_axe | Quest Two: The Dragon | The dragon was the neighbour's cat. Pip went over the fence with his wooden axe and said "yield, foul beast!" The cat did not yield. The cat sat on Pip. Pip lay in the grass for ten minutes with a cat on him. He said he was "letting it think it won". I got the cat off.
shield | Quest Three: The Princess | The princess was our grandma. Pip rescued her from the tower, which was the upstairs bathroom, by knocking very loudly and shouting until she came out. She was very cross and also laughing. She knighted him again with a hairbrush. He says that makes him a double knight.
fishing_rod | Quest Four: The Kraken | The kraken was in the pond in the park. Pip fished for it for a whole afternoon with my fishing rod. He caught a boot. He said it was the kraken's boot and the kraken must have been very big. He wanted to keep fishing for the rest of the kraken. Mum said it was teatime.
leather_boots | Quest Five: Me | Last week I was sad because of something at school and I didn't want to talk about it. Pip came into my room in his pot and stood by my bed and said "I will guard you." He stood there till I fell asleep. I don't know how long. Mum said a long time. He's a good knight, actually.

@ sailorswife | The Sailor's Wife
> I married a sailor at nineteen, and for thirty years I saw him four months in every twelve. People asked me how I could stand it. I didn't stand it. I made a life, and when he came home, I made room in it, and when he left again, I closed it back up. These are the things that stayed when he went.
leather_chestplate | His Spare Coat | He left his spare coat on the hook every time he went, so the house would look as if he were in it. I'd wear it in the evenings in winter. The neighbours thought it was sweet. It was practical. It was the warmest coat in the house. It was also sweet. Both things can be true.
fishing_rod | The Rod in the Corner | He never fished. He was on the sea every day; he said fishing would be like a baker going home and baking. The rod was mine. I fished off the harbour wall every evening while he was away, looking at the horizon. I caught a great many fish. I never once saw his ship come in early.
shears | The Garden | I kept a garden. Every time he came home it was bigger, and he'd walk round it with me and admire everything. One year I'd planted a rose for every month he'd been away. Eight roses, by the end of our first year. Hundreds, by the end. The garden is all roses now.
leather_boots | The Waiting | I walked to the harbour in these every day of the month he was due, to watch for the sail. Thirty years. I knew every ship on the coast by the shape of its sail. I always knew his first. He said he always saw me first, on the wall. We had a competition. I always won.
iron_helmet | The Last Voyage | He came home for good at sixty, with a bad leg and his sea chest and a helmet he'd been given for long service. He sat in the garden among the roses and said: "it's very quiet." I said: "you'll get used to it." He didn't. Neither did I. We're both learning. It's nice.

@ werewolffennick | The Wolf of Fennick Wood
> When a wolf began killing sheep in Fennick Wood, larger than any wolf, the village hired a monster hunter. He found the wolf. He also found that it was the miller's son, cursed, who woke every morning with blood on his hands and no memory of the night. The hunter had a choice. He made a different one.
iron_sword | The Hunter's Sword | The steel sword, for men. He drew it, at the edge of the wood, when the boy came stumbling out in the dawn, naked and bloody and weeping. The boy saw the sword and knelt and said, "do it. Please." The hunter put the sword back in its sheath. He said, "not yet."
golden_sword | The Silver Sword | The gilded sword, for monsters; silver was dear. He held it at the wolf's throat the next night, under the full moon, and didn't strike. The wolf stopped, and looked at him, and for a moment, the hunter swore, there was the boy in its eyes. He lowered the sword. The wolf went back into the wood.
shears | The Curse-Cutting | Curses can be broken. It takes the one who cast it, or the one it was cast for, or love. The hunter found the witch who'd cursed the boy's grandfather, over a debt, eighty years ago. She was very old. She cut a lock of the boy's hair with these, and burned it, and said: "it's done."
leather_boots | The Last Night | On the next full moon, the hunter sat with the boy in the mill and they waited, all night, the hunter with his sword on his knee. The boy shook. The moon rose. Nothing happened. In the morning the boy laughed and cried and couldn't stop. The hunter ate breakfast.
leather_chestplate | The Fee | The miller paid him in flour and a coat. The hunter took both. He said the coat would do, the winter was coming. When he left the village, the boy walked him to the edge of Fennick Wood, and stood there, and watched him go, and then went home to the mill. He's the miller now.

@ watchdogs | The Stone Watchdogs of the Catacombs
> Under the old land there are catacombs where the honoured dead were laid to wait for rebirth, and at their doors stand watchdogs of stone, with swords in their jaws, who guard the dead from the living. They are very old. They are very loyal. When the rebirth never came, they kept guarding anyway. They have no other purpose.
iron_sword | The Sword in the Jaws | Each watchdog holds a great sword in its mouth, and swings it with its whole head, slowly, terribly. They do not tire. They do not stop. A grave-robber who got past one said it watched him the whole time, out of the corner of its stone eye, as if disappointed.
iron_helmet | The Stone Helm | Their heads are carved like hounds wearing helms, and between their ears there is a small brazier, which still burns, after all this time. Nobody lights it. Scholars say the dead light it. Scholars say a great many things. The flame is green. It smells of ash and old flowers.
leather_boots | The Pilgrim's Boots | A pilgrim came to the catacombs, not to rob them, but to visit the grave of her mother. The watchdog let her pass. It had never let anyone pass. The scholars still argue about it. The pilgrim says she just asked. She says, after all, they're dogs. Dogs know who loves the dead.
shield | The Broken One | One watchdog lies broken at the bottom of a pit, still holding its sword, its flame still burning. Its head is turned towards the door, as if it was guarding even as it fell. Travellers leave bones beside it. Nobody knows who started it. It seems right.
stone_sword | The Pup | In the deepest catacomb, beside a very small grave, there's a very small watchdog, the size of a cat, with a stone dagger in its jaws, guarding a child. Its flame is the brightest of all. Nobody goes near it. Not because it's dangerous. Because it's the saddest thing in the land.

@ dreamisland | The Island That Was a Dream
> A boy washed up on an island after a storm, and was nursed back to health by a girl who sang, and spent a year exploring the island's caves and towers, gathering eight instruments to wake a great whale that slept in an egg on the mountain. Everyone on the island begged him not to. He did it anyway. He had to go home.
leather_helmet | The Green Cap | He washed up in his green cap and a tunic, with nothing else. The girl who found him on the beach said, "you're awake! You were mumbling." Her father had a mushroom on his head. A dog on a chain kept trying to eat him. He decided, quite early, not to ask questions.
iron_sword | The Sword on the Beach | His sword had washed up a little way along the shore, half buried, as if the sea had decided to give it back. He fought his way through the island's dungeons with it. Every monster he killed said nothing, as if they were very sad. He didn't think about that until the end.
shield | The Shield From the Shop | He bought this shield in the village shop. The shopkeeper said "and if you steal anything, I'll kill you." He stole a bow. The shopkeeper killed him. He woke up on the beach. Everyone called him "thief" for the rest of his stay. He said it was worth it.
fishing_rod | The Fishing Pond | There was a fishing pond by the village, run by an old man, and the boy spent far too long there, catching the same fish over and over, because it was the only quiet place on the island. The girl found him there. She sang to him. He doesn't remember the song any more. He tries.
leather_boots | The Waking | He played all eight instruments on the mountain, and the whale woke, and the island faded around him, the village, the dog, the girl last of all, singing. He woke on a piece of driftwood at sea. Above him, a whale passed over, in the clouds. He didn't know if he'd dreamed it. He does.

@ groansalot | A Quiet Tuesday for Sir Bertil
> Songs are written about the great days of knights: the battles, the dragons, the rescues. Nobody writes about the other days. Sir Bertil of Low Mardle kept a diary of one of them, an ordinary Tuesday in the thirtieth year of his service, and his widow gave it to the chronicle. It is the most honest account of knighthood ever set down.
iron_sword | Morning | "Rose at six. Polished sword. Nothing to use it on. Polished it again. The squire asked if there was any news of the dragon. There is no dragon. There has never been a dragon in Low Mardle. He asks every morning. I admire his hope. I told him to muck out the horses."
shield | Mid-Morning | "Rode the bounds of the village to look for threats. Found: a loose sheep, a gate left open, a hole in the road. Closed the gate. Returned the sheep, which kicked me. Reported the hole to the reeve, who said he knew. The shield took the kick. It was a good kick. The shield has seen worse."
iron_helmet | Noon | "Ate bread and cheese on the church wall in full armour, which the children find hilarious. A small girl asked if I'd ever killed anybody. I said no. She looked very disappointed. Her brother asked if I'd ever been kissed by a princess. I said no. He looked relieved."
iron_boots | Afternoon | "Settled a dispute over a pig between two farmers by declaring the pig belonged to the pig. Both farmers agreed this was fair and went home confused. The pig followed me. Walked it back twice. It is now in the castle yard. I think it is mine now. My boots are full of mud."
fishing_rod | Evening | "Went fishing at the mill pond. Caught nothing. Sat until dark. Thought about the war I was in when I was young, and how loud it was, and how quiet it is here. Thanked God for the quiet. Went home. The squire asked if there was any news of the dragon. Tomorrow, I told him. Maybe tomorrow."

@ icecaravan | The Ice Road Convoys
> Every winter, when the great lake of the north freezes, the ice road opens: a track straight across the frozen lake, sixty miles, from the mining towns to the coast. The convoys of sledges cross it in the dark months. The chronicle of the road lists every convoy that set out. A few of the entries say only "did not arrive."
leather_boots | The First Crossing | The first convoy crossed in the year of the great freeze, led by a trapper who'd walked the ice alone the winter before to prove it would bear a sledge. The chronicle records that he walked ahead of the lead sledge the entire sixty miles, tapping the ice with a stick, whistling.
iron_axe | The Ice Axe | Every convoy carries axes, to cut holes to test the ice, and to cut free a sledge that's frozen in. One driver, in a whiteout, used hers to cut the traces of her own sledge when it went through the ice, and saved the dogs. She lost the cargo. The chronicle says she lost nothing worth having.
leather_chestplate | The Driver's Furs | Drivers wear three coats, and still freeze. A driver named Ogden crossed forty times, more than anyone, and was asked his secret. He said: "it's not the furs. It's knowing that the cold will win if you let it think about you. Don't let it think about you." Nobody knew what he meant.
fishing_rod | The Midway Camp | At the midpoint of the road there's a camp of huts on the ice, where convoys stop, and the camp keeper fishes through a hole for whoever arrives. The fish of the great lake are huge and slow and very cold. The chronicle says a hot fish stew at the midway has saved more lives than any doctor.
shield | The Last Entry | The road closes when the thaw comes. The last convoy of every year races the thaw. The chronicle's last entry, every year, is the same: the date, and "road closed", and below it, in a different hand, the keeper's: "everyone home". When it doesn't say that, it says who isn't.

@ barberbleakmoor | The Barber of Bleakmoor
> In the town of Bleakmoor, the barber was also the surgeon, the dentist and, when the vet was drunk, the vet. He had one chair, one set of tools and one rule: if you sat in the chair, you got whatever he decided you needed. People went in for a shave and came out without a tooth. Nobody ever asked for a second opinion.
shears | The Shears of Everything | He cut hair with them. He cut bandages with them. He cut stitches out with them. He trimmed a horse's hoof with them, once, while the vet slept it off on the floor. He cleaned them between customers with a quick wipe on his apron, if he remembered. He usually didn't.
iron_axe | The Hard Cases | For the hard cases. Bones, mostly. He swore he only used it on bones that were already broken, to make them break better. Several patients swore otherwise. They swore it through clenched teeth. Some came out with fewer teeth. He did good work, though. Most of them walked again.
leather_chestplate | The Apron | Red-and-white striped, like every barber's pole, though in his case the red was not paint. He wore it to church on Sundays, not knowing why people moved away from him. He said it was his good apron. It was. The other one was worse. Much worse.
flint_and_steel | The Hot Towel | He offered every customer a hot towel at the end, heated on the fire, which was the only gentle thing he did. The towel was so good, people say, that it was worth everything that came before. Old men in Bleakmoor still close their eyes and smile when they remember the towel.
iron_sword | The Last Customer | His last customer was a highwayman with a price on his head, who came in for a shave and drew a sword when the barber recognised him. The barber gave him a perfect shave anyway, a towel and a bill. He paid and got as far as the door. Then the sheriff arrived. The barber had sent for him.

@ golemchess | The Old Man and the Golem Who Played Chess
> An old man in a village by the woods had no one left to play chess with. His friends had died, his children had moved away, and the village iron golem stood all day in the square doing nothing. So one morning he put a chessboard on a barrel in front of the golem and moved a pawn. The golem looked at it for a long time.
iron_chestplate | The Golem | It took the golem three days to make its first move. It moved its pawn, very carefully, with one enormous iron finger, without knocking any of the other pieces over. The old man cheered so loud the whole village came out. The golem looked embarrassed. Golems can look embarrassed.
golden_helmet | The King Piece | The golem lost every game for a year. Then it won one. The old man sat back and looked at the board and said, "well I never." The golem picked up the old man's king and handed it to him, very gently, as if apologising. The old man laughed until he cried. They played every day.
leather_boots | The Walk to the Square | Every morning the old man walked to the square in his slippers, slower every year, and sat on his stool by the barrel, and the golem was always there, waiting, the pieces already set up. He never asked who set them up. The village children say the golem does it at dawn.
fishing_rod | The Rainy Days | When it rained, the old man couldn't come out, so the golem brought the barrel and the board to his window and they played through the glass, the old man telling it his moves, the golem making them. The golem got very wet. It rusted a bit. It didn't seem to mind.
shield | The Last Game | When the old man died, the golem stood in the square by the barrel for a week, not moving, the board set up. Then the old man's grandson came, and sat on the stool, and moved a pawn. The golem looked at him for a long time. Then it moved. They play every day. The grandson always loses.

@ ladybirdknight | The Smallest Knight
> The smallest knight in the kingdom was a girl called Wren, who stood no taller than a sword, and was made a knight by accident, when the king, who was short-sighted, tapped the wrong head. Nobody had the courage to tell him. So Wren became a knight. She took it very seriously. She was, in the end, the best one.
iron_sword | The Sword She Couldn't Lift | Her sword was taller than she was. She dragged it everywhere on a little cart. When she had to fight, she braced the hilt against the ground and let the enemy run onto the point. It worked surprisingly often. The other knights started copying her. She didn't mind.
leather_helmet | The Helm That Fit | No knightly helmet in the armoury fitted her. The blacksmith's apprentice made her one from a leather bucket. It had a little plume. She wore it to the king's feast and the king, who still couldn't see well, complimented her on her "fine bearing". She bowed so low she fell over.
shield | The Shield She Hid In | Her shield was big enough to hide behind completely, which, at the battle of Dun Ford, she did, while thirty arrows struck it. Then she came out from behind it and ran under the enemy's horses, which were so confused they threw their riders. She won the battle. Nobody knows how.
chainmail_boots | The Long March | The army marched three hundred miles that summer, and Wren marched all of it, on legs half the length of anyone else's, taking two steps for every one. She never asked for a cart. The soldiers started carrying her on their shoulders. She said it was undignified. She let them.
golden_helmet | The Truth | When the king finally got spectacles and saw her clearly, at her twentieth birthday feast, he laughed until he wept, and then he stood up and said: "it was the best mistake I ever made." He knighted her again, properly, on purpose. She grew another inch that year. She says that's why.
