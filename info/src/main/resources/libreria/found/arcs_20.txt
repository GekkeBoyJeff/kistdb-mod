@ firstapple | The Apple of the First Maker
> It is written in the oldest records that when the world was young, its maker set into it a handful of golden apples that glowed with a light of their own, to be found by those who dug deepest. Whoever ate one could not be killed that day. The records list everyone who ever found one. The list is short. The stories are long.
golden_helmet | The Crown of the First Finder | The first apple was found by a miner in the reign of nobody, before kings, and eaten at once, because she was hungry. She walked into a lava lake that afternoon to see what would happen. Nothing happened. She came out laughing. She lived to be a hundred and four.
golden_sword | The Sword of the Second | The second apple was found by a soldier who did not eat it, but carried it into battle in his pack, believing it would protect him. It did not. Apples have to be eaten. The apple was found in his pack by the soldier who killed him, who ate it, and lived for ever. Allegedly.
golden_chestplate | The Plate of the Third | The third was bought at enormous price by a king, who kept it under glass, to eat on the day he was in mortal danger. He died in his sleep, of old age, without ever being in mortal danger. His heirs fought over the apple for forty years. It rotted.
golden_boots | The Boots of the Fourth | The fourth was found by a child, who gave it to her grandmother, who was dying. The grandmother ate it. She did not die that day. She did not die the next day. She died the day after, at peace, having said everything she needed to say. The child calls it the best apple.
golden_axe | The Fifth, Not Yet Found | The records say there are more, in the deep places, under the old temples and in the forgotten chests. The fifth line of the list is blank. The scribes say that whoever finds the next apple should write their own name there, in their own hand, and decide for themselves what it's for.

@ walkerwest | The Man Who Walked to the Edge
> They say that if you walk far enough in any direction, the world stops making sense: the land goes strange, the mountains stack up in walls, and the ground itself comes apart into stripes. One man set out to see it with nothing but a dog and a compass. He walked for years. People sent him money to keep going, just so they'd know.
leather_boots | The Walking Boots | He wore out forty-one pairs of boots on the way. He kept count. He kept every pair, too, in a chest in a little hut he'd built every few thousand steps, so he could look back and see how far he'd come. These were the forty-first. He was still going.
iron_sword | The Sword He Rarely Drew | The road west was long and lonely and dangerous, but he avoided fights whenever he could, because a fight would slow him down, and he wanted to see the edge. When he had to fight, he fought. The dog did most of it. The dog was very good.
leather_helmet | The Dog's Hat | His dog walked every step beside him, for years, and when the dog got old, he walked slower, so the dog could keep up. The dog wore a little hat in the rain. He'd made it himself. It didn't fit. The dog didn't mind. The dog was very old by the end.
fishing_rod | The Rod for Rest Days | Every seventh day he stopped and fished, and talked to the people who'd sent him money, wherever they were, by letter. They sent him so much he gave most of it away, to charities, to people who needed it more. He said he only needed boots, bread, and the dog.
shield | The Last Marker | He hasn't reached the edge yet. Nobody knows if he ever will. Every so often a traveller far in the west finds a little hut, with a chest, with old boots in it, and a sign: "still walking. dog says hi." People leave things in the chest for him. He always takes them.

@ nautilusstable | The Nautilus Stable of Kelp Point
> Off the coast of Kelp Point there's a stable under the sea. It keeps nautiluses: great spiral-shelled creatures that the people of the point ride through the deep, holding their breath and laughing. Nobody else does this. Nobody else thinks it's a good idea. The people of Kelp Point have never once asked anyone else's opinion.
leather_helmet | The Breath Helm | A leather cap with a turtle shell sewn on top, so you can stay down longer. The stablemaster's grandmother made the first one. She rode under the sea for sixty years and drowned in her bath at ninety-one. The family considers this a great joke. She would have too.
trident | The Herding Fork | The nautiluses are herded with tridents, gently, by a tap on the shell. They spin when they're happy and pull in their tentacles when they're cross. The stablemaster's son once tapped the wrong one and it pulled him a mile out to sea and dropped him. He walked back along the seabed.
leather_chestplate | The Riding Coat | Waterproofed with whale oil and sealant, and still wet, always, from the moment you put it on to the day you die. The riders of Kelp Point smell of the sea at all times, even in their best clothes. At weddings, the guests can't tell the bride from the fish.
fishing_rod | The Rod for Feeding | Nautiluses eat fish, which the riders catch with these rods and then hold out, upside down, underwater, for the nautilus to take. The nautilus takes the fish. Sometimes it takes the rod. Once, the stablemaster's niece. She was fine. They found her the next day, at the bottom, waving.
copper_spear | The Racing Lance | Every summer they race the nautiluses round the reef with copper lances, one rider on each. The winner gets to name the next foal. The last three winners named them Seaweed, More Seaweed and Gerald. Nobody knows why Gerald. The winner refuses to say.

@ hoglinranch | The Hoglin Rancher of the Crimson Wood
> In the crimson forest of the burning world, where the trees are red and the air smells of iron, a woman from the overworld keeps a ranch of hoglins: huge, tusked, furious pig-beasts that hate everything that moves. She loves them. They tolerate her. Her neighbours, the piglins, think she is the bravest and stupidest creature in the nether.
golden_helmet | The Golden Cap | Piglins leave you alone if you wear gold, so she always wears this helmet when she goes out to the fence. The hoglins don't care about gold. They charge her every morning. She jumps aside, every morning. She's been doing it for nine years. She says it keeps her young.
iron_shovel | The Fungus Spade | Hoglins are scared of the blue warped fungus, so she plants a ring of it round the ranch with this, and the hoglins stay inside the ring. Mostly. The biggest one, a scarred boar called Nan, has learned to jump over it. Nan comes to the house at night. Nan wants the cabbages.
leather_boots | The Hoof-Proof Boots | Reinforced at the toe, which has saved her toes from a hoglin hoof forty-one times. She has lost two toes regardless. She says the two toes were in the wrong place at the wrong time, and she bears the hoglins no grudge. She bears Nan a small grudge.
crossbow | The Defence | Once, a band of piglin brutes came to raid the ranch for meat. She stood at the gate with this and didn't fire a shot. She just opened the gate. The hoglins did the rest. The brutes have never come back. The piglins of the crimson wood bring her gold now, every month.
iron_axe | The Nether Steak | She says hoglin steak is the finest meat in either world. She sells it at the portal, to overworld travellers, at a terrible price. She keeps the best cuts for Nan, who has never once said thank you and once bit the fence in half. She loves Nan most of all.

@ shulkerwindow | The Shulker Who Wanted to See Outside
> Shulkers live in the cities at the edge of the void, in purple boxes, peeking out of their lids only to shoot at strangers. They never leave. They've never seen anything but the city. One young shulker, peeking out at a traveller, didn't shoot. It asked, in its way, what was out there. The traveller took it home.
shield | The Shield Against Its Shots | The traveller carried this to the city, because shulker bullets make you float, and floating, at the edge of the void, is how you die. The young shulker shot at the shield three times out of habit. Then it stopped, and opened its lid, and looked at him. He put the shield down.
diamond_chestplate@end_city | The City Plate | The traveller found the shulker in the highest tower, next to a ship with a dragon's head on the front, guarding this armour as its parents had, and their parents. It had never seen anyone take it. He didn't take it. He asked if it wanted to come with him. It blinked.
leather_boots | The Way Home | He carried it home in his arms, box and all, through the portal, back into the world of green and rain. It peeked out of its lid at the trees, the sky, the cows. It shook so hard he thought it was dying. It wasn't. It was the shulker equivalent of shouting with joy.
shears | The Garden | He put the shulker in his garden, and it sat on a hill, and looked out at the valley, and changed colour, slowly, over a week, from purple to green. Nobody knew shulkers could change colour. Nobody had ever taken one out of the city to see.
fishing_rod | Still Looking | It's old now, for a shulker, and it sits on the same hill, green as the grass, peeking out every morning to look at the valley. It's never shot at anyone since. When strangers come up the hill, it opens its lid and looks at them, as if to ask what's out there, where they come from.

@ ocelotexpedition | The Expedition of Lord Pemberton
> In the year of the great expeditions, the Royal Society sent Lord Pemberton into the jungle with forty porters, a hundred cases of tea and orders to bring back a living ocelot for the royal menagerie. He was the most well-equipped explorer in history and the least prepared. He brought back an ocelot. The ocelot brought back him.
leather_helmet | The Pith Helmet | Lord Pemberton wore it everywhere, including in the river, which he fell into on the second day, and in bed, where he insisted on sleeping upright in full kit "in case of tigers". There are no tigers in the jungle. He saw several anyway, every night, at the edge of the firelight.
iron_axe | The Machete | He was not good with it. He cut more vines from his own hat than from the jungle. The porters did the cutting. Lord Pemberton followed behind them making notes on the flora, most of which were wrong, all of which were later published, to great acclaim.
fishing_rod | The Fish Strategy | A porter told him ocelots only trust you if you offer them raw fish, very slowly, without moving. He sat in a clearing with a fish for eleven days. On the twelfth, an ocelot came, took the fish, and sat on his lap. He did not move for six hours. He called it the best day of his life.
leather_chestplate | The Torn Jacket | On the way home, the porters deserted, the tea ran out, and Lord Pemberton got lost in the jungle for a month. The ocelot led him out. It walked ahead of him through the vines, looking back every few steps, until they reached the coast. It then refused to get on the boat.
leather_boots | The Report | He wrote in his report to the Society that the expedition had failed to bring back an ocelot. Then he resigned, and sailed back to the jungle, and built a house at the edge of it. He lives there now, in his pith helmet. The ocelot visits on Tuesdays. He saves it a fish.

@ ashcrowned | The Knight of the Ash-Crowned King
> When the ash-crowned king fell, his knights were bound by oath to guard his tomb until he rose again. Most broke their oaths and fled. One did not. He stands there still, in the dark, in armour gone grey with ash, and he asks every visitor the same question: is the king risen yet? Nobody has the heart to tell him.
iron_helmet | The Helm of the Unbroken Oath | His face has not been seen in four hundred years. Beneath the helm there is only ash and loyalty and a voice that sounds very tired. He speaks courteously. He bows. He will not let you pass to the king's chamber. You may try. Many have tried.
iron_chestplate | The Ash-Grey Plate | Once it was white, painted with the king's crown in gold. Now it is grey all the way through, a coat of ash a finger thick from four hundred years of standing in the same doorway. When he moves, a little of it falls. He has not moved much. There is a lot of it left.
iron_sword | The Sword of the Last Watch | His sword is very sharp still, because he sharpens it every day, by the light of a candle that never goes out. It is the only thing he does besides guard. When asked why, he says the king will need a sharp sword at his side when he rises. He says it in a whisper.
shield | The King's Seal | His shield bears the king's seal: a crown on fire. It is cracked down the middle. He has not mended it. He says the crack happened the day the king fell, by itself, and that it will mend itself the day the king rises. He checks it every morning. It has not mended.
iron_boots | The Doorway | A hero came at last who did not fight him, but sat down in front of him and told him, very gently, that the king was not going to rise. He was quiet for a long time. Then he said, "I know." Then he lay down his sword, and the ash fell away, and there was nobody in the armour at all.

@ vellmoor | The Siege of Vellmoor
> In the spring of the year of the long frost, the free city of Vellmoor was besieged for one hundred and twelve days by the army of the margrave. The city did not fall. The records of the siege survive in the city archive, kept by a scribe named Tomas Ilver, who recorded everything, including, it emerges, his opinions.
stone_sword | The Militia's Blades | When the siege began, the city had two hundred trained soldiers and a militia of four thousand bakers, tanners and clerks armed with whatever could be found. Ilver records that the militia's swords were "of poor quality and worse temperament". He was in the militia. His sword broke on day one.
crossbow | The Wall Crossbows | The walls were held by crossbowmen, mostly women, mostly from the weavers' guild, who had been practising at targets in the guild hall for years "for sport". Ilver notes that the margrave's captains had never seen such accuracy and assumed the city had hired mercenaries. It had not.
iron_shovel | The Counter-Mine | The margrave's sappers tried to tunnel under the walls. The city's gravediggers, who knew the ground best, dug down to meet them. What happened underground, Ilver does not describe. He records only that the gravediggers came back up, and the sappers did not.
wooden_hoe | The Gardens | By the eighth week the city was starving. The abbess of Saint Merin ordered every courtyard, square and roof to be planted with turnips. Ilver complains bitterly about the turnips for eleven pages. He also records that, because of the turnips, nobody in the city died of hunger.
iron_helmet | The End of the Siege | On the hundred and twelfth day, the margrave's army withdrew, its men having grown tired, sick, and, Ilver suspects, ashamed. The city held a feast. Ilver records the menu in full. It was turnips. He ends the record: "never again. Not the siege. The turnips."

@ frontiernurse | Letters From the Frontier Nurse
> I came out to the frontier forts at twenty-three, against my father's wishes and my own better judgement, to nurse the soldiers of the border war. I wrote to my sister every week. She kept the letters. She gave them back to me when I came home. I want them kept together, with the things I carried.
shears | My Bandage Shears | Dear Lise, it's worse than I thought. There are four of us nurses for two hundred men. I cut so many bandages yesterday I couldn't open my hand after. The surgeon is a drunk. The cook is a drunk. The colonel is a drunk. The men are lovely. I'm staying. Don't tell Father.
leather_chestplate | The Apron I Wore | Dear Lise, I've stopped washing my apron. There's no point. It's always red by noon. A boy of seventeen died holding my hand today. He asked me to write to his mother. I did. I told her he was brave. He was. He was so frightened, Lise, and so brave. I can't stop shaking.
flint_and_steel | The Lamp | Dear Lise, I do the night rounds with a lamp. The men call me "the lamp lady". One of them, a sergeant with no left leg, has started waiting up for me so he can tell me jokes. They're terrible jokes. I laugh every time. He knows. I think that's the point of them, for both of us.
leather_boots | The Boots I Wore Through | Dear Lise, the war is over. I don't know what to do with myself. I've worn holes in these boots walking the wards. I'm coming home next month. Please don't make a fuss. Please tell Father I'm sorry, and that I'd do it again. Please tell him I was useful.
iron_sword | The Sergeant's Sword | Dear Lise, I'm not coming home next month. The sergeant with the terrible jokes asked me to marry him. I said yes. We're going to farm, here, on the frontier. He gave me his sword, because he says he doesn't need it now. Come and visit. Bring Father. He'll hate the jokes.

@ piratenan | My Grandma Is a Pirate
> My grandma says she used to be a pirate, and Mum says she wasn't, and Grandma says Mum doesn't know everything. Grandma has a parrot and a sword and a map with an X on it in the attic and she knows ALL the sea songs. I think she was a pirate. This is my proof.
leather_helmet | Exhibit One: The Hat | Grandma has a hat in the attic with a feather in it and it is EXACTLY a pirate hat. When I asked her about it she put it on and said "arr" in a really good voice, not like a pretend voice, like a voice she'd done a lot. Then she winked. Grandmas don't wink. Pirates wink.
iron_sword | Exhibit Two: The Sword | Grandma's sword is in the umbrella stand by the door, between the umbrellas. Mum says it's a souvenir. I asked Grandma where it was from and she said "from a man who doesn't need it any more" and then she laughed for ages. I don't know what was funny.
fishing_rod | Exhibit Three: Fishing | Grandma took me fishing and she knew every knot in the world and she tied them all really fast without looking, and she called the boat "she", and she said the sea was "in a mood today". Mum says that's normal fishing. I say Mum doesn't know everything.
leather_boots | Exhibit Four: The Map | In the attic, in Grandma's old boots, there was a map. It had an island and an X. I showed Grandma and she went very quiet and then she said "well, well. I wondered where that went." Then she said we're going on a trip in the summer. Mum doesn't know.
shield | Exhibit Five: The Proof | On Grandma's arm there's a tattoo of a ship, and under it it says a name, and the name is a ship that Mum says sank sixty years ago with all the pirates on it. I asked Grandma. She said "not ALL of them, sweetheart." That's my proof. Case closed.

@ surgeon | The Field Surgeon of Redmarch
> The surgeon of the army at Redmarch had a rule: no man under his knife would ever be told how bad it was. He had a second rule, which was that he would cut fast. He held the record in the army for the fastest amputation: forty-one seconds. In his memoir he says that number haunted him more than any battle.
iron_axe | The Bone Axe | Not a battle axe. A surgeon's axe, for when the saw wouldn't do. He used it once, he writes, at the worst moment of the worst day, on a man who survived and lived to eighty and sent him a letter every year thanking him for "the chop". He kept every letter.
shears | The Cutting Shears | For the cloth, the skin, the thread. He sharpened them every night by the light of the fire, and every night he said a prayer over them, which he never wrote down. His assistant, who heard it, would only ever say: "it was short, and it was about being quick."
leather_chestplate | The Leather Apron | Heavy, stiff, stained so dark that you couldn't tell it had ever been another colour. He wore it at every battle for nineteen years. He burned it, at the end of the war, in the camp fire, and stood and watched it until it was ash. Then he went home and slept for three days.
flint_and_steel | The Cautery | Hot iron to close the wound, because that was all there was. The smell, he writes, never left him. He became a vegetarian after the war. His wife said he could never again stand in a room where meat was cooking. He took up baking instead. He was very good at it.
iron_sword | The Last Patient | His last patient was the general, who'd been shot in the leg and refused to have it off. The surgeon held his hand and told him exactly how bad it was, the only time he broke his rule. The general let him cut. Forty seconds. A new record. He never told anyone.

@ lancelittle | Sir Lancelittle and the Dragon of Mild Inconvenience
> Every kingdom has a dragon. The kingdom of Puddlemere had a small one, about the size of a goat, who did not burn villages but did steal washing from lines, eat the vicar's petunias and set fire to the occasional hedge. Sir Lancelittle, knight of very little renown, was sent to deal with it. He was quite excited.
iron_sword | The Sword of Moderate Valour | Sir Lancelittle polished his sword for three days before setting out. He practised his speech in the mirror: "Foul beast, thy reign of terror ends!" His squire said it wasn't really a reign of terror. Sir Lancelittle said he knew, but it was his first dragon and he wanted it to be special.
leather_helmet | The Helm, Slightly Singed | When he found the dragon, it was asleep on a pile of stolen washing, snoring little puffs of smoke. He gave his speech. The dragon woke up, sneezed, and singed the top of his helmet. Then it went back to sleep. Sir Lancelittle was not sure what to do next.
shears | The Petunia Snips | He decided to reason with it. He brought it petunias from the vicar's garden, freshly cut, as a peace offering. The dragon ate them happily and then sat on his foot. The vicar was very cross. Sir Lancelittle said it was diplomacy. The vicar said it was theft.
fishing_rod | The Bargain | It turned out the dragon was hungry, because there were no fish in its pond. Sir Lancelittle stocked the pond. The dragon stopped stealing washing. The hedges stopped catching fire. The village was delighted, apart from the vicar, who was still cross about the petunias.
shield | The Dragon's Friend | Sir Lancelittle is now the Royal Dragon Liaison, a post created for him, and the dragon follows him about like a goat. It sits on his foot during council meetings. Visiting dignitaries are told that this is a fearsome beast tamed by the bravest knight in the land. He's never once corrected them.

@ fairybottle | The Fairy in the Bottle
> There's an old trick that every hero in a certain green land knows: catch a fairy in an empty bottle, and keep it corked, and when you fall in battle, it will burst out and bring you back. Nobody ever asks the fairy what it thinks of this. One fairy, after the seventh time, told the hero exactly what she thought.
leather_helmet | The Hero's Cap | Green and pointed, as all the heroes' caps are in that land. He was a good hero. He was brave and kind and he fell down a lot. He fell into pits, off cliffs, onto spikes. Every time, the fairy came out of the bottle and fixed him. Every time, he put her back in.
leather_chestplate | The Tunic | The first time she saved him, she was flattered. The second time, pleased. The third time, she noticed that he'd walked into exactly the same spiked pit as before. By the seventh time, she came out of the bottle, healed him, and then hit him very hard on the nose.
iron_sword | The Sword She Lectured | She gave him a lecture, sitting on the hilt of his sword, that lasted an hour. About pits. About spikes. About looking where you're going. About how many bottles a hero could carry. About how it felt to be kept in a bottle. He listened. He said sorry.
shield | The Shield He Raised | After that he was much more careful. He held his shield up. He looked where he was going. He didn't fall in pits. He asked her, every morning, if she'd like to come out of the bottle and fly about a bit. She said yes. She flew about. She always came back.
fishing_rod | The Open Bottle | He never put the cork back in again. She lives in the bottle on his shelf, with the cork beside it, and comes and goes as she likes. He's old now. He hasn't fallen down in years. She says she rather misses it. She says it's the only time he ever really listened.
