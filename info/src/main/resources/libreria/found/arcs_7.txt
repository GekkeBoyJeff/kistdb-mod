@ admiralty | The Admiralty of the Outer Isles
diamond_helmet@end_city | Admiral Seluun's Helm | Log of the flagship, first entry: we set sail from the last island. The void is calm. The Admiral wears her helm even to dinner. She says the stars here are watching. They are.
diamond_chestplate@end_city | The Navigator's Plate | Log, twelfth entry: the Navigator reports that the islands are moving away from us, not we from them. The Admiral has ordered the ship to turn back. The ship has not turned back.
diamond_leggings@end_city | The Purser's Greaves | Log, fortieth entry: rations are low. The purser has started counting the stars instead of the biscuits. He says there are more of them every night. He is right.
diamond_boots@end_city | The Boatswain's Boots | Log, ninetieth entry: the boatswain stepped off the deck to walk on the void. He said it felt like a carpet. He is still walking. We can see him, getting smaller, waving.
diamond_sword@end_city | The Admiral's Ceremonial Sword | Last entry, in a different hand: the Admiral is gone. She left her sword on the wheel and her helm on the deck, and walked to the bow, and asked the stars a question. They answered.
diamond_pickaxe@end_city | The Quartermaster's Pick | Found in the hold of an End ship, with a note: "for chipping our way back in, if the islands ever come close enough again". They never did. The ships wait in the sky, docked and empty.

@ bastionprisoner | The Vault Keeper's Servant
diamond_sword@bastion | Day One in the Vault | My name is Hollis. The piglins keep me to polish the treasure because my hands are small. This sword was the first thing I polished. I could see my face in it. I looked very tired.
diamond_helmet@bastion | Day Thirty | I talk to the helmet now. It is the best listener in the vault. The piglins think it is funny that the human talks to the treasure. Maybe the treasure thinks so too.
diamond_chestplate@bastion | Day One Hundred | I have scratched a little sun on the inside of this plate, where nobody will see. I don't remember what the real one looks like. I remember it was warm.
diamond_leggings@bastion | Day Three Hundred | A new prisoner came today, a girl from a village by the sea. I taught her how to polish without leaving smudges, so they won't hurt her. She laughs sometimes. I had forgotten that sound.
diamond_boots@bastion | The Night We Ran | We ran during the great feast, when the brutes were fat and sleeping. These were the fastest boots in the vault. I gave them to her. She made it to the portal. I heard her call my name from the other side.
diamond_pickaxe@bastion | Hollis's Pick | Found by a collapsed tunnel in the vault, pointing towards the surface. Whoever dug it got very, very far. On the handle: a tiny, careful sun.

@ gardener | The Last Gardener of the Deep
diamond_hoe@ancient_city | Her Hoe | Down in the city of the deep dark, where nothing grows, someone kept a garden. Moss and glow berries in a square of soil, behind a wall, tended with this. In silence, of course. Always in silence.
iron_leggings@ancient_city | Kneeling Greaves | Worn through at both knees from kneeling in the soil. She knelt there every day for years, whispering to the berries, which is the only safe way to talk to anything down there.

@ shepherd | A Shepherd's Year
shears@village | Spring | In spring the shears come out of the drawer, and the sheep, who know exactly what that sound means, all suddenly remember an appointment on the far side of the hill.
leather_helmet@village | Summer | In summer she wears this against the sun and lies in the grass while the sheep do the work of being sheep. Clouds go over. Some of them look like sheep. She counts them, out of habit.
leather_chestplate@village | Autumn | In autumn the wool is dyed in big pots behind the barn: red for the market, blue for the neighbours, one bright pink fleece for her little brother, who asked very nicely.
leather_boots@village | Winter | In winter she walks the fields at dawn in these, breaking the ice on the troughs, and talking to every sheep by name. There are forty-one names. She has never once got one wrong.
shears@village | The Old Shears | Her grandmother's shears, sharpened so often they're half the size they used to be. She doesn't use them. She keeps them in the drawer with the new ones so they can hear the sheep.

wooden_axe@village | The First Axe | In the beginning there was a chest, and in the chest there was an axe, and the first of us took it up, and the first tree fell, and lo, it was very surprised.
wooden_pickaxe@village | The First Pick | And from the tree came a table, and from the table came this pick, and the first of us struck the stone, and the stone said nothing, as stone will, but it was the beginning of a long conversation.
stone_pickaxe@village | The Second Pick | And the stone was made into a better pick, and with it the first of us went down into the dark, and found coal, and made light, and understood that there would be no end to digging.

@ stonesong | The Song of Saltmere
stone_spear@shipwreck | First Verse | Down in the bay where the old town lies, the stones still remember the children's cries; we sang as we swam and we swam as we sang, and the bell in the tower rang and rang.
stone_axe@shipwreck | Second Verse | Father built boats with this axe on the sand, the best boats in Saltmere, the best in the land; then the sea came up softly one night in the spring, and the boats were too slow for so big a thing.
golden_helmet@shipwreck | Third Verse | The mayor wore gold so the sea would see that here was a town of good company; but the sea doesn't bargain and the sea doesn't bow, and the mayor's gold hat is the sea's hat now.
fishing_rod@shipwreck | Fourth Verse | Mother still fishes, they say, from the street, with the fish swimming by at the height of her feet; and if you dive down where the old houses lie, you can see her, still waiting, still looking up high.
leather_chestplate@shipwreck | Last Verse | So sing it again if you're out on the bay, for the town that is sleeping a fathom away; and if something below hums along to the tune, it's only Saltmere, waking up under the moon.
