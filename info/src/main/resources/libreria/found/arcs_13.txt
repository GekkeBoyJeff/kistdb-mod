@ punknight | Sir Cedric the Punisher
> Sir Cedric was the strongest knight in the kingdom, and the kingdom would have loved him, if he had ever said a single sentence that wasn't a pun. He fought with a sword, a shield and a groan-inducing sense of humour. His enemies surrendered just to make him stop talking.
iron_sword | The Pun-ishing Blade | Cedric named his sword the Pun-isher. Every time he drew it, he said "draw!" and then explained that it was a joke about drawing. The dragon he fought at Hollowmere gave up after the third explanation. Witnesses say it looked relieved.
shield | The Sheild of Spelling | Painted with his motto, misspelled, on purpose: "I'm not sheilding anyone from these jokes." The smith refused to paint it. Cedric painted it himself. He was very pleased with himself for a week.
iron_helmet | The Knight-Cap | He called it his knight-cap and wore it to bed. Nobody laughed. He laughed for everyone. His squire once tried to hide it. Cedric found it straight away. "It was right under my nose," he said, "where I keep my helmet." The squire resigned.
iron_chestplate | The Breast-Plate Special | He never ate breakfast without his armour on, because he said it was the most important meal of the day and he wanted to be protected from it. He said this every morning for twenty years. The cook added salt to his porridge every morning in revenge.
iron_boots | The Sole Survivor | At the siege of Kettlebridge, Cedric was the last knight standing. "Sole survivor," he said, pointing at his boots, and waited. The enemy commander wept, laid down his sword and surrendered, on condition that Cedric be gagged. The siege is in the history books. The joke, mercifully, is not.

@ mimicdiary | The Diary of a Chest That Bites
> Every dungeon has one: a chest that looks just like the others, until it doesn't. Nobody ever asks the chest how it feels about it. Somebody once found a diary in one. It was, unexpectedly, very moving, and surprisingly well spelled for something with no hands.
iron_sword | Day 1 of Being a Chest | I was put here to guard the treasure. I am the treasure. That is the trick. A man opened me today. I bit him. He screamed "MIMIC". I do not know who Mimic is. My name is Doris.
leather_boots | Day 40 | Adventurers kick me now before they open me. It is very rude. I do not kick them. I bite them, which is different, because I am a chest and biting is the only thing I can do with my face.
leather_helmet | Day 112 | A small girl opened me today and I did not bite her. She said "hello, you're a nice chest". Nobody has ever said that. I gave her my best sword. She will need it. She also left me a biscuit. I could not eat it but I kept it.
iron_chestplate | Day 300 | A knight came to slay the mimic. He looked at me for a long time. Then he sat down and told me about his wife, who had left him, and his dog, who had not. I did not bite him. He needed someone to listen. Chests are very good at listening. We are mostly inside.
iron_pickaxe | Day 1000 | The girl came back, grown up, with a pickaxe, and dug me out of the dungeon floor, and carried me home. I am a bedside chest now. I hold her socks. It is the best job I have ever had. I still bite her brother.

@ butcherfen | The Butcher of Saltfen
> Saltfen was a quiet fishing village with one butcher, who was very well liked, very well fed and very generous with his sausages. Then the children started disappearing. This is a horror story. It ends badly for almost everyone, and worst of all for the butcher.
iron_axe | The Cleaver | Ground so many times the blade is half the width it was. The butcher of Saltfen sharpened it every morning on the step of his shop and waved to the children as they went to school. They waved back. They always waved back.
leather_chestplate | The Apron | Scrubbed white every evening, so nobody ever saw what it looked like at the end of the day. His wife scrubbed it. When she stopped scrubbing it, three days before anyone else knew anything, it was because she had finally looked closely.
leather_boots | The Cellar Boots | Worn only in the cellar. Left on the top step every morning, wiped clean. The cellar had a lock. The cellar had two locks. In the end the village broke the door down, and what they found down there is the reason Saltfen has no butcher now.
shears | What the Mayor Kept | The mayor kept the butcher's shears in a box under his bed for forty years and never told anyone why. When he died, his daughter opened the box. The shears were clean. Under them, a list of eleven names, in a child's handwriting, each one crossed out.
iron_shovel | Eleven Small Graves | The village dug them in the churchyard with this, one Sunday morning, in silence. Nobody spoke the whole day. On the last grave somebody left a little wooden horse. Nobody ever owned up to it.

@ snowsculptor | The Snow Sculptor of Frostmere
> Every winter, an old woman in Frostmere carves the village square full of snow statues: knights, dragons, a dancing bear, the mayor with a slightly bigger nose than in real life. Every spring they melt. One winter she carved someone she'd lost, and it didn't melt at all.
iron_shovel | The Shaping Spade | She starts every statue with this, shovelling the snow into a hill taller than she is. Children help. Children mostly throw the snow at each other, which she says is also helping, because it gets the snow moving.
shears | The Fine Work | For the eyelashes, the feathers on a snow owl, the stitches on a snow knight's gloves. She uses kitchen shears. She says sculpture is just cooking with very cold ingredients and no lunch at the end of it.
leather_helmet | Her Snow Hat | Knitted by her husband, badly, the first winter they were married. Forty years she wore it. It has holes. She says the holes are where his love got in. She also says it's freezing, and won't get a new one.
leather_chestplate | The Statue That Stayed | The winter after he died she carved him in the square, sitting on his bench, reading. In spring every other statue melted. His stayed. It stayed all summer. The village built a little roof over it. She sits next to it on warm days and reads to him.
leather_boots | The Last Winter | On her last winter she carved nothing. In the morning, the village woke to find a new statue in the square: an old woman, in a holey hat, sitting on a bench beside an old man with a book. Nobody ever admitted to carving it.

@ wellate | What the Well Ate
> There's a well at the edge of Brindlecombe that the villagers don't use any more. They put a lid on it, and then a stone on the lid, and then a second stone. They won't tell you why. Things that were thrown into it come back up sometimes, years later, cleaner than they went in.
iron_sword | First Thing Thrown In | A boy dropped his father's sword down the well by accident and climbed down after it on the rope. The rope came back up. The sword came back up, forty years later, in a bucket of water, polished like new. The boy never did.
leather_helmet | The Bucket Hat | A child's cap that came up in the bucket one morning, dry, and folded. Nobody in the village had lost a cap. Somebody's grandmother went very pale and said she had, sixty years ago, when she was six, and she'd never told anyone.
iron_chestplate | The Lowered Guard | The village sent a guard down on a rope with a torch to look. He went down, and down, and the rope went slack, and then he called up, cheerfully, that it was lovely down there and they should all come. His voice was a little wrong. They cut the rope.
leather_boots | The Boots by the Well | Every spring, a pair of boots appears by the well, still warm, and still wet. Nobody's. The village leaves them there. By morning they're gone again. The baker says it's somebody's idea of a joke. The baker has never once walked past the well alone.
iron_shovel | The Filling In | The village tried to fill the well. Seven carts of gravel went in. In the morning the well was as deep as ever and there was a neat pile of gravel next to it, and on top of the pile, this shovel. So they put a lid on it instead.

iron_axe | Bearbiter | There was a man named Hrolf. He went into the forest with this axe when he was fifteen winters old, and a bear came, and they fought from morning to dark. The bear took his leg. Hrolf took the bear. Each thought it had won. Men say they were both right.

> In the market town of Brackenford, a herbalist called Ellery was put on trial for witchcraft, on the evidence of a cat, a toad, a cured cough and a man who did not like her. The record of the trial survives. So, as it turns out, does Mistress Ellery.
leather_helmet | Exhibit One: The Hat | The prosecution presented the accused's hat as evidence. It was pointed at the top. The accused stated it was pointed because it was old and had been sat on. The court was shown the hat. The court agreed it had been sat on. The court moved on.
shears | Exhibit Two: The Shears | The prosecution stated the accused used these to cut herbs by moonlight. The accused stated she used them to cut herbs by daylight, but that moonlight was free and candles were not. Several members of the court nodded. The prosecutor did not.
leather_chestplate | Exhibit Three: The Apron | The prosecution showed the court a stain on the accused's apron, described as "devilish". The accused identified it as gooseberry jam. The judge asked to smell it. The judge confirmed it was gooseberry. The judge asked for the recipe.
flint_and_steel | The Witness Who Did Not Like Her | The chief witness testified that the accused lit fires with no flint. Cross-examined, he admitted she had a flint, and that he had seen it, and that she had once refused to marry him. The court record notes laughter. The witness left the town that week.
leather_boots | The Verdict | The jury found the accused not guilty, and three of them asked if she did house calls. She walked home in these boots that evening, through a market that was suddenly very friendly. She did house calls for forty more years. She never once turned anyone into a toad. Probably.

> In a wide green land there live a hundred little forest spirits, each hiding under a rock or behind a waterfall or on top of something very tall. Find one and it laughs at you and gives you a seed. A traveller once found every single one. What she got at the end was, by any measure, disappointing.
shield | The First Rock | She lifted a rock by a pond on the first day, because it looked suspicious. A tiny leaf-faced spirit jumped out, shouted "you found me!" and gave her a seed. She did not know what it was for. She had to know. That was the start of it.
leather_boots | The Climbing Boots | Thirty spirits lived on top of things: trees, pillars, the heads of very large statues. She climbed every one of them in these. The boots wore out on the twentieth climb. She finished the last ten barefoot, out of spite.
bow | The Balloon Shot | Some spirits hid in balloons floating over lakes. She shot every one of them down with this. Each spirit fell into the lake, surfaced, and said "you found me!" in a slightly damper voice. She apologised to each one.
leather_helmet | Seventy-Three | She kept count by scratching marks inside her hat. At seventy-three the marks ran together and she lost count, and had to go back over the whole country in her head. She did it in one night, lying awake. She'd missed none.
wooden_hoe | The Golden Reward | When she found the hundredth spirit, the great tree-spirit who had sent them all out thanked her and gave her a gift, wrapped in leaves, golden and gleaming. She unwrapped it. It was dung. Golden dung. She laughed for an hour. She kept it.
