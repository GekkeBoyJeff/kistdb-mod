# Sets: stories told by a whole outfit or a whole kit. Armour sets, a miner's kit, a fisher's kit, an archer's,
# a farmer's, an explorer's, a pilot's… Each part is one piece of the set.

@ odric | Ser Odric's Armour
helmet | Ser Odric's Helm | Ser Odric was the bravest knight in the valley for exactly one afternoon. This helm still has the dent from the evening.
chestplate | Ser Odric's Plate | Polished every morning for twenty years by a squire who hated him. The squire wrote rude words on the inside. They're still there.
leggings | Ser Odric's Greaves | Ser Odric marched into every battle in these. He marched out of most of them backwards, which he called "advancing the other way".
boots | Ser Odric's Sabatons | The only part of the armour that ever saw the front line, because Ser Odric kept kicking doors open and then remembering he was afraid.
sword | Ser Odric's Oathblade | He swore an oath on this blade to protect the valley. The valley still talks about it. Mostly about how loud he swore.
shield | Ser Odric's Crest | Painted with a lion. It looks more like a cat. Ser Odric insisted it was a lion. The cat on it looks embarrassed.

@ bramble | The Bramblewick Miners

shovel | Bramble's Second Shovel | Nobody knows what happened to the first shovel. Bramble only ever said "the mountain kept it".
helmet | Bramble's Lamp-Helm | The lamp went out three hundred metres down, and he walked home in the dark by counting his steps. He was off by one. He's never told anyone where the one went.
boots | Bramble's Pit Boots | Steel toes, iron soles, and a secret compartment with a single emergency biscuit. The biscuit is older than you.
axe | Bramble's Prop-Cutter | Used to cut the beams that held up the tunnels. The tunnels are still up. Bramble said that was the only part of mining that mattered.

@ saltwife | The Saltwife's Kit

boots | Nessa's Tide Boots | She walked out at low tide to where the old harbour used to be. The harbour comes back at night, she said, and so do the ships.

boots | Muddy Boots | There is mud on these from every spring for sixty years. It's less mud than history at this point.
