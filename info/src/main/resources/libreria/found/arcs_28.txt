@ lettershorse | Letters to My Horse
> When I retired from the king's cavalry, my horse retired with me, to a field by my cottage. She's old now, as I am. She can't read, of course. I write to her anyway, every week, and read the letters to her over the fence. She listens better than most people I've known. I'm putting them here, with the rest of my things.
iron_helmet | The First Letter | Dear Clover. You carried me through nine battles and never once threw me, though I deserved it at least twice. I've bought you a field. It's the best field in the valley. The neighbour says it's too good for a horse. I told him it's not good enough. He doesn't understand. You do.
leather_boots | The Second Letter | Dear Clover. I walked the field this morning in my old boots, the ones I wore at Redmarch, and you followed me the whole way round, as if we were on patrol. We were, in a way. We'll always be on patrol, you and I. The fence is the frontier. The apple tree is the enemy.
iron_sword | The Third Letter | Dear Clover. I've hung my sword over the door. I won't need it again. I keep looking at it. I keep thinking about the men I lost. You were there. You know. You never looked back, though. I've decided to learn that from you. I'm trying. You'll have to be patient with me.
shears | The Fourth Letter | Dear Clover. I've learned to trim your mane myself, with these shears, since the groom left. I've made a terrible mess of it. You look like a hedge. You don't seem to mind. The neighbour laughed. I told him you'd been decorated for bravery. He stopped laughing.
fishing_rod | The Last Letter | Dear Clover. You went in your sleep last night, in the field, under the apple tree. I'm going to sit by the river today and fish and not catch anything and think about you. I'll keep writing. I don't think I know how to stop. Thank you for carrying me. You were the bravest of us all.

@ banditbaker | The Bandit Who Became a Baker
> Big Rolf robbed the coach road for twenty years, and was the most feared bandit in the province, until the day he held up a coach and the only passenger was an old baker, who looked at him, sighed, and said "you look hungry." Rolf was hungry. The baker gave him a bun. It changed everything. It was a very good bun.
iron_axe | The Bandit's Axe | Rolf's axe was famous on the coach road: huge, notched, with a name carved in the handle, which was "Persuasion". He'd hold it up and the coach would stop. When the baker gave him the bun, he put Persuasion down on the road to eat it. He never quite picked it back up.
leather_chestplate | The Bandit's Coat | Black leather, studded, with a hood. He wore it to his first day as the baker's apprentice and the customers fled screaming. The baker gave him an apron to wear over it. The customers came back. The coat is still under the apron. Rolf says it reminds him where he's been.
iron_sword | The Sword Turned Knife | The baker said bread can't be cut with an axe. Rolf had his old sword cut down into a bread knife. It's the best bread knife in the province. It still has the notches from the coach road. He says each one is a reminder of a loaf he didn't bake. He bakes a lot of loaves.
leather_boots | The Early Mornings | Bakers get up at three in the morning. So, it turns out, do bandits. Rolf says it was the only part of the job he didn't have to learn. He walks to the bakery every morning in the dark, down the coach road. The coaches pass him. They don't stop. He waves.
flint_and_steel | The Oven | When the old baker died, he left Rolf the bakery and the oven, and the oven's fire, which had never once gone out in forty years. Rolf keeps it going. Every morning, on the coach road, there's a little table with buns on it and a sign that says "free. you look hungry."

@ blooddynasty | The Lord of Blood's Dynasty
> Beneath the broken land, in a palace of flesh by a river of blood, there sits a lord who was born an omen, with horns, and was hidden away, and grew bitter in the dark. He took a demigod for his bride, sleeping, against her will, and waits for her to wake. He calls his halls a dynasty. It is a dynasty of one.
iron_helmet | The Horned Crown | Born with horns, as omens are, and kept in the sewers beneath the capital until he was grown. He wears the crown his mother never gave him, wrought from his own shed horn. He says it was his right. Nobody was alive to argue. He made sure of that.
golden_sword@nether | The Bloodflame Trident | A spear of blood that burns, fashioned from his own veins. He calls down the fire with it, in his throne room, on any who come to wake his bride. Many have come. The floor of the throne room is stained in a hundred colours. He keeps it that way. He says it's decorative.
leather_chestplate | The Robe of the Dynasty | Crimson, soaked through, never once dry. His followers wear robes like it, and kneel in the blood river, and chant his name. They are pilgrims who came to stop him and stayed, enchanted, with blank eyes. He calls them his family. They do not answer to their own names.
shears | The Cut of the Bloodflame | He cuts his own palm with these silver shears before every battle, to let the blood flame rise. The scars on his hands are a map. Nobody has read it. Some scholars think it is a map back to the surface, where his mother lived. They think he drew it for himself, and never used it.
iron_sword | The Sword of the Waking | A hero came at last and cut him down, and woke the sleeping demigod, who looked around at the palace of blood and the kneeling pilgrims and the dead lord, and said only, "how long?" The hero said it had been a very long time. She walked out without looking back.

@ greatfire | The Great Fire of Emberbury
> The Great Fire of Emberbury began in a bakery on Pudding Lane in the early hours of a dry autumn morning and burned for four days. Two-thirds of the city was lost. The chronicle of the fire, kept by the parish clerk, records the names of everyone who fought it. It is a long list. It is mostly women.
flint_and_steel | The Bakery | The chronicle records that the baker swore under oath he had put out his oven before going to bed. The chronicle records also that he was known to swear a great many things under oath, and that the jury did not believe him, and that nobody in the city ever bought bread from him again.
iron_axe | The Firebreak | The fire was stopped, in the end, by pulling down houses in its path to make a firebreak. The mayor would not allow it at first, because the houses belonged to wealthy men. A widow called Agnes Thorne began pulling down her own house with this axe. The mayor gave way.
iron_shovel | The Bucket Line | For four days the women of the city stood in a line from the river to the fire and passed buckets hand to hand. The chronicle names one thousand two hundred of them. The clerk notes that his wife was eleventh in line and that he wrote her name with particular care.
leather_boots | The Running Boy | A boy of twelve ran messages between the fire captains for four days and nights, through streets that were burning. The chronicle records his name as "Tom, nobody knows whose". After the fire, he was given a house, an apprenticeship and the freedom of the city. He's the mayor now.
shield | The New City | The city was rebuilt in stone, with wide streets and no thatched roofs. On the site of the bakery on Pudding Lane there now stands a tall stone column, and on the column, at the top, a golden flame. It is called the Monument. The bakery that stands beside it is very, very careful.

@ grandmacookbook | Grandma's Cookbook of Disasters
> My grandmother was a terrible cook, and she knew it, and she didn't care. She kept a cookbook of every disaster she ever made, with notes, so that nobody in the family would ever make the same mistakes. It's the most-read book in our house. I've added my own disasters. I think she'd like that.
flint_and_steel | The Christmas Goose | "Goose, Christmas, year 14. Set it alight. Also set the curtains alight. Also the cat, briefly. Everybody fine. Ate bread and cheese. Best Christmas we ever had, the children say. Note: never baste a goose with brandy near an open fire. Note: never baste a goose with brandy at all."
iron_axe | The Pumpkin | "Pumpkin pie, autumn. Couldn't cut the pumpkin with a knife, so used your grandfather's axe. Pumpkin went through the kitchen window. Hit the vicar. Vicar fine. Made pie from what was left. Vicar came in to complain and stayed for pie. Note: buy smaller pumpkins."
fishing_rod | The Fish | "Fish, Friday. Caught it myself. Forgot to clean it. Cooked it anyway. Note: clean the fish. Note: no really, clean the fish. Your father was sick for two days and still talks about it. He says it's why he never eats fish. He's lying. He just likes the story."
leather_chestplate | The Apron | "Wedding cake, your aunt's wedding. Seven tiers. Collapsed during the speeches. Into the groom's lap. Your aunt laughed so hard she cried and said it was the best thing that ever happened at a wedding. They've been married forty years. Note: cake is not the point."
shears | The Last Page | In my handwriting: "Grandma's funeral. Made her famous terrible scones, from her book, with all her mistakes left in. Everyone ate one. Everyone cried. Everyone laughed. Note: she was right. It was never about the cooking." I'm still terrible. I'm keeping the book going.

@ horsefear | Sir Gareth, Who Was Afraid of Horses
> Sir Gareth was the bravest knight in the kingdom. He had fought a troll, a giant and a man who said his mother was a hedgehog. He was afraid of exactly one thing: horses. A knight who can't ride a horse is not much of a knight. He spent his whole career hiding it. Everybody knew.
iron_helmet | The Helmet With the Visor Down | He kept his visor down whenever there was a horse nearby, so nobody would see his face. This meant he kept his visor down almost all the time. He walked into a great many doors. The king thought he was very devoted to his duty. The king was wrong about most things.
iron_boots | The Walking Boots | He walked everywhere. Into every battle, behind the cavalry, on foot, in full armour, explaining that a true knight should feel the earth beneath his feet. Several other knights tried it, because it sounded noble. They gave up after a mile. Gareth walked a thousand.
iron_sword | The Sword He Fought With | He was the best swordsman in the kingdom because, unlike every other knight, he'd spent his career fighting on foot, where swords are actually useful. At the great tournament, he lost every joust and won every sword fight. The trophy was a horse. He gave it to his squire.
shield | The Horse That Came Back | The horse he gave away followed him home. And everywhere, after that. It stood outside his window. It walked behind him into battle. It nuzzled his visor. He shook. He shouted. It didn't leave. After a year, he reached out and patted its nose. It was very soft.
leather_chestplate | Sir Gareth Rides | He rode it in the end, once, at the age of sixty, very slowly, round the courtyard, with the whole castle watching and pretending not to. When he got off, he was white as a sheet, and he said, "that was terrible," and then he went back and did it again. The horse looked very pleased.

@ butcherbird | The Tree of the Butcher Bird
> On the moor above Crowfell there's a thorn tree, and on its thorns, every spring, there are things. Mice. Beetles. Small birds. A shrike puts them there, to keep for later. The village children dare each other to touch the tree. One year, the things on the thorns started to get bigger.
shears | The Gamekeeper's Snips | The gamekeeper went up to the tree in the spring to cut the things down, as he did every year. He came back down and wouldn't say what he'd found. He went up again with a sack. He burned the sack. That autumn he moved to the city and never came back to the moor.
leather_boots | The Shepherd's Boots | A shepherd lost a lamb on the moor. He found it on the tree, high up, on the biggest thorn. He looked up into the branches and saw something looking back that was not a bird. He said it had a face like a man and the wings of something much larger. He ran.
bow | The Hunter's Bow | A hunter went up with this bow to kill whatever was nesting in the thorn tree. He sat beneath it all night. In the morning, his bow was on a thorn at the top of the tree, out of reach. He didn't remember climbing up. He hadn't climbed up. He went home very quietly.
iron_axe | The Woodcutter's Axe | The village decided to cut the tree down. Six men went up with axes. They cut for a whole day. The wood was hard as iron. The thorns cut their hands. At sunset they heard something above them, settling in the branches, and they left the axes in the trunk and ran.
flint_and_steel | The Tree Now | The axes are still in the trunk, rusted, with the tree grown round them. Nobody touches the tree now. Nobody dares. In the spring, the village leaves a basket of meat at the bottom, and in the morning the basket is empty, and the tree is bare. It seems to be enough. For now.

@ dragoncastle | The Dragon Who Loved a Castle
> Dragons hoard things: gold, gems, the occasional princess. The dragon Emberwing hoarded none of these. She fell in love with a castle. A great grey keep on a hill, with nine towers and a moat, and she curled up around it and refused to leave. The family who lived in the castle had some concerns.
golden_helmet | The Lord's Crown | Lord Ashby woke up one morning to find a dragon wrapped round his castle, her head resting on the gatehouse, her tail in the moat, purring. He put on his crown, went to the battlements, and asked her to leave. She licked the tower he was standing on. He went back inside.
shield | The Family Shield | The castle's banner was a grey tower on a red field. Emberwing gazed at it, adoringly, for hours. The family tried taking it down. She sulked. The sky went dark. They put it back up. She cheered up. The family began to suspect that their castle was, technically, being courted.
iron_sword | The Knight Sent to Fight Her | The king sent a knight to fight her. The knight arrived, and drew his sword, and Emberwing curled tighter around the castle, protectively, and looked at him with such betrayal that he lowered his sword and said, "oh, she loves it." He went home. He told the king it was romantic.
fishing_rod | Fishing From the Tail | The family learned to live with her. The children fished from her tail, which lay in the moat. The cook warmed the bread on her flank. Bandits stopped coming entirely. Lord Ashby admitted, after a year, that it was the safest the castle had ever been, and the warmest.
golden_chestplate | The Wedding | After three years, Lord Ashby performed a small ceremony on the battlements, marrying the dragon Emberwing to Castle Ashby, in sickness and in health. She wept fire with joy. Two towers were lost. Nobody minded. The castle is the only one in the kingdom with a dragon on its banner.

@ debtbard | The Bard Who Owed Everyone Money
> There's a bard who travels the northern roads with a lute and a feathered hat and a reputation for the finest ballads in the land. He also owes money to every innkeeper, every tailor, two dukes, a monster hunter and, it was recently discovered, a goat. He pays in songs. Not everyone accepts songs.
leather_helmet | The Feathered Hat | He bought it on credit from a tailor in the capital, who's been chasing him for the money for nine years. The tailor has a song about him now. It's called "The Tailor's Lament". It's very popular. The tailor hates it. Everybody who hears it orders a hat from him. He's never been richer.
leather_chestplate | The Doublet | Red, slashed, very fashionable, also on credit, from a different tailor. The bard says that a man who dresses well is a man people want to give money to. He's right about that. He's wrong about the part where he pays it back. He never gets to that part.
iron_sword | The Monster Hunter's Sword | He borrowed this from a monster hunter, for "a moment", to look dashing in a painting. The monster hunter has been waiting eleven years. The painting is very good. The monster hunter has a copy of it on his wall. He looks at it and sighs and says, "he looks good with it, at least."
leather_boots | The Running Boots | When the creditors come, he runs, in these, which he also hasn't paid for. He's very fast. He says it's the lute. It keeps his balance. The cobbler who made them chased him for a mile once and then sat down on the road and laughed. He says it's the best advertisement his boots ever had.
fishing_rod | The Goat | The goat was owed a carrot. The bard ate it. The goat followed him for a hundred miles, bleating, until he gave in, went to a farm, bought a carrot with real money, and gave it to the goat. The goat ate it and left. It's the only debt he's ever paid. He's written a song about it. It's his best.

@ ninethane | The Thane of Every Hold
> The hero who killed the dragon at the top of the world was rewarded, in every city she saved, with the title of thane, a house and a housecarl: a sworn warrior to follow her everywhere and carry her things. She saved nine cities. She had nine housecarls. They followed her everywhere. All nine. At once.
iron_helmet | The First Housecarl | The first was a woman who said, every time she was asked to do something, that she was sworn to carry the hero's burdens. The hero handed her everything. She carried it. She complained, but she carried it. The others called her "the original". She liked that.
iron_chestplate | The Fourth Housecarl | By the fourth, the hero couldn't walk into a shop without a small army behind her. Shopkeepers hid. Guards saluted. A dragon, seeing them coming over a hill, turned round and flew away. The fourth housecarl said that was the best fight he'd ever been in. Nobody had fought.
iron_sword | The Seventh Housecarl | The seventh got lost. Nobody noticed for a week. When they found him, he was standing in a doorway in a village two cities back, still waiting for the hero to come out. He'd been there the whole time. He said he was sworn to wait. She took him for a nice dinner.
shield | The Nine Shields | When they all held up their shields together, in a line, they made a wall nine shields wide. Bandits who met them on the road gave up immediately, without a word, and handed over their own gold, which the housecarls carried. Everything the housecarls carried was the hero's burden.
iron_boots | The Hero Retires | She retired to a cottage with eight rooms. The housecarls took one each. The ninth slept in the barn by choice. She says she's never once been lonely and never once had a quiet cup of tea. She says she wouldn't change it. She says please don't make her a thane of anything else.
