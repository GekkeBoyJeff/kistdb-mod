@ drownedchoir | The Drowned Choir of Saltmere Bay
> When the old church at Saltmere slid into the bay in the great storm, it took the choir with it, mid-hymn. The drowned still walk the bottom of the bay, in their robes, with tridents they took from the sea. On calm nights, fishermen say, you can hear them singing the last verse. They never get to the end.
trident | The Choirmaster's Trident | He was conducting when the church went down, and he's conducting still, with this trident instead of a baton, at the bottom of the bay. Divers who've seen him say he keeps perfect time. They say the drowned watch him. They say if you swim too close, he points at you, and they all turn.
trident | The Soprano's Trident | She had the finest voice in Saltmere, and she was the one who held the last note as the water came in. Fishermen hear her more than the others. She throws her trident at boats that come too close. She's never hit one. Some say she's not trying to.
trident | The Choirboy's Trident | The youngest of the choir was nine. He stays near the surface, the divers say, and swims up sometimes, to the light, and stops just below it, and looks up, and goes back down. A fisherman's wife leaves a lantern on the shore for him every night.
leather_chestplate | The Robe Washed Up | One choir robe washed up on the beach a year after the storm, dry, folded, as if it had been put there carefully. It belonged to the organist, who'd been late for practice that night and wasn't in the church. He took it as a message. He went into the sea the next day.
fishing_rod | The Last Verse | An old fisherman, who'd sung in the choir as a boy and moved away before the storm, came back at eighty and sat in his boat over the drowned church and sang the last verse of the hymn, all the way to the end. The singing below stopped. It hasn't been heard since.

@ wanderingtrader | Confessions of a Wandering Trader
> You know me. I'm the one who appears out of nowhere at night with two llamas and a selection of goods at outrageous prices. I drink an invisibility potion when the zombies come. I vanish in a few days. I've never once explained myself. I'm retired now. I can finally tell you how the business works.
leather_chestplate | The Robe | Blue, with a hood, very mysterious. That's the point. Nobody haggles with a mysterious man. I've sold a single fish for an emerald. I've sold sand. Sand! To a man who lived on a beach. He thanked me. That's the power of a good robe.
shears | The Llama Clippers | The llamas are not mine. I hire them from an agency. They're paid in hay and they're union. When I'm attacked, they spit on the attacker, which is in the contract. Twice, they've spat on me, because I was late with the hay. That was also in the contract.
leather_boots | Walking Boots | I appear at night beside your house because I've walked all day to get there and I'm too tired to knock. That's the whole secret. You think it's magic. It's blisters. Forty years of blisters. I'd sell you some, if you were offering an emerald. You probably would.
fishing_rod | The Overstocked Item | Fishing rods. Every trader has too many fishing rods. Nobody knows why. We can't shift them. We've tried everything: discounts, sales, a free fish. The agency keeps sending them. I think there's a warehouse somewhere just full of fishing rods. I've never found it.
iron_shovel | Retirement | I retired to a little house in a valley and built a fence and planted a garden. Last week, a wandering trader appeared beside my house at night with two llamas. I bought a fish for an emerald. I don't know why. I think it's the robe. It's a very good robe.

@ golempoppy | The Iron Golem and the Poppy
> Iron golems are made to protect villages, and that's all they do: walk the paths, and watch the walls, and throw anything hostile very high into the air. This one did that too. But every morning, before his rounds, he stopped by the same small girl and handed her a single red poppy. Nobody had built him to do that.
iron_chestplate | The Iron Body | He was made of iron blocks and a pumpkin, as they all are, and he walked the village for nine years without stopping, cracked a little more every year by zombies, pillagers, and one very angry ravager. He mended himself with iron the villagers left out. He never asked for it.
iron_sword | The Arms That Threw | He threw a ravager forty feet into the air once, and it landed on the pillager captain, and the raid ended. The village held a feast for him. He didn't eat. He stood at the edge of the square and watched them. In the morning, he gave the girl her poppy and went on his rounds.
shears | The Poppy Snips | He grew the poppies himself, by the well, in a bed he'd dug with his hands. He couldn't hold the shears very well. The girl taught him. It took a year. She was six. She was very patient. He was very large. He never once crushed a flower.
leather_boots | The Girl's Boots | She grew up and left for the city, and he walked to the edge of the village every morning and stood there with a poppy, and looked down the road. She came back every summer. Every summer, he was there, at the edge, with a poppy. Every summer she cried.
wooden_hoe | The Last Poppy | When he finally rusted still, she was a grandmother, and came home for good, and found him standing at the edge of the village, with a poppy in his hand, and moss on his shoulders. She took the poppy. She planted it by the well. It's a field of them now.

@ librarianillager | The Pillager Who Wanted to Read
> Pillagers raid, burn, and steal. That's what they're for. One young pillager, raiding a village library, picked up a book to throw on the fire and opened it by mistake. It had pictures. He couldn't read the words. He stole it anyway, and his life changed, very slowly, as he tried to work out what it said.
crossbow | The Crossbow He Put Down | He was the best shot in the outpost. He put the crossbow down the day he took the book, to hold it with both hands, and never quite picked it back up. The captain shouted at him. He nodded, and went on looking at the pictures. The captain gave up.
iron_axe | The Axe for the Door | The next raid, he used his axe to break into the library again, instead of the houses, and stole the alphabet book from the children's shelf. The librarian saw him do it. She didn't shout. She watched him put it in his coat very carefully, like it might break.
leather_chestplate | The Coat With the Books | He kept the books in his coat, against his chest, and read them in the watchtower at night by moonlight, sounding out the letters one at a time. It took him a year to read the first book. It was about a duck who lost her hat. He thought it was the best thing he'd ever read.
iron_helmet | The Desertion | He deserted the outpost and walked into the village with his hands up and the books held out. The iron golem picked him up. The librarian told it to put him down. She asked what he wanted. He said, very slowly, in her language, the first words he'd ever said in it: "more books."
leather_boots | The Assistant Librarian | He works in the library now, in a grey coat, shelving books. The children are scared of him for about a week, then they make him read the duck book out loud, every day, in his strange voice. He does all the voices. The duck's is the best. He's very proud of the duck.

@ withercult | The Cult of the Three Skulls
> In the age of the black castles there was a cult that believed the end of the world would come from three skulls and four blocks of soul sand, and that whoever built it would be spared. They hunted the fortress skeletons for their heads for a hundred years. They built it. They were not spared.
iron_sword | The Ritual Blade | Every member of the cult carried one, to cut the heads from the black skeletons in the fortresses. A fortress skeleton only gives up its skull one time in forty. The cultists killed thousands. They said each one was a step closer to salvation. The skeletons did not agree.
golden_helmet@nether | The High Priest's Crown | The high priest wore a crown of gold, so the piglins wouldn't kill him on his long walks through the fortresses. He walked them for thirty years. He found two skulls. The third was found by a novice, aged twelve, on her first day. He never forgave her.
flint_and_steel | The Altar Fire | The altar was built in the open, under the sky, on top of a hill, so that the world could see. They placed the soul sand in a T. They placed the skulls. The novice who'd found the third placed it last. The fire went out. Then it all went very white, and very loud.
leather_boots | The Novice's Boots | The novice was the only survivor. She was thrown clear by the first blast, and ran, and didn't stop running for three days. Behind her the hill and the cult and the altar were gone. In front of her, a black three-headed thing was flying, east, toward the cities.
shield | What She Did | She spent the rest of her life warning the cities, building walls, teaching people how to fight it. When it was finally brought down, by an army of a thousand, she was there, old, with a shield. She told the commander: "they thought it would spare them. Nothing spares anyone."

@ raidbanner | The Banner of the Raid Captain
> Every pillager raid is led by a captain who carries a banner: grey, with a black face, and a long dark border. Whoever kills the captain takes the omen. Whoever takes the omen into a village brings the raid. This is the chronicle of one banner, which passed through eleven hands in eleven years.
crossbow | The First Captain | The first captain to carry it led nine raids and lost none. The chronicle describes him as cruel, effective, and very fond of his banner, which he would not allow to be washed. He was killed in the tenth raid by a shepherd with a sling. The shepherd took the banner home.
iron_axe | The Shepherd | The shepherd hung the banner on his wall as a trophy and felt very strange for a week, and then walked into his own village, and the raid followed him. Three houses burned. He never forgave himself. He burned the banner. It did not burn. He buried it.
iron_shovel | The Digger | A boy found it, digging in the shepherd's old field, and took it to the next town to sell. The raid followed. The chronicle lists, with a terrible patience, nine more owners: a merchant, a knight, a thief, a priest, two farmers, a soldier, a child and a widow.
iron_helmet | The Knight | The knight who carried it seventh knew what it was and rode with it deliberately to the pillager outpost and planted it in front of their tower, to bring the raid there instead. It did not work like that. It works like nothing else. He was the chronicle's only owner to die laughing.
shield | The Widow | The eleventh owner, the widow, simply did not go into any village. She lived alone in the woods with the banner on her wall for the rest of her life and told every traveller who came to her door to stay away from her. When she died, the chronicle ends. Nobody knows where the banner went.

@ evokerlast | The Last Spell of the Evoker
> The evokers of the dark mansions are the oldest of the illagers, and the cruellest: they summon fangs from the earth and little winged spirits to do their killing. They are very old and very bitter. One of them, dying, cast one last spell. Nobody has ever been able to work out what it did.
iron_axe | The Mansion Axe | The vindicators of the mansion carried these, and guarded the evoker in the room at the top of the mansion, which was his study. He never came out. They brought him food on a tray. He sent it back, every time, with a note: "less salt." They never stopped adding salt.
leather_chestplate | The Robe of Fangs | His robe was black and green and stitched with a pattern of teeth. When he raised his arms, the fangs came out of the floor in a line, snapping. He used it to kill intruders. He also used it, the vindicators say, to open jars. He could not open jars.
iron_sword | The Hero's Sword | The hero who finally reached the top of the mansion and struck him down was a young woman with an iron sword and absolutely no patience for spells. He fell. He looked up at her. He raised his hand. She raised her sword. He said: "no. Not for you." And cast.
golden_helmet | The Totem | Evokers carry a little golden totem, which saves your life once, if you're holding it. He was holding it. He didn't use it. The hero picked it up, and looked at it, and at him, and understood that he'd chosen. He'd been alive a very long time. He was tired of salt.
shears | The Spell | The spell went out of the window. Nobody saw where it went. The next spring, every flower in the forest around the mansion came up black, with tiny teeth. They're harmless. They snap at bees, a little. The hero says it was the last joke of a very old, very bitter man.

@ furnacegolem | The Keeper of the Furnace Giant
> In the burning lands, a giant made of iron and fire walks a circuit around the ruined castle, and has done so since the war that broke the world. It burns everything it finds. Every night at its feet, unburned, walks a small figure in a ragged cloak: its keeper, who tends its fire, and who has never once looked up at what it does.
iron_helmet | The Keeper's Helm | Blackened by smoke to the colour of the night. The keeper does not take it off, for the heat would kill her, and because, she says, she does not want to see. She walks at the giant's heel, between its steps. It has never stepped on her. It knows where she is.
iron_shovel | The Coal Shovel | Every night when the giant stops, she climbs a ladder of iron rungs up its leg and shovels fuel into the furnace in its belly. If it goes out, the giant stops. If it stops, she says, it dies. She doesn't want it to die. She has never said why. Nobody has asked her twice.
iron_chestplate | The Heat-Plate | She wears iron against the fire of its belly, and the iron is warped and pitted and has melted into her shape over the years. She says it is the only armour that ever fitted. She says it was made for her by the fire. She sounds proud. She sounds very tired.
leather_boots | The Ash Boots | She walks in ash up to her ankles, the ash of everything the giant burns. Villages. Forests. Soldiers who come to stop it. She walks through it without looking down. She says the ash is not her fault. She says this to nobody, every night, over and over.
flint_and_steel | The Last Spark | A traveller asked her once, at last, why she kept it burning. She was quiet a long time. Then she said: "it was my brother. They put him in it. If it goes out, he goes out." The traveller didn't know what to say. He left. The giant still walks. The fire is still lit.

@ sirpounce | Why My Cat Is Called Sir Pounce
> My cat is called Sir Pounce and he's a knight. He was knighted by me, with a spoon, on the kitchen table. He's the bravest cat in the whole street and he has defended our house from at least nine monsters. I'm writing down his deeds so everyone knows. Mum says only three of them were monsters.
leather_helmet | His Helmet | Sir Pounce has a helmet I made from the toe of one of Dad's old socks. He wears it for about one second and then he takes it off with his back paw. But that's how knights do it, I think. They take their helmet off to show you their face, so you know they're brave.
iron_sword | His First Battle | His first monster was a moth. It came in the window and Sir Pounce jumped at it from the top of the sofa and missed and landed in the fruit bowl. But the moth was so scared it flew back out of the window. So Sir Pounce won. I gave him a medal. It was a bottle top.
shield | The Great Dog Battle | The neighbours' dog came into our garden and Sir Pounce went all puffy like a dandelion and hissed so loud the dog ran away and fell in the pond. Mum says that was a real monster. Dad says that was a real dog. I say a dog is a monster when it's in our garden.
fishing_rod | His Payment | Knights get paid in treasure but Sir Pounce gets paid in fish. Dad took me fishing and we caught a little one and I brought it home for Sir Pounce and he ate it in one bite and then he slept on my bed all night, purring. That's how I know he's loyal.
leather_boots | The Monster Under the Stairs | There's a monster under the stairs. I know there is. I can hear it at night. Sir Pounce sits outside the cupboard every night, guarding. Mum says it's mice. But Sir Pounce has never let it come out. Not once. That's why he's called Sir Pounce. Because he's ready.

@ wizardspelling | The Wizard Who Couldn't Spell
> Magic is a precise art, the archmage told his students. A single wrong letter in a spell can have disastrous consequences. His least promising student, Barnaby, nodded seriously, and then went home and summoned a "fireball" that came out as a "ferret ball", which is, as you might imagine, very different.
leather_helmet | The Pointed Hat | Barnaby's hat had stars on it and the word "WISSARD" embroidered on the front by his mother. He wore it with pride. The archmage asked him to take it off. Barnaby didn't understand why. Nobody had the heart to tell him for six years.
flint_and_steel | The Fire He Meant | He tried to light the classroom fire with a spell and set the archmage's beard on fire, then put it out, then turned it into a bird, which flew away. The archmage grew a new beard. It took two years. He made Barnaby light the fire with flint after that.
fishing_rod | The Fish Spell | He was asked to summon a fish for the school dinner. He summoned a "fist". It punched the archmage. The second time he summoned "fifsh", which was a sort of fish with legs that ran away. The third time he gave up and went down to the river with a rod.
shears | The Sheep Mistake | He was asked to turn a sheep into a ship. The sheep became a shop. It's still there, at the edge of the school grounds, and it sells wool. It's the only shop in the world run by a sheep. It's very successful. Barnaby says it was his plan all along.
bow | The Final Exam | In his final exam, he cast a spell to defeat a troll, and spelled "troll" as "trowel", and the troll became a garden trowel. He passed. The examiners said results were what mattered. He's a professional gardener now. His tomatoes are, people say, magical.

@ lastwolf | The Last Wolf of the Grey Hills
> There were wolves in the grey hills for ten thousand years, until there weren't. The farmers hunted them, the cold winters starved them, and by the time anyone thought to count, there was one: an old she-wolf with a white muzzle, who came down to the farms every winter and looked, but never took anything.
bow | The Hunter's Bow | The best hunter in the hills had her in his sights three times in three winters, and never loosed. His friends asked why. He said there'd be no wolves after her, and he didn't want to be the one. He said it gruffly. He said it every time. Every time, she looked at him, and went away.
leather_chestplate | The Farmer's Coat | A farmer's daughter found her one winter in the barn, half dead from cold, lying beside the sheep, not touching them. The farmer came with his gun. The daughter stood in front of the wolf in her father's coat and wouldn't move. He went back inside. The wolf stayed the winter.
leather_boots | The Spring | In the spring, the wolf went back up into the hills, and the farmer's daughter followed her as far as the tree line in these boots, and watched her go. She said the wolf stopped at the top and looked back at her. The farmer said she imagined it. She didn't.
shears | The Collar | The daughter made her a collar out of red wool, the next winter, when she came down again. The wolf let her put it on. Travellers who saw her in the hills after that, a grey wolf in a red collar, left her alone. Word spread. Nobody in the hills would shoot a wolf with a collar.
fishing_rod | The Pups | In the fifth spring, the wolf didn't go up into the hills alone. Behind her, there were three grey pups. Nobody knows where the father came from. Somewhere further north, perhaps, where there were still wolves. The farmer's daughter sat in the barn doorway and cried all morning.

@ noonwraith | The Contract on the Field Wraith
> At noon, in the long grass of the old wheat field, there's a woman in white who walks and weeps and kills whoever she finds there. The village posted a contract on the notice board. A monster hunter with white hair and two swords took it. He found out who she was first. He always found out who they were first.
iron_sword | The Steel Sword | For men. He didn't draw it. He spoke to the village elders instead, and to the old women, and to the drunk at the tavern who remembered everything, and he learned that forty years ago a bride had been killed in that field on the morning of her wedding, by men who were never caught.
golden_sword | The Silver Sword | For monsters. He drew it at noon, in the long grass, and she came at him, screaming, faster than wind. He couldn't hurt her. Not like this. She had to be bound to something first. Something she'd lost. Something that still held her there.
iron_shovel | The Dig | He dug in the field, where the drunk said she'd been found, and found a ring, a small one, silver, engraved with two names. One was hers. He went back at noon with the ring in his hand and held it up, and she stopped, and looked at it, and went very still.
leather_boots | The Groom | The other name on the ring was a man still living, an old man, in the next village, who had never married. The hunter went to see him. The old man came to the field at noon. The hunter stayed back. Whatever was said in the long grass, nobody heard it.
golden_helmet | The Fee | He took his fee, which was small, because the village was poor. The old man tried to pay him more. He wouldn't take it. He said the old man had already paid enough. The field has been quiet since. The old man was buried at the edge of it. His bride's ring is on his finger.

@ mudcrabmerchant | The Mudcrab Merchant
> On a certain stretch of coast there's a mudcrab, an ordinary-looking crustacean the size of a dog, who will, if you don't kill it immediately, talk to you, and offer to trade. Nobody knows how. Nobody knows where it gets its stock. It is the best merchant on the coast. It is extremely rude about its competitors.
fishing_rod | The Crab's Best-Seller | It sells fishing rods, mainly, which it says it gets from "the sea", and which are suspiciously high-quality. The fishermen on the coast all buy from it. They don't ask where the rods come from. They have noticed, however, that rods left on the beach tend to go missing.
iron_sword | The Sword It Bought | It buys anything. It bought a sword from a hero for twice what any shop would pay. The hero asked what a crab wanted with a sword. The crab said it was diversifying. The hero went away very confused. The crab sold the sword the next day to a shop, at a profit.
leather_helmet | The Customer Service | It insults its customers, mainly. It says your armour is ugly. It says your prices are ridiculous. It says the merchant in the next town is a thief with a face like a boot. Nobody minds. Everyone says it's the most honest merchant in the province.
shield | The Competition | A new merchant opened a shop on the coast. The mudcrab took it personally. It undercut every price. It insulted the merchant's mother, in public, by the docks. The merchant closed after a month and went back to the city. The crab said he'd been "warned".
golden_helmet | The Gold Stash | Nobody knows where it keeps its gold. A treasure hunter once spent a summer digging up the whole beach. He found nothing. When he left, the crab called after him, "you missed a bit," and laughed, a horrible clicking crab laugh. He's still not sure which bit.
