@ odricsquire | The Squire Who Polished Ser Odric
> For twenty years I polished the armour of Ser Odric, the loudest coward in the valley, and every morning I wrote something rude on the inside of his breastplate where he'd never look. I'm old now. I want to set down what I wrote, and what I found out about him in the end, because nobody else will.
iron_chestplate | The Inside of the Breastplate | The first thing I wrote was "fool", very small, by the left strap. The second was "coward". By the tenth year there wasn't room, so I wrote round the edge. He never found out. Or I thought he never found out. I'll come to that.
iron_boots@village | My Own Boots | I walked behind his horse for twenty years in these, carrying his shield, his sword, his lunch and his excuses. When he advanced backwards, I advanced backwards. When he swore his oaths, I held the oath-book. Nobody ever swore an oath at me.
leather_helmet | The Squire's Cap | He gave me this on my first day. "A squire must look the part," he said, and it was too big, and I hated him for it. I wore it every day anyway. I'm wearing it now. It fits now. I suppose I grew into it, or it shrank. Probably both.
wooden_axe | Firewood Duty | Every night I chopped his firewood and he sat by the fire and told me about the quarter of an hour he was brave. I knew it by heart. I could have told it to him. One night I did, by mistake, and he laughed so hard he cried, and then I did too.
shield | The Crest I Painted | His lion looks like a cat because I painted it. He knew. He said it was the best lion he'd ever seen. When he died, I opened his breastplate to polish it one last time, and under all my words, in his handwriting, very small: "thank you, son."

@ crumbsister | Mathilda Crumb, the Competent One
> You've heard of Tobias Crumb, the worst adventurer in the land, saved over and over again by enormous and undeserved luck. You've never heard of his sister. That was how she liked it. The luck, you see, had a name. The name was Mathilda.
iron_axe | The Tree That Fell | When her brother swung at a zombie and missed, and the tree fell on it, she was on the other side of the tree with this axe. She'd been chopping since dawn. Tobias told the story four thousand times. She was there for all of them. She never said a word.
leather_chestplate | The Quiet Coat | Brown, plain, the colour of nothing in particular. Perfect for standing behind trees. She followed him into every dungeon he ever got lost in and walked out a different way. People thought he had a guardian spirit. In a way he did.
fishing_rod | The Squid, Actually | When Tobias claimed he'd caught a dragon, it was a small squid, and it got away. What he never knew is that she'd hooked it for him, underwater, and let it go when he started shouting. She thought the squid deserved better. She was right about that too.
crossbow | The Second Arrow | Tobias fired three hundred arrows and hit nothing but himself. Every target the bards say he hit, she hit, a quarter-second later, from a hill. The bards wrote songs about his aim. She hummed along. She thought they were rather good songs, actually.
leather_boots | Her Walking Boots | When their father asked her, on his deathbed, why she let Tobias take the credit, she said: "because he needs it, and I don't." Her father said she was the wisest of his children. Then he asked her not to tell Tobias. She hasn't.
iron_pickaxe | The Water Under the Lava | When he mined straight down into lava and landed, luckily, in water, she'd dug the water there the week before. She'd guessed where he'd fall. She always guessed right. She says it's not a gift. She says it's just paying attention to an idiot you love.

@ reginaldwife | Lady Agnes, Who Married Sir Reginald
> Sir Reginald came seventh out of seven in every tournament he ever entered. This is not his story. This is the story of the woman who sat in the stands and cheered for him as if he'd come first, every single year, and of the letters she wrote him while he was away being perfectly adequate.
leather_helmet | The Bonnet in the Stands | She wore the same blue bonnet to every tournament, so that he could find her face in the crowd. He always did. When he came seventh, she stood up and clapped until the herald asked her to sit down. She never sat down.
shears | The Tapestry Shears | While he was away at the Battle of the Potato Field, she stitched a tapestry of it, and made it look magnificent: banners, dragons, Reginald on a white horse. He said, "it wasn't like that." She said, "it was to me." It hangs over their fireplace.
iron_chestplate | Her Letters | She wrote to him every week for forty years, and every letter ended the same way: "come home safe, you remarkable man." He kept them all, tied with string, inside his breastplate. He'd tell you he was unremarkable. He never said it in front of her.
fishing_rod | The Pond | The day Sir Reginald hit the dragon on the nose with his fishing rod, it was at her pond, by her house, in front of her garden. She saw the whole thing. She says it was the bravest thing she ever saw. He says he was surprised. She says that's what bravery is.
golden_helmet | The Crown She Made | For their fortieth anniversary she made him a crown out of melted-down tournament medals, all seventh-place, forty of them. He wore it to dinner. He wore it to bed. He's wearing it in his portrait, smiling, and next to him, in the blue bonnet, so is she.

@ twinsmother | Mother of the Saltmarsh Twins
> The Saltmarsh Twins swindled every village on the coast, and sold their mother's sword eleven times. Their mother would like to clarify a few things. She taught them everything they know. She did not teach them everything she knows.
golden_sword | The Sword They Kept Selling | They sold "their mother's sword" eleven times, to eleven villages, and bought it back each time for half. They thought it was the perfect con. It was. It was her con. She'd sold it to their father thirty years before, and he'd paid full price.
leather_chestplate@village | The Widow's Weeds | She wore black mourning clothes for twenty years, for a husband who was alive and well and living three villages over, selling fake weathervanes. Mourning gets you sympathy, she says. Sympathy gets you discounts. Discounts get you everything.
golden_axe | The Family Axe | The other twin's "family axe" is a real family axe. She knows, because she stole it from a family. They were very rich and very unpleasant and she gave them a lovely forged replacement. They haven't noticed. That was forty years ago.
flint_and_steel | The Original Fire Insurance | She sold fire insurance to a village too, once, decades before the twins did. The difference is that she never set a fire. She waited. Villages have fires eventually. She says that's the difference between a swindler and a businesswoman: patience.
leather_boots | Retired, Allegedly | She lives by the sea now, in a cottage she bought from a duke who, it turns out, didn't own it. Her sons visit for her birthday. She always gets them something nice. They always check it for a hallmark. It's always genuine. That's what worries them.

@ embermessenger | The Messenger of the Ember Queen
> Every year, for fifty years, the queen on the blackstone throne sent a piglin messenger through the last portal to ask whether her love still lived, and every year he came back with nothing. Nobody ever asked the messenger what he saw on the other side. He saw a great deal.
golden_boots@nether | The Messenger's Boots | Fifty journeys through the lava fields to the last portal, and through it, into a world of green and rain that frightened him to his bones, though he could bear it only briefly. He walked a little further into it each year. He told the queen nothing about that. She only asked about one man.
crossbow | The Crossbow at the Gate | He carried it for protection in the green world, and never needed it. The humans there ran from him. The children didn't. A little girl in a village by the portal gave him an apple every year. He didn't know what it was. He kept them all.
golden_helmet | What He Found | In the fortieth year he found the man the queen loved: an old farmer, by the portal, who had waited for her his whole life and never once stopped looking at the purple light. The messenger couldn't speak his language. He couldn't tell either of them.
golden_chestplate | The Fiftieth Year | In the fiftieth year, the farmer was buried by the portal, and the girl with the apples, grown now, showed the messenger the grave. He went back and stood before the queen and for the first time in fifty years he didn't say "nothing." He wept.
golden_sword@nether | The Sword on the Throne | She walked to the portal herself after that, and it lit for her, and she didn't come back. He never knew what happened. He sat at the foot of the empty throne for the rest of his life and guarded her sword, and if anyone asked, he said: "she went home."

@ guidedies | The Guide Who Always Dies
> In a certain world, every new settler is given a guide: a helpful young man in a brown coat who knows every recipe and gives terrible advice about monsters. He is very nice. He is also, for complicated reasons involving an enormous wall of meat, sacrificed every time the world gets hard. He has died nine thousand times. He's fine.
leather_chestplate | The Brown Coat | He always appears in the same coat, the same morning, to the same new settler, and says "greetings!" He knows every recipe there is. He knows nothing whatsoever about why he keeps dying. He thinks it's just bad luck.
wooden_pickaxe | The First Advice | His first piece of advice to everyone is: "you can use your pickaxe to dig through dirt." Everyone already knows. He says it anyway, very kindly, every time. Settlers have been known to cry, a bit, years later, remembering him saying it.
flint_and_steel | The Doll | Somewhere in the deep, hot underworld, settlers learn that a little doll of the guide, thrown into lava, summons the great wall. They feel terrible about it. They do it anyway. They make the doll themselves. It looks just like him. He saw one once. He said it was very flattering.
iron_sword | The Sword of Regret | Every settler who has ever done it swears they'll never do it again. They do it again. The guide comes back the next morning, in the same coat, and says "greetings!" and they can't look him in the eye. He doesn't mind. He thinks they've just had a rough night.
leather_boots | The Way Out | One guide, so the story goes, saw a settler making the doll and simply walked out of the world. Nobody knows where he went. Settlers in other worlds report a man in a brown coat who greets them, gives them advice, and then runs away very fast.

@ hurdygurdy | The Pirate Who Only Played Music
> On the seas of a certain world, crews of pirates sail about looking for treasure, sinking each other and drinking grog until they fall over. One pirate in all that sea never once fought, dug or sailed. She stood on deck playing a hurdy-gurdy, and was the most famous pirate of them all.
leather_helmet | The Tricorn | A tricorn hat with a feather far too long for it, which flapped in the wind and slapped her in the face whenever the ship turned. She never took it off. She said a pirate without a hat is just a sailor, and a sailor has a job.
shield | The Hurdy-Gurdy Case | She played the same three songs, all day, every day, as her crew fought and bailed and died and bailed some more. When ships came to sink them, she played louder. Most ships sailed away. Most crews say it was the music. Some say it was the playing.
trident | The Captain's Fork | She was never captain. The captain was whoever held the trident that week. She never once held it. She says she never wanted to. She says the captain is the one who gets shot at, and the musician is the one everyone protects. She's been at sea thirty years.
iron_shovel | The Shovel She Lent Out | She never dug for treasure herself. She lent this shovel to whoever on the crew was digging and played them music while they dug. They always found it. She says it's because nobody can stop digging while a hurdy-gurdy plays. It's true. Try it.
leather_boots | The Dancing Boots | When the ship sank, which it did every evening, she was the last one on deck, still playing as the sea came up over her boots. She'd swim to the next ship and they'd let her on board. Everyone always let her on board. Nobody could say no to her.
fishing_rod | The Rod She Gave Away | On her last voyage, a quiet boy on the crew caught a golden fish on her rod, and she gave him the rod and the hurdy-gurdy both, and swam off into the sunset singing. He plays it now. He's terrible. The crews love him anyway. It's tradition.

@ criminalscum | Stop Right There
> The guards of a certain province were famous for three things. They knew everything you'd ever done. They could run faster than any horse. And they would chase you across mountains, rivers and three cities for stealing a single spoon. This is the tale of the spoon.
iron_helmet | The Watch Helm | He was an ordinary town guard who stood at the gate all day and said things like "citizen" and "keep your nose clean". Then a stranger walked past the inn with a spoon in his pocket that wasn't his, and the guard's eyes went very wide. "Stop," he said. "Right. There."
iron_boots | The Chasing Boots | The stranger ran. The guard ran after him. They ran through the market, out of the city, over a mountain, through a lake and into another city, where the stranger hid in a barrel. The guard sat on the barrel. He sat there for three days.
iron_sword | The Sword of Justice | When the stranger came out at last, the guard pointed his sword and said: "you have broken the law. Pay your fine or do your time, and the goods stay with me." The stranger asked, "it's a spoon?" The guard said, "it's the principle."
shield | The Guard's Shield | The stranger escaped by talking to a horse. Nobody knows how. The guard has been looking for him ever since, across the whole province, for nineteen years. He still has the spoon. He polishes it. He says he's going to give it back to the innkeeper one day.
iron_chestplate | The Guard's Retirement | He retired last year. They gave him a gold watch. He checked it wasn't stolen. At his leaving party, someone pocketed a fork. He was out the door before anyone saw him move. That was eleven months ago. Nobody has seen him since.

@ deepfather | The Diver in the Drowned City
> In a city at the bottom of the sea, where the people went mad and the lights still flicker, there walk great divers in brass helmets, each one guarding a little girl. They do not speak. They moan, like whales, and the girls call them "Mister Bubbles". If you hurt the girl, the diver will kill you. If you hurt the diver, she will cry.
iron_helmet | The Brass Helm | Round, with little windows that glow yellow when he's calm and red when he isn't. Nobody knows who was inside the suit. The suit doesn't know either. It only knows the girl, and that she must be kept safe, and that is all that's left of him.
iron_chestplate | The Diving Suit | Bolted shut, heavy enough that he walked slowly and the floor shook when he came. The girl rode on his shoulders and sang. He couldn't sing back. He moaned along, very softly. She said it was the best singing in the city.
trident | The Drill | He carried a great drill on one arm, for anyone who came too close to her. Many did. None of them got close twice. When the girl was frightened, he would stand between her and the dark corridor, and wait, all night, without moving, until morning came.
iron_boots | The Heavy Boots | He fell, in the end, in a corridor full of water, to a stranger who had come for the girl. The girl knelt by his helmet and patted it and said "Mister Bubbles, get up." He couldn't. The stranger stood over her and had to choose.
leather_boots | Her Little Shoes | The stranger chose to save her. He took her up to the surface, to the sun, which she had never seen. She grew up. She had children. She told them, at bedtime, about a great brass man who loved her and couldn't say so. They asked if he was real. She said: "he was the realest."

@ boy | Marta the Axe-Thrower
> For thirty years the star of the travelling fair was Marta, who could throw an axe through a ring the size of an apple from thirty paces, blindfolded, standing on a barrel. Crowds came from three counties. Rival fairs tried to hire her away. She never left. This is why she never left, as told by the boy who swept up after her act.
iron_axe | The Throwing Axe | One axe, all her life, balanced so perfectly it spun like a coin and landed like a word. She threw it a hundred times a day. It never once missed. She said it wasn't skill. She said she'd thrown it so often it knew where she meant before she did. I believed her. I was nine.
bow | The Rival's Bow | The archer of the fair across the river challenged her every summer: his bow against her axe, at the river meadow, for the crowds. She won every year. One year she lost on purpose, because his wife was ill and the prize was money. He knew. He never challenged her again. He sent her a pie every year.
iron_chestplate | The Armour for the Act | For the grand finale she stood against a board in iron plate while her husband threw the axe at her. He was a terrible thrower. The crowds didn't know. They screamed every night. He hit the board every time. She said it was the bravest thing either of them did, and the most romantic.
leather_boots | On the Road | The fair moved every week, and she walked behind the wagons in these boots, rather than ride, because she liked to see the country. I walked with her. She taught me to throw on the road, stones at fence posts. I was terrible. She said so. She said terrible was a start.
shield | Why She Stayed | When her husband died, a rich fair from the capital offered her a fortune. She said no. I asked why. She pointed at the wagons, the tents, the juggler's children, me. "Because it's mine," she said. "All of it." She left me the axe. I'm not terrible any more. I'm not good either.

@ readingsmith | The Blacksmith's Son Who Couldn't Read
> His village was burned, his parents killed, and he set out to avenge them with a sword his father made, which was promptly stolen. He couldn't read, couldn't fight and couldn't ride a horse. He was not a hero. He was a blacksmith's son. Over the next two years he became one anyway, mostly through stubbornness and a lot of soup.
iron_sword | His Father's Sword | His father made it for a lord and never got to deliver it. The son carried it out of the burning village and lost it in a week to a bandit he'd mouthed off to. He spent the rest of his journey trying to get it back. He's still trying. He'll tell you he's close.
leather_chestplate | The Torn Tunic | He got into a fist fight in his first town, lost, got into another, lost that one, and kept getting into them until he started winning. He was too stubborn to stop. The town drunk taught him to read in return for wine. He reads very well now. Mainly wine labels.
iron_helmet | The Helm That Didn't Fit | Given to him by a lord who took a liking to him. It came down over his eyes. He fought a whole battle in it, swinging blind, and won, more or less by accident. The lord said he'd never seen such courage. He'd never seen anything, the boy said. That was the problem.
leather_boots | The Boots of the Priest | He went drinking with a priest in the third town, which was a mistake, and woke up in the priest's boots, in a field, a day's walk from anywhere, preaching to sheep. The sheep were very attentive. He says it was the best sermon he ever gave.
shield | The Lord's Shield | They made him a knight in the end, for bravery, and stubbornness, and for always eating his soup. He still can't ride a horse properly. He falls off at least once a week. He always gets back on. He says that's all being a knight is.

@ calibrations | The Gunner Who Was Always Calibrating
> On the great starship there was a gunner who, whenever you went to talk to him, was in the middle of some calibrations. He was always in the middle of calibrations. Every single time. The captain once asked what he was calibrating. He said, "can it wait for a bit? I'm in the middle of some calibrations."
crossbow | The Big Gun | He calibrated this crossbow for three years until it could hit a coin from a mile away. Then he calibrated it some more. The captain asked if it needed it. He said it was a matter of honour. Nobody on the ship knew what honour had to do with calibrating.
iron_helmet | The Visor | He wore a visor over one eye that told him how far away things were, and how hard they'd be to hit, and, he claimed, whether they deserved it. He shot a mercenary once who was about to shoot the captain. "That one deserved it," he said.
iron_chestplate | The Scarred Plate | He got the scar on his face from the same rocket that scorched this plate, saving a squad of soldiers in the slums. He was rather proud of it. The captain said it made him look distinguished. He said he'd been distinguished before. Now he was just distinguished with more texture.
iron_sword | The Sword He Never Used | He kept it on his belt and never drew it. "It's for close range," he said. "I don't do close range." He was very good at not doing close range. At the end of the last battle, on the roof of the citadel, the captain found him still calibrating.
bow | The Last Bottle | On the night before the end, they stood on the roof and shot bottles off a wall, and he missed one on purpose so the captain could win. The captain knew. The gunner said, "there's no captain I'd rather follow into hell." Then he said he had to go and do some calibrations.

@ chickenchaser | The Hero Called Chicken Chaser
> When you become a great hero in that land, you get to choose the title people call you by. He was a boy when he chose his. He thought it was funny. It stayed with him through the wars, the dragons, the death of the old king and his own coronation. They call him King Chicken Chaser. He still thinks it's funny. Mostly.
leather_boots | The Boy's Boots | In the town where he grew up, there was a chicken called Mrs Feathers, and he chased her round the square every morning until his mother called him for breakfast. He says that's the start of every hero's journey. Mrs Feathers did not agree.
diamond_sword | The Hero's Blade | He killed a dragon with this, and a great knight, and his own corrupt brother. Each time, the crowd cheered "Chicken Chaser! Chicken Chaser!" The dragon, in its last moments, looked at him with deep confusion. He says that's the face he remembers.
golden_helmet | The Crown | At his coronation, the archbishop placed the crown on his head and said, "all hail King Chicken Chaser, first of his name." Somebody in the crowd started clucking. Then everyone did. He laughed so hard the crown fell off. The archbishop has never recovered.
shield | The Royal Arms | His royal arms are a chicken, rampant, on a field of gold. He designed them himself. Visiting kings bow to the chicken. He considers this the greatest achievement of his reign. His advisors consider it the second greatest, after the treaties.
fishing_rod | The King's Day Off | On his days off, he goes back to his old town, and sits by the square, and fishes in the pond. There's an old chicken there, the great-great-granddaughter of Mrs Feathers. He doesn't chase her. He brings her grain. She lets him. That's enough.
