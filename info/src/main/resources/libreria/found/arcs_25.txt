@ snowgolemsummer | The Snow Golem Who Wanted Summer
> A girl in the snowy north built a snow golem in her garden, with a pumpkin for a head, and it followed her about all winter, throwing snowballs at anything that frightened her. In the spring, she told it about summer: the flowers, the warm sea, the long evenings. It wanted to see summer more than anything. Snow golems cannot see summer.
iron_shovel | The Shovel She Built It With | She built it with her father's shovel: two blocks of snow, and a pumpkin she'd carved with a smile. When it opened its eyes and looked at her, she screamed, and it threw a snowball at the thing that had made her scream, which was itself. It fell over. They've been friends since.
leather_helmet | Her Woolly Hat | She gave it her woolly hat, to go over the pumpkin, when the winter wind was bad. It wore it every day. When spring came and she had to take it back, it stood very still in the garden for a long time, as if it had lost something. She knitted it a new one.
shears | The Pumpkin Carving | Every autumn, she carved it a new pumpkin face, with these, and it chose which face it wanted by standing next to the one it liked. It always chose the smiling one. One year she carved it a frown, as a joke. It stood next to it for a day, very sad, until she made a new one.
leather_boots | The Journey South | When she was grown, she put it on a cart, packed in ice, with a big umbrella over it, and drove it south, as far as she could, to the edge of the summer. It started melting the moment the sun came out. She stopped the cart at the top of a hill, by a field of sunflowers. It looked.
fishing_rod | What It Saw | It saw the sunflowers, and the sea in the distance, and the long evening, gold and warm. It looked for a long time, melting. Then it turned to her, and very slowly, threw one last snowball, very softly, at her shoulder. She took the pumpkin home. She plants sunflowers by it every year.

@ foxthief | The Fox Who Stole Everything
> A fox lived at the edge of Willowmere, and every night it crept into the village and stole one thing, and in the morning that thing was gone. It took berries, mainly. Also a sword, a boot, the mayor's spectacles and, once, a whole fish from the fishmonger's hand while he was holding it. The village wanted it gone. The children wanted it to stay.
leather_boots | The Single Boot | The blacksmith woke to find one boot missing. He walked to work in one boot, cursing. Over the next month, every man in the village lost one boot. Never both. Never the left. The village took to wearing odd boots out of solidarity. The fox, it seemed, was building something.
iron_sword | The Sword It Couldn't Carry | It tried to steal the blacksmith's sword and got as far as the gate, dragging it in its mouth, before it gave up and sat down beside it, panting. The blacksmith found it there in the morning, sitting next to his sword, looking at him. He laughed so hard he forgave it for the boot.
fishing_rod | The Fish From the Hand | The fishmonger was holding a fish up to show a customer when the fox ran under his arm, took the fish, and was gone. The customer clapped. The fishmonger was furious. The customer bought another fish to say thank you for the show. The fishmonger made a profit.
shears | The Den | The children followed it one evening and found the den, under the roots of the old willow. Inside: forty-one right boots, the mayor's spectacles, a hundred berries, and three fox cubs asleep on a pile of the village's socks. The children went home and didn't say anything.
leather_helmet | The Village's Answer | When the grown-ups found the den, they wanted to dig it out. The children stood in front of it. The mayor, who had his spectacles back, looked at the cubs for a long time, and said the village could spare a few boots. Now the village leaves berries out at night, by the gate.

@ dolphinmap | The Dolphins of Saltwhistle
> The fishermen of Saltwhistle have an old saying: feed a dolphin, and it'll take you to treasure. Most people think it's a story. The Pell family have been feeding dolphins for four generations, and their house is built from shipwreck timbers, and their chests are full of gold. They'd like everyone to keep thinking it's a story.
fishing_rod | Great-Grandfather's Rod | It began with him, a poor fisherman who threw a fish to a dolphin because it looked hungry. The dolphin swam away and then came back and swam round his boat, three times, and then swam off again, slower, looking back. He followed it. It took him to a wreck. He was a poor fisherman no longer.
leather_helmet | The Diving Cap | His son dived for the wrecks, in this cap, and never once got lost, because the dolphins swam beside him underwater and pushed him back up when he needed air. He said the dolphins were the real family. His wife said that was fine as long as they didn't come to dinner.
iron_shovel | The Buried Chest | His granddaughter dug up the chests the dolphins led her to, on the beaches of a hundred islands. She kept a map. She never sold it. When the treasure hunters came asking, she showed them a map of the wrong ocean. She said the dolphins would have wanted it that way.
trident | The Drowned Guard | Some wrecks are guarded by the drowned, who come up out of the dark with tridents. The dolphins warn them: they swim in circles, very fast, and click. One Pell ignored them, once. He came back with this trident and without two fingers. He says it was a fair trade. Nobody else does.
leather_boots | The Youngest Pell | The youngest Pell is nine and has never found any treasure. She feeds the dolphins every morning and swims with them and doesn't follow them anywhere. Her family asked why. She said: "because then they'd think I only liked them for the treasure." The dolphins seem to agree.

@ turtlehome | The Turtle Who Came Home
> Turtles come back to the beach where they hatched to lay their own eggs, no matter how far they've swum. An old fisherman, whose father had watched over that beach before him, watched a turtle hatch there once and tied a little red thread round its shell. Forty years later, he was still waiting for the thread to come back.
fishing_rod | His Father's Rod | My father fished off that beach every morning and kept the gulls off the turtle eggs with his rod. I thought he was mad. Then he died, and I did the same, every morning, because I couldn't think what else to do with the mornings. I'm seventy-two. I still do it.
shears | The Red Thread | When I was thirty I saw a little one hatch, and I tied this red thread round its shell with these, very gently, so I'd know it if it came back. My wife said I was sentimental. She was right. She also said it would never come back. I said we'd see. We waited.
leather_boots | The Waiting | Forty years. I walked the beach every evening in these, looking for a red thread. My wife died. My children moved away. The beach got smaller as the sea came in. Turtles came, every year, and laid their eggs. None of them had a thread. I kept walking.
leather_chestplate | The Night | On the forty-first year, at night, a turtle came up the beach the size of a cart, old and barnacled, and on its shell, faded almost to pink, was a scrap of thread. I sat down in the sand and wept like a child. It didn't care. It was very busy. It laid its eggs.
iron_shovel | The Next Thread | I've tied a new thread round one of the little ones. My granddaughter helped. She's eight. I told her she has to wait for it, maybe forty years. She said: "that's okay, I'm not busy." I believe her. I've given her the rod. I'll lend her the boots when she's bigger.

@ frogchoir | The Frog Choir of the Mangrove
> Every evening, deep in the mangrove swamp, the frogs sing. Most people think it's noise. A retired choirmaster who moved to the swamp for the quiet discovered that it isn't noise: it's three choirs, in three colours, singing three parts. They were very good. They needed a conductor. She's been conducting them for eleven years.
leather_helmet | The Conductor's Bonnet | She wears her old bonnet from the cathedral choir, which looks very strange in a swamp. She stands on a mangrove root every evening and raises her hands, and the frogs, all three colours, go quiet. Then she brings them in. Orange, then white, then green.
fishing_rod | The Baton | She lost her baton in the move, and uses a fishing rod instead, with no line on it. The frogs don't mind. The orange frogs, who sing the low part, watch the tip of the rod very closely. She suspects they think it might be a fly. She's never told them otherwise.
leather_boots | The Wading Boots | The swamp is deep, and she conducts up to her knees in water. The fireflies light her score. She can't read it in that light anyway. She knows it by heart. She wrote it for them. It's in three parts. It's called "Mangrove Evensong". It has no words. It doesn't need them.
leather_chestplate | The Rain Cape | On the night of the great storm, she stood in the rain and conducted all night, so the frogs would keep singing through it, because she'd read that frogs go quiet in storms and she didn't want them to. They didn't go quiet. They sang through the whole thing. She cried.
shears | The Concert | Last spring, the cathedral choir came down to the swamp to hear her frogs. They stood on the bank, in their robes, and the frogs sang Mangrove Evensong, perfectly, all three parts. At the end, the cathedral choir sang back. The frogs listened. Then they sang it again, better.

@ parrotmimic | The Parrot Who Knew Too Many Sounds
> Parrots copy the sounds of whatever monsters are near, which is useful, as a warning. Captain Odessa's parrot, Biscuit, had sailed with her for twenty years and had heard every monster in the world, and copied them all, constantly, at the worst possible moments. Biscuit did not understand timing. Biscuit understood sounds.
leather_helmet | The Captain's Hat | Biscuit sat on this hat for twenty years, and the hat has a hole in the top from Biscuit's feet, and Captain Odessa refuses to get a new one because Biscuit refuses to sit on any other hat. She tried once. Biscuit made the sound of a wither. The crew jumped overboard.
iron_sword | The Wedding | At the captain's daughter's wedding, during the vows, in total silence, Biscuit made the hiss of a creeper. Every guest threw themselves to the floor. The groom drew his sword. The bride, who grew up with Biscuit, didn't turn round. She said "I do." Then she glared at the bird.
leather_chestplate | The Captain's Coat | Biscuit learned the sound of the ship's bell, the sound of the cook's laugh, and the sound of Captain Odessa saying "all hands on deck", which it said, in her exact voice, at three in the morning, every night, for a month. The crew have never been so tired. Or so ready.
shield | The Real Warning | Once, at sea, in fog, Biscuit made a sound nobody recognised: deep, wet, slow. The captain had never heard it. She turned the ship anyway, because twenty years teaches you something. The fog cleared. Where they'd been heading, the sea was boiling with drowned.
fishing_rod | Biscuit's Retirement | The captain retired to a cottage by the sea, and Biscuit retired with her, and sits on her hat by the fire. On quiet evenings he makes the sound of the ship's bell, softly, and the crew laughing, and the sea against the hull. She says it's the only way she can still hear them.

@ glowsquid | The Lantern of Glowhollow
> The cave village of Glowhollow is built deep underground, on the edge of a lake where glow squids swim. The villagers have never needed torches: the lake lights the caves blue, and they read and work by it. One year, the squids left. The village went dark. A girl went down into the water to find out why.
leather_helmet | The Diving Hood | Her grandmother's hood, sealed with wax and sewn with glow-ink so that you could see the wearer in the dark lake. She put it on at fourteen and went into the black water alone, at night, and the village stood on the shore with candles, which nobody in Glowhollow had ever needed.
trident | The Spear From the Deep | Deep down, past the old lake, she found where the squids had gone: a cave of drowned, who'd moved in from the sea through a crack in the rock. The squids were hiding behind them, in the dark. She took a trident from the first drowned she fought. It was the first fight of her life.
leather_chestplate | The Waxed Coat | She led the squids back up the tunnel, swimming ahead of them with her hood glowing, and they followed her light, because hers was the only light in the dark. When she came up into the lake, the whole village saw it at once: a girl in a glowing hood, and the blue light rising.
iron_pickaxe | The Sealed Crack | The village went down with pickaxes and sealed the crack in the rock so the drowned couldn't come back. It took a week. The lake was bright the whole time. The squids watched the work, the villagers say, from the edges, as if they were checking it was done properly.
fishing_rod | The Feast | Glowhollow holds a feast every year now, on the night the squids came back, and the girl, an old woman now, rows out into the middle of the lake and drops in a fish for the squids, and they circle her boat, glowing. The children make glowing hoods out of ink and wax.

@ camelpost | The Camel Post of the Long Sands
> In the desert kingdom, the post is carried by camels, which are faster than horses on sand and far less complaining than the people who ride them. Each camel carries two riders: one to steer, and one to sort. The best postal team in the kingdom was Jala and her grandfather, and a camel called Thursday, who hated both of them.
leather_helmet | The Sand Veil | Jala steered. She wore her mother's veil against the sand and the sun, and talked to Thursday the whole way, very sweetly. Thursday never once responded. Thursday spat at her, every morning, at precisely sunrise. Jala said it was his way of saying good morning.
leather_chestplate | The Sorting Coat | Her grandfather sorted, sitting behind her, with the letters in the pockets of his coat, one pocket per town. He'd been sorting for sixty years. He could sort in a sandstorm, blindfold, upside down. He did, once, when Thursday threw them. Every letter was in the right pocket.
leather_boots | The Stirrup Boots | The route through the long sands takes nine days and passes through four oases and one town that isn't on any map, which they deliver to anyway, because there's a man there who writes to his daughter every week. Thursday knows the way. Thursday knows every way.
fishing_rod | The Oasis Stop | At the oases they rest, and Jala's grandfather fishes in the pools, which have fish nobody knows how got there. He catches one every time. He gives it to Thursday. Thursday eats it. Camels don't eat fish. Thursday does. Thursday is a special camel. Everybody says so.
shears | The Last Delivery | When her grandfather was too old to ride, Jala took one last letter to him herself: from Thursday. It was a single straw, wrapped in paper. She said she didn't know how it got into the bag. Her grandfather held it for a long time. He said, "that camel always liked me best." He was right.

@ polarmother | The Polar Bear and the Fisherman
> On the ice in the far north, polar bears are left alone if you leave them alone. Unless you come near the cubs. An old fisherman, who'd fished the ice for fifty years, once fell through it, near a mother and her cub. What happened next he told to everyone in the village, for the rest of his life. Nobody believed him.
fishing_rod | The Ice Rod | He fished through a hole in the ice with this short rod, as he had done for fifty years. That morning the ice was thinner than he thought. It cracked all round him and he went into the black water. He says the cold is the worst thing in the world. It goes into your bones and stops your thoughts.
leather_chestplate | The Fur Coat | His fur coat was so heavy in the water that it dragged him down. He couldn't get it off. He was sinking. Then something grabbed him by the collar, something with huge, gentle teeth, and pulled. He came up out of the hole on the ice in the jaws of a polar bear.
leather_helmet | The Cap She Took | She dropped him on the ice and stood over him, and her cub came and sniffed his face, and she took his cap in her teeth, and walked away with it. He lay there and shivered and laughed. He said she'd earned it. He says it's the best price he ever paid for anything.
leather_boots | The Walk Home | He walked home soaked, without his cap, and told the village a polar bear saved his life. They said he'd been drinking. He said he'd been drowning. His wife wrapped him in blankets and didn't say anything at all. She believed him. She was the only one.
shield | Every Spring | Every spring after that, he left a fish on the ice, by the place where he fell. Every spring, it was gone the next morning. In the spring he died, his wife left the fish. The next morning, beside where it had been, there was his cap, very old, very chewed, frozen to the ice.

@ mushroomhermit | The Hermit of Mooshroom Isle
> Far out to sea, there's an island where the grass is purple, the trees are giant mushrooms, and the cows are red and grow mushrooms on their backs. Nothing hostile spawns there. A man went to live on it, alone, forty years ago, to get away from monsters. He says he's never been happier. He also says he's very sick of mushroom stew.
shears | The Mushroom Shears | You can shear a mooshroom, and it gives you mushrooms, and turns into an ordinary cow. He found this out on his first day and was so horrified he apologised to the cow for a week. He hasn't sheared one since. The cow, now plain brown, follows him everywhere. He calls it Penance.
wooden_hoe | The Purple Garden | He's tried to grow everything on the island. Wheat, carrots, potatoes. The purple soil grows nothing but mushrooms. He tried for nine years. He has a field of mushrooms where his wheat should be. He says he's proud of the field. He says he'd kill for a carrot.
leather_chestplate | The Hermit's Coat | So covered in spores that it's grown its own small mushroom on the collar. He's named it. He talks to it. He says he's aware that this is what happens to hermits. He says it's a very good listener and has never once interrupted him, unlike the cows.
fishing_rod | The Fish He Craves | He fishes off the beach, every day, for anything that isn't a mushroom. He's caught ten thousand fish. He eats them all. Visitors who come by boat bring him carrots. He weeps at the sight of a carrot. He's started paying for them with mushrooms. The visitors have started declining.
leather_boots | The Mainland | He went back to the mainland once, after thirty years, to see what it was like. A creeper blew up his boots in the first hour. A skeleton shot him in the second. He went straight back to the island. He's never left since. He says he'll take the stew.

@ squirequeen | The Squire Who Became Queen
> The chronicle of the kingdom of Ardmere records that its greatest monarch, Queen Hesper the Unexpected, began her career as a squire, in disguise, carrying the shield of a knight who never noticed she was a girl. She was crowned at twenty-six. The knight was at the coronation. The chronicle notes that he still hadn't noticed.
shield | The Knight's Shield | She carried the shield of Sir Rowan for eight years, through three wars. She polished it, carried it, and held it over him when he fell, at the battle of Grey Ford, where she took two arrows in it and one in her shoulder. He said she was the bravest lad he'd ever known.
leather_helmet | The Squire's Cap | She wore her hair short under this cap, and her voice low, and nobody looked twice at a squire. The chronicle records that the disguise was, in fact, very poor: half the army knew, and said nothing, because she was the best squire in it and nobody wanted to lose her.
iron_sword | The Sword of Grey Ford | At Grey Ford, when the king's line broke and the king fell, she picked up his sword, which was the closest one, and held the line herself, shouting, in her own voice, at last. The army heard a woman's voice and rallied to it. Nobody knows why. The chronicle says "it was a good voice."
golden_helmet | The Crown | The king had no heir. The lords argued for a year. The army settled it: they marched to the capital and asked for the squire who had held the line at Grey Ford. The lords asked who that was. The army said: "her." The lords looked at Sir Rowan. He looked very surprised.
iron_chestplate | The Queen's Armour | She ruled for thirty-nine years and was called the Unexpected, and kept Sir Rowan as her champion, who served her loyally until his death, and who, at the very end, the chronicle records, said to her: "you know, I've always thought there was something different about you."
